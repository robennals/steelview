---
question: Do differences in outcomes and attitudes by country of origin persist into the second generation in Britain?
kind: assumption
divides: [cultural-cost, open-and-build]
positions:
  - viewpoint: cultural-cost
    holds: For some groups, yes. Where countries measure it, outcomes differ by origin and some get worse in the second generation; Britain does not measure it, so it cannot claim to be different.
  - viewpoint: open-and-build
    holds: Mostly not. The children of earlier migrants outperform at school and segregation has fallen; the foreign evidence is about refugee intakes unlike Britain's work and study arrivals.
---
The evidence each side points to is real, and none of it measures the thing in dispute. In Denmark, [men born to non-Western immigrants are convicted more often than the immigrants themselves](#fact-crime-terrorism-and-group-exploitation/danish-descendants-offend-more-than-parents), and a Dutch study finds that [where the first generation is a large net cost, the second usually is too](#fact-integration-outcomes/netherlands-by-region). In Britain, [most minority groups outscore White British pupils at GCSE](#fact-integration-outcomes/minority-pupils-outperform) and [residential segregation fell for every group](#fact-integration-outcomes/segregation-fell).

Each side has a reply to the other's evidence. The foreign figures [carry over differences in who those countries admitted](#fact-integration-outcomes/what-is-not-measured), mostly refugees and family members. The British school figures [are by ethnicity, not generation](#fact-integration-outcomes/ethnicity-is-not-generation), and say nothing about the children of recent arrivals. On attitudes, [the one study comparing generations is old and its results are mixed](#fact-integration-outcomes/attitudes-generations).

What would settle it is British data following immigrants and their children by country of origin, as Denmark publishes. If the second generation converges, the cultural case loses its central worry about Britain; if some groups diverge, the open case must say what integration policy would change that.
