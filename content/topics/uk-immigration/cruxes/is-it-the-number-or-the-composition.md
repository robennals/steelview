---
question: Is it the total number that matters, or who is admitted?
kind: assumption
divides: [restore-control, select-for-contribution, open-and-welcoming]
positions:
  - viewpoint: restore-control
    holds: The total is the thing consent is given or withheld to. A million people is a million people whatever they earn, and a policy that cannot state a number cannot be voted on.
  - viewpoint: select-for-contribution
    holds: Earnings explain almost all of the variation in outcomes, and dispersion within the migrant population dwarfs the gap between migrants and residents. Cap the composition and the number becomes an output, not a target.
  - viewpoint: open-and-welcoming
    holds: Neither, mostly. The effects attributed to both are small next to Britain's own failures in housing, training and care funding, and a fixation on either dial is a way of not fixing those.
---
This is the crux most likely to move someone, because the evidence bears on it
directly and cuts across the usual sides. If the fiscal and labour-market results
really are driven by earnings rather than headcount, a numerical cap is a blunt
instrument that binds hardest on the arrivals with the best case — and the
restrictionist should want a threshold instead. If instead the effects people
actually object to are about pace, density and consent, then composition is
beside the point and a threshold is a way of ignoring the objection.
