---
question: How much of the pressure on homes and public services comes from population growth, and how much from too little building and investment?
kind: assumption
divides: [economic-cost, open-and-build, blame-underinvestment]
positions:
  - viewpoint: economic-cost
    holds: Population growth is the larger part now. Migration drives most household growth and the country cannot build fast enough to keep up, so numbers must come down.
  - viewpoint: open-and-build
    holds: Mostly building. Migration adds demand, but the shortfall comes from building too little, and that can be fixed.
  - viewpoint: blame-underinvestment
    holds: Mostly investment. Decades of low public investment and the collapse of social-rent building did the damage; migrants are blamed for it.
---
Both readings have official numbers behind them. [Migration accounts for just over half of projected household growth in England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth), and [building has run below that growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). But [immigration explains only about 4–6% of the real rise in house prices over three decades](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), [public investment was below the OECD median in every year from 1995 to 2024](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/public-investment-low), and [social-rent building fell sharply after 2010-11](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/social-rent-building-fell-after-2010).

The recent record fits both. [Rent growth fell as net migration fell](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell), but [the timing does not isolate migration's effect](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-timing-not-causal). National service pressure is weaker than the housing case: [pupil numbers are falling](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/pupil-numbers-falling) and [GP numbers rising](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/gp-numbers-rising), though [national totals can hide local pressure](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/local-pressure).

[The same review gives no rent equivalent of its house-price figure](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects), and that estimate would move this. If migration explains a large share of rents, cutting numbers is the quickest relief; if it explains little, building and investment are the only real answer, and blaming migration delays them.
