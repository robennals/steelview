---
question: Can Parliament change the terms of settlement for people already in Britain, or do the terms they came on bind it?
kind: tradeoff
divides: [end-settlement, keep-our-word, contribute-and-earn, economic-cost]
positions:
  - viewpoint: end-settlement
    holds: Parliament can change anything, including settlement already granted. Voters never consented to the numbers, and their will outranks a migrant's expectation.
  - viewpoint: keep-our-word
    holds: The terms bind. People who came on a five-year path planned their lives on it and should finish on it; new terms are for new arrivals.
  - viewpoint: contribute-and-earn
    holds: Terms for people not yet settled can change, with transitional help for some groups; settlement already granted stays.
  - viewpoint: economic-cost
    holds: The wait should be lengthened for those not yet settled, before the coming wave qualifies. Taking away settlement already granted goes too far.
---
The rules say less than either side claims. [The skilled-worker guidance says migrants “may be able to apply”, while the Hong Kong route was a “historic and moral commitment”](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/guidance-said-may-be-able-to-apply). [The High Court struck down a mid-route change in 2008](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/high-court-struck-down-mid-route-change-2008), on wording that has not been tested against today's guidance, and [there is precedent both for protecting people already here and for changing their terms](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/transitional-protection-precedent). [Removing settlement already granted would need new law](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/revocation-needs-new-law).

The stakes are large: [about 2.2 million people were on a five-year path](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/path-2-2-million), and [about 1.6 million are expected to qualify in 2026–30](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million). [The public opposes removing settlement from people who hold it](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/revoking-opposed-58-29) and [splits evenly on ending it for new arrivals](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/ending-for-new-arrivals-splits).

This is a collision between [democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) and [rules that mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say). Evidence on whether longer waits change behaviour, which [is not yet established](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/behaviour-evidence-limited), would sharpen the cost of breaking the expectation but would not settle which value comes first.
