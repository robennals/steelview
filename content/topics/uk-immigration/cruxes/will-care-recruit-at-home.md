---
question: With overseas care recruitment ended, will care employers recruit at home, or will care go short?
kind: prediction
divides: [contribute-and-earn, economic-cost, blame-underinvestment]
positions:
  - viewpoint: contribute-and-earn
    holds: They can recruit at home if pay rises. Vacancies have not risen since recruitment ran down, which shows the route was not holding the sector up.
  - viewpoint: economic-cost
    holds: Care will adjust as other sectors do when a cheap supply of labour is withdrawn. Closing the route is right whatever the short-run strain.
  - viewpoint: blame-underinvestment
    holds: Not without money. Care pays near the minimum because it is underfunded; closing the route without funding pay rises will leave people without care.
---
So far vacancies have held. [Care vacancies fell to the lowest rate in the series](#fact-work-and-study-routes/care-vacancies-fell), and [they did not rise as overseas recruitment ran down](#fact-work-and-study-routes/vacancies-held-as-recruitment-fell). But that is under a year of data after the route closed, and Skills for Care credits part of the fall to weaker demand elsewhere in the economy.

Domestic recruitment has not taken up the slack: [British-held care posts fell by about 130,000, because fewer British people are starting care jobs](#fact-work-and-study-routes/british-held-posts-fell). [Care pay sits close to the legal minimum](#fact-work-and-study-routes/care-pay-near-the-floor), and the committee that recommended the route [named underfunding as the root cause and saw its pay recommendation go unimplemented](#fact-work-and-study-routes/mac-names-underfunding).

What would move people is two or three more years of vacancy and staffing data, alongside care pay. If vacancies stay low while British-held posts start rising, the first two positions are borne out. If vacancies climb and British recruitment keeps falling, the third is right that closing the route without funding pay shifts the shortage onto people who need care.
