---
question: Would lower migration leave the people already in Britain richer per head, or poorer?
kind: prediction
divides: [economic-cost, open-and-build, contribute-and-earn]
positions:
  - viewpoint: economic-cost
    holds: Richer, or at least no poorer. A larger population has not raised output per person, investment never keeps up with it, and fewer people means more spending and housing per head.
  - viewpoint: open-and-build
    holds: Poorer. Fewer workers means more borrowing, a faster-ageing population and short-staffed services, with no gain in productivity to show for it.
  - viewpoint: contribute-and-earn
    holds: Neither, as a matter of numbers. Income per head turns on who comes; fewer low-paid arrivals and more high earners would raise it, and a cut that fell on high earners would lower it.
---
The data agree on the headline and not on its cause. [Total GDP grew after 2019 while GDP per head barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew), but [the series shows timing, not cause](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/timing-not-cause), and [other G7 economies stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike).

The Office for Budget Responsibility's scenarios turn on two assumptions. [If capital does not keep pace, more migrants lower output per person; if nearly all adults arrive to work, they raise it](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small). [Public investment does not adjust quickly to population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust), which favours the first reading; [the gains concentrated among high earners](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/gain-from-top-earners-and-stayers) favour selecting rather than cutting. Over five years, [lower migration raises borrowing](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing) but also [raises spending per person](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/lower-migration-raises-spending-per-person).

What would settle it is a measure of whether capital per worker fell as the population grew after 2021, and of productivity by visa route. If capital kept pace, the case for a hard cut on economic grounds weakens; if it did not, the case for open routes must say how investment will catch up.
