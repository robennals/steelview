---
question: When more immigration would make the country richer but change its culture faster, which should come first?
kind: tradeoff
divides: [cultural-cost, open-and-build]
positions:
  - viewpoint: cultural-cost
    holds: Continuity. A shared way of life is worth more than a gain in GDP, and once lost it cannot be bought back.
  - viewpoint: open-and-build
    holds: Growth and the freedom to work together. Cultures have always changed, and a richer country can afford the homes and services that make integration work.
---
The two viewpoints rest on different goods. [Continuity of a shared way of life](#principle-continuity-of-a-shared-way-of-life) asks that change be slow enough for newcomers to join it; [freedom to cooperate and exchange](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) asks that people be free to come and work where they are wanted.

The conflict is real in some cases and not in others. [Skilled Workers are a large net gain to the public finances](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost), while [recent arrivals are the most likely to struggle with English](#fact-integration-outcomes/recent-arrivals-struggle-with-english) and [British identity rises with time in the country](#fact-integration-outcomes/identity-rises-with-time). The research on trust [measures diversity, not immigration or its pace](#fact-integration-outcomes/diversity-not-immigration), so it cannot say how fast is too fast.

Evidence can narrow the conflict without settling it. If high-earning arrivals integrate as fast as others, the trade-off is small for the routes that bring most of the gain. Where it remains, the choice is between values, and each side has to say how much of one it would give up for the other.
