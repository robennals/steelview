[Priority to the worst off](#principle-priority-to-the-worst-off) leaves a difficult
question about whose needs a government must count. A resident unable to afford
rent and a person seeking safety can both be vulnerable. The
[duty to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger) can pull
against giving residents first claim on limited resources.

If residents have an overriding claim, the protection argument must accept limits
even for people in danger. If urgent need counts regardless of nationality, the
national-control argument must explain why residence should outweigh a greater
need elsewhere. Accepting either answer changes who a policy must protect first.

The economic position also disputes the presumed tradeoff: admitting people can
help support residents. Evidence about housing and services can show whether a
particular policy creates a conflict or eases it. Where a conflict remains,
evidence alone cannot decide whose claim should prevail.
