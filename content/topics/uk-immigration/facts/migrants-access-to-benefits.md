## Observations {#migrants-access-to-benefits--context}

### Most people arriving on visas cannot claim most benefits, usually for at least five years {#migrants-access-to-benefits--most-people-arriving-on-visas-cannot-claim-most-benefits-usually-for-at-least-five-ye}

The No Recourse to Public Funds condition blocks Universal Credit, Child Benefit and most other welfare until indefinite leave to remain, which normally follows "at least five years of stay". [1](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-1) [2](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-2)

### "Migrants can't claim benefits" is wrong too {#migrants-access-to-benefits--migrants-can-t-claim-benefits-is-wrong-too}

The condition lifts on indefinite leave, and it attaches to work, study and family visas rather than to everyone who arrives. [1](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-1)

## Subtleties {#migrants-access-to-benefits--subtleties}

### Refugees are a separate case, and are not in the fiscal modelling at all {#migrants-access-to-benefits--refugees-are-a-separate-case-and-are-not-in-the-fiscal-modelling-at-all}

The MAC says it has not assessed them: a group "likely to affect the public finances more negatively than the groups discussed above because they have lower employment rates". [3](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-3)

### Asylum seekers are separate again {#migrants-access-to-benefits--asylum-seekers-are-separate-again}

, supported through asylum accommodation rather than through the benefit system. [1](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-1)

### So access depends on route and length of stay {#migrants-access-to-benefits--so-access-depends-on-route-and-length-of-stay}

, which is why fiscal estimates are published per route and never for "migrants" as a class. [1](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-1) [3](#source-migrants-access-to-benefits-3)
