The American literature finds a clear negative association between neighbourhood
diversity and trust. The British literature does not replicate it cleanly, and
three problems stop this being narrowed into something well-supported.

- **The association shrinks or disappears once deprivation is controlled for.**
  Which suggests that what estranges people in a poor diverse area may be the
  poverty — or that the two cannot be separated with the data available.
- **Almost all of the research measures ethnic diversity, not immigration.** They
  are demonstrably different variables: at the 2021 Census 16.8% of England and
  Wales was born outside the UK while a quarter did not identify as White
  British, and nearly half the foreign-born identified as White.
- **The best British data come from a survey that stopped in 2011.** So there is
  no good evidence at all on the period this page covers.
- **And "cohesion" here means survey questions about trusting neighbours**, which
  is one narrow slice of what people mean by the word.
- **Unknown, not contested, and the distinction is deliberate.** A contested fact
  is one where good evidence points both ways on a live question. Here the
  evidence does not reach the question. Nobody on this page is entitled to build
  on it in either direction. The honest statement is that Britain stopped
  collecting the data needed to find out.
