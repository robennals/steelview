Both halves of this are true and each is quoted almost exclusively by one side.
2024 was the highest year for asylum claims since the records begin in 1979 — but
only just, at about 108,000 against about 103,000 in 2002, and that earlier peak
came from a different set of wars twenty-two years before. The level moves with
conflict abroad more than with anything Britain decides: the 1998–2002 spike was
Somalia, Afghanistan, Iraq and the former Yugoslavia.

Set against Europe, the ranking flips depending on whether you divide by
population. The UK was fifth in the EU+ by number of claims in 2024 and
seventeenth per head, at 16 claims per 10,000 residents; on grants of protection it
was fourth by number and fourteenth per head, at six per 10,000. The Home Office's
own comparison for the year to December 2025 puts the UK fifth by number and
fifteenth per head — a slightly different window, so the two per-head ranks should
not be merged into one figure. Claims themselves have since come down: 94,000 in
the year to March 2026, 12% fewer than the year before.

Two cautions on reading the series. "Applications" and "people" differ, because a
claim can cover dependants; the figures above are people. And this page carries
only the 1979, 2002, 2024 and 2026 anchors, because the intermediate points of the
series — the 2010 low, the 2010s average — could not be verified from a source
that met this project's bar.
