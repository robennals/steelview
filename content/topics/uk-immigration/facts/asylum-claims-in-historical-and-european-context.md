## Observations {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--context}

### The 2024 total exceeded the previous modern-record peak {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--claims-are-at-their-highest-in-the-modern-record-but-only-just}

Around 108,000 people claimed asylum in 2024, compared with about 103,000 in 2002. The later rolling-year peak was 110,051 in the year to September 2025. [1](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-1) [10](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-10)

### They have since come down {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--they-have-since-come-down}

93,525 people claimed asylum in the year to March 2026, 12% fewer than the year before, after a peak of 110,051 in the year to September 2025. [10](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-10) [11](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-11)

### Per head of population the UK is mid-table, not top {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--per-head-of-population-the-uk-is-mid-table-not-top}

Fifth in the EU+ by number of claims in 2024 and seventeenth per head, at 16 claims per 10,000 residents. On grants of protection, fourth by number and fourteenth per head, at six per 10,000. [2](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-2) [6](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-6) [7](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-7)

## Subtleties {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--subtleties}

### The intervening years were lower {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--between-2004-and-2020-the-yearly-total-ran-between-22-000-and-46-000}

Annual claims ranged between 22,000 and 46,000 from 2004 to 2020. The current level exceeds that range. [9](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-9)

### The two per-head ranks come from different windows and should not be merged {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--the-two-per-head-ranks-come-from-different-windows-and-should-not-be-merged}

The Migration Observatory says seventeenth for calendar 2024; the Home Office says fifteenth for the year to December 2025. [2](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-2) [3](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-3)

### Wars are an important influence on claims {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--the-level-tracks-wars-not-british-policy}

The Migration Observatory attributes a large part of changes in claims to geopolitical events, including the conflicts associated with the 1998–2002 increase. This does not establish that UK policy has no effect. [5](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-5)

### Applications and people are different counts {#asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context--applications-and-people-are-different-counts}

A claim can cover dependants; the figures here are people. The chart's 2001 point is main applicants only, because dependants only enter the data from 2002. [12](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-12) [13](#source-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context-13)
