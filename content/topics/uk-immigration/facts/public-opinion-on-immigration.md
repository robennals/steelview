## Observations {#public-opinion-on-immigration--context}

### The measured level swings about twenty points on question wording {#public-opinion-on-immigration--the-measured-level-swings-about-twenty-points-on-question-wording}

In April 2023, 32% said immigration was a bad thing while 52% said numbers should be reduced — the Migration Observatory's reading is that people are answering about different things. [1](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-1) [3](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-3)

### British attitudes are among the more liberal in Europe {#public-opinion-on-immigration--british-attitudes-are-among-the-more-liberal-in-europe}

25% on the European Social Survey's measure in 2023, second only to Norway, after a decade of softening before 2022. [2](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-2)

## Subtleties {#public-opinion-on-immigration--subtleties}

### Salience is not direction {#public-opinion-on-immigration--salience-is-not-direction}

The rise after 2022 mixes people who want less immigration with people angry about small boats, hotels, or a system that visibly does not work. No poll separates them. [1](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-1)

### The source cited here contradicts itself, and a reader should know {#public-opinion-on-immigration--the-source-cited-here-contradicts-itself-and-a-reader-should-know}

The Ipsos page's headline says two-thirds of Britons think the total too high; its own first key finding puts it at 46%. This page uses the key findings. [1](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-1)

### The aggregate is too unstable to carry a policy conclusion {#public-opinion-on-immigration--the-aggregate-is-too-unstable-to-carry-a-policy-conclusion}

The route-by-route questions are steadier, and they are a separate fact: *Immigration has been a top-tier public concern since 2022*. [1](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-1) [3](#source-public-opinion-on-immigration-3)
