As stated, this is true and unhelpful, which is why it is tagged complicated
rather than well-supported.

Three things are solid. Immigration is a top-tier issue and has been since 2022.
Majorities say numbers are too high when asked that way. And the public
distinguishes sharply between kinds of migrant — asylum draws far more objection
than work or study, and there are consistent majorities for admitting doctors,
nurses and care workers.

Three things are not. The measured level of opposition swings by twenty points on
question wording, which means "the public wants X" is partly a claim about the
pollster. British attitudes were softening for a decade before 2022 and remain
among the more liberal in Europe. And salience is not direction: the sharp rise
after 2022 is partly people who want less immigration and partly people angry
about small boats, hotels, or a system that visibly does not work.

There is a real democratic argument in here, but it has to be made about a
specific policy, because the aggregate "the public wants" is not stable enough to
carry weight.
