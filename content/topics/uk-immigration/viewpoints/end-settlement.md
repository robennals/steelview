Britain should stop giving migrants the right to stay for good. People who already have it should move onto visas that must be renewed. And for a time, more people should leave Britain than arrive.

### A wave of permanent settlement is about to arrive

Settlement, or indefinite leave to remain, is the right to stay in Britain for life. [The Home Office expects about 1.6 million people to settle by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million), [mostly from health and care, skilled-worker and Hong Kong visas issued since 2021](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-drivers-in-detail). [Once settled, a person can claim most of the benefits that people on temporary visas cannot](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/settlement-unlocks-benefits). Voters should get to decide that before it happens.

### Settling low earners turns a working stay into a lifetime cost

Government advisers find that [care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost more than they pay over a lifetime](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/care-route-net-cost), and that [for lower earners, a temporary stay is better for the public purse than a permanent one](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/temporary-better-for-lower-earners). [About half of adult skilled-worker dependants were not working, and so were a third of adults on family visas by their fifth year](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/non-working-groups). A renewable visa keeps the workers who pay in, such as skilled workers outside health and care, who add hundreds of thousands of pounds each. The person who does not work is not renewed.

### Skilled workers were never promised settlement, and the rules have changed before

[The official guidance said only that workers "may be able to apply"](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/guidance-said-may-be-able-to-apply). [Rule changes have been applied before to people already in Britain](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/transitional-protection-precedent). And [the family route's wait for settlement went from two years to five in 2012](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/family-route-two-to-five-2012).

### Taking settlement back would need a new law, passed openly by Parliament

[Today's law lets settlement be revoked only on individual grounds](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/revocation-needs-new-law). So ending it for everyone would need a new Act of Parliament, passed in the open by the people's representatives. A country has the right to decide who becomes a permanent member of it.

While [people with settlement outside the EU scheme were 2.7% of those on Universal Credit](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/no-official-data-on-ilr-holders-work-or-benefits), this step rests on principle, not numbers. And while [people with EU settled status far outnumber other holders](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/euss-outnumber-non-eu-ilr), and [are outside the reforms under the Brexit treaty](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/eu-status-out-of-scope), Britain should act where Parliament can.

### Only more people leaving than arriving would undo the change

[Net migration added about 2.5 million people in 2021–24](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net), and [England is adding fewer homes each year than the households it expects](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). Lower arrivals would leave those people in place; only years in which more people leave than arrive would reverse it. Britain's past years of net emigration came when arrivals were a fraction of today's, but [British citizens already leave on net](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/british-net-emigration), so arrivals need only fall below departures. While [the population ages fastest with no net migration](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-slows-ageing-but-does-not-stop-it), [most people wanted immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and a country should not have to accept for ever a change it did not choose.

### So replace settlement with renewable visas and keep benefits for citizens

A government's first duty is to its own citizens, and the welfare state they pay for should be theirs. Britain should replace indefinite leave with renewable five-year visas, and keep benefits for citizens. It should keep arrivals below departures until the country has absorbed the change.

While [most people oppose taking settlement from those who hold it](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/revoking-opposed-58-29), [ending it for new arrivals already splits the country evenly](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/ending-for-new-arrivals-splits). And [most Reform UK voters back taking it from those who hold it](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/reform-voters-back-removal). The argument has to be won, and it can be.
