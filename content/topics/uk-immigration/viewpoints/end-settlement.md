Britain should stop giving migrants the right to stay for good. People who already have it should move onto visas that must be renewed, and for a time more people should leave Britain than arrive.

### A wave of permanent settlement is about to arrive

Settlement, or indefinite leave to remain, is the right to stay in Britain for life. The Home Office expects about 1.6 million people to settle in 2026 to 2030. The rise is driven by health and care, skilled-worker and Hong Kong visas issued since 2021. Once settled, a person can claim benefits on the same terms as British citizens. Voters should get to decide that before it happens.

### Settling lower earners costs the public purse more than a temporary stay

Care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost the public purse more than they pay over a lifetime. For lower earners, a temporary stay is better for the public purse than a permanent one. About half of adult skilled-worker dependants were not working. A renewable visa keeps the workers who pay in. The person who does not work is not renewed.

### Skilled workers were not promised settlement, and the rules have changed before

The skilled-worker guidance says only that after five years, a worker "may be able to apply" to settle. The Home Secretary says the rules that apply are those in force when a person applies, not when they arrived. MPs found precedent for changing the rules for people already here, and in 2012 the family route's wait went from two years to five. While the High Court struck down a change made part-way through one scheme in 2008, that ruling turned on what that scheme had promised.

### Only more people leaving than arriving would undo the change

Net migration added about 2.5 million people in 2021 to 2024. Lower arrivals would leave those people in place; only years in which more people leave than arrive would reverse it. Britain had such years when arrivals were a fraction of today's. British citizens already leave on net, by about 136,000 in 2025, so other people's net migration need only fall below that. While the population ages fastest with no net migration, a country should not have to accept for ever a change it did not choose.

### So replace settlement with renewable visas, and keep benefits for citizens

A government's first duty is to its own citizens, and the welfare state they pay for should be theirs. Today's law lets settlement be taken away only on individual grounds, so Parliament should pass a new law, openly. It should replace indefinite leave with renewable five-year visas, keep benefits for citizens, and keep arrivals below departures until the country has absorbed the change.

This cannot reach people with EU settled status, who far outnumber other holders and fall outside the reforms under the Brexit treaty. Britain should act where Parliament can.

While most people oppose taking settlement from those who hold it, ending new grants already splits the country evenly, and most Reform UK voters back taking it from those who hold it. The argument has to be won, and it can be.
