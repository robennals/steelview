---
name: We have a moral duty to help people fleeing danger
summary: "People fleeing persecution deserve safety and a fair hearing. Britain should meet that duty through a humane, workable asylum system, even when doing so has a cost."
order: 5
---
Someone fleeing persecution needs a place where they can be safe. Their claim on our help does not depend on whether they are highly paid, speak our language or happen to have been born nearby. Our [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger) is a reason to offer protection even when there is little in it for us. That is what makes asylum a duty worth honouring.

Britain should take its share of that responsibility. [Asylum claims have reached historically high levels in the UK](#fact-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context/claims-are-at-their-highest-in-the-modern-record-but-only-just), so providing protection requires a serious commitment. But [the UK receives fewer claims per person than many European countries](#fact-asylum-claims-in-historical-and-european-context/per-head-of-population-the-uk-is-mid-table-not-top). The pressure is real; it does not establish that Britain is being asked to carry an exceptional share or that we should withdraw from helping.

A fair system must examine each person’s circumstances. [Some people who claim asylum arrived with visas or other permission](#fact-how-people-actually-arrive/asylum-and-small-boats-overlap-but-are-not-nested), so the route someone travelled cannot answer whether they need protection. We should hear their claim promptly, protect those who qualify and allow a fair appeal. When a claim is finally refused, removal should follow lawful procedures. The commitment that [rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say) protects both the person seeking safety and public confidence in the decision.

The cost deserves a practical answer. [Asylum accommodation has become far more expensive than originally planned](#fact-asylum-accommodation-cost-overrun/the-contracts-are-running-at-more-than-three-times-their-original-price), with [hotels taking a disproportionate share of the spending](#fact-asylum-accommodation-cost-overrun/hotels-are-the-reason). That calls for better decisions and accommodation. [The backlog and hotel use have already fallen substantially](#fact-asylum-backlog-and-hotel-use-have-fallen/finding), showing that those pressures can ease. We should keep improving the system while accepting that protecting people will still cost money.

I would provide safe routes to seek protection, invest in timely decisions and let people work while they wait. These choices should be funded and explained honestly. Giving [priority to the worst off](#principle-priority-to-the-worst-off) means being willing to devote resources to people in grave danger. A workable asylum system is how Britain can turn that duty into dependable help.
