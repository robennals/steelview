[Immigration is a small part of why homes cost so much](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise); the answer to the pressure that remains is to build."

---
Britain is richer with the people who come here to work, and it should go on welcoming them. The government's advisers model [one year's intake on the Skilled Worker route as a lifetime gain of £47.1bn to the public finances](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost). In the Office for Budget Responsibility's scenarios, [200,000 fewer migrants a year would raise borrowing by £13.1 billion to £19.9 billion within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), and [total GDP is higher with more migration in every case](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-moves-with-migration).

The country is ageing, and migration slows it. [With no net migration there would be 641 people over 65 for every 1,000 of working age by 2074, against 525 on the central projection and 457 with high migration](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-slows-ageing-but-does-not-stop-it). Migration does not stop ageing, and [migrants' own old age reverses much of the early gain](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains). But the alternative is a country with fewer workers carrying more pensioners sooner. Migrants already keep services running: [about a fifth of NHS staff in England, and over a third of doctors, are not British](#fact-work-and-study-routes/fifth-of-nhs-staff), and [students from abroad pay close to half of university course fees](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/non-uk-fee-share) at a time when [a third of English universities are in deficit](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/a-third-of-universities-in-deficit).

Housing is the strongest objection, and it is mostly a building problem. A review for the government's advisers finds immigration explains [about 4–6% of the real rise in house prices over three decades](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise). [Building had already started falling while migration was at its peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-peaked-before-migration), and [net migration has fallen by four-fifths](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all) while [rents are still rising](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rents-are-still-rising). Migration does add demand: [rent growth halved as numbers fell](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell), and [migration is just over half of projected household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth). But [prices respond most where planning limits construction, and migrants are around 46% of London's construction workforce](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/local-versus-regional-effects). The fix is to build more, which needs those workers.

Workers already here lose little. The government's advisers find [average wage effects small, slightly negative at the very bottom and positive further up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/small-wage-effects). Low-paid routes with dependants [are net costs over a lifetime](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost), and selection should reflect that; it is no case against the workers who pay their way. Migrants also [found a disproportionate share of the fastest-growing firms](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/foreign-born-founders), in one count of a hundred companies.

Living standards stalled after 2019, but [Canada and Germany did worse than the UK, and other G7 economies stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike), and the OBR finds [migration's effect on output per person small, in either direction](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small). Turning workers away is no plan for productivity.

[Freedom to work and trade together](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) is how countries grow rich. Keep the doors open to people who come to work and study, and spend the gains on homes, transport and services so that growth is shared.
