Britain is richer with the people who come here to work. It should keep welcoming them, and build the homes and services a growing country needs.

### Skilled migrants pay far more in than they take out

[The government's migration advisers expect one year's skilled workers to add tens of billions of pounds to the public purse over their lives](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost). Cutting numbers would cost money. [In the government's budget watchdog's scenarios, lower migration raises borrowing within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing). And [the economy is bigger with more migration in every case the watchdog modelled](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-moves-with-migration).

### Migrants keep hospitals and universities running, and slow the ageing of the country

[About a fifth of NHS staff in England are not British, and over a third of doctors](#fact-work-and-study-routes/fifth-of-nhs-staff). [Students from abroad pay close to half of university course fees](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/non-uk-fee-share), and [a third of English universities are already in deficit](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/a-third-of-universities-in-deficit).

Britain is ageing. [With no net migration, there would be 641 people over 65 for every 1,000 of working age by 2074, against 525 on the main projection](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-slows-ageing-but-does-not-stop-it). While [migrants grow old too, which reverses much of their early gain](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains), the alternative is fewer workers carrying more pensioners, sooner.

### Immigration is a small part of why homes cost so much

[A review for the government's advisers found immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise). [Housebuilding was already falling while migration was at its peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-peaked-before-migration). And [net migration has fallen by four-fifths](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), yet [rents are still rising](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rents-are-still-rising).

Migration does add demand. [Rent growth halved as numbers fell](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell), and [migration is just over half of expected household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth). While both are true, they are a reason to build more homes, not to shut the door.

### Building more homes needs migrant workers

[Prices rise most where planning rules hold back building, and migrants are almost half of London's construction workers](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/local-versus-regional-effects). Fixing the planning rules would let Britain build. Cutting migration would take away many of the people who do the building.

### Cutting migration would do little for the people already here

[The government's advisers find wage effects small: slightly negative for the lowest paid, and positive further up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/small-wage-effects). While [low-paid routes with dependants cost more than they pay over a lifetime](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost), that is a reason to choose carefully. It is no case against the workers who pay their way.

[Output per person has stalled since 2019, but it stalled in other big economies too, and Canada and Germany did worse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike). [The watchdog finds migration's effect on output per person small, either way](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small). Migrants also start businesses: [in one count of a hundred fast-growing firms, over half had a founder born abroad](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/foreign-born-founders).

### Keep the door open, and spend the gains on homes and services

[Freedom to work and trade together](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) is how countries grow rich. Britain should keep its doors open to people who come to work and study. It should spend the gains on homes, transport and services, so that everyone shares in the growth.
