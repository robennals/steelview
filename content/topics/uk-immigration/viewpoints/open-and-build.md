Britain is richer with the people who come here to work. It should keep welcoming them, and build the homes and services a growing country needs.

### Skilled migrants pay far more in than they take out

[Skilled workers outside health and care add hundreds of thousands of pounds each over their lives](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-large-net-gain). [The government's migration advisers expect one year's skilled workers to add tens of billions of pounds to the public purse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/mac-cohort-adds-47bn). Cutting numbers would cost money. [In the government's budget watchdog's scenarios, lower migration raises borrowing within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing). And [the economy is bigger with more migration in every case the watchdog modelled](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-moves-with-migration).

### About a fifth of NHS staff are not British, and universities and an ageing Britain need migrants too

[About a fifth of NHS staff in England are not British, and over a third of doctors](#fact-work-and-study-routes/fifth-of-nhs-staff). [Students from abroad pay close to half of university course fees](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/non-uk-fee-share), and [over a third of English universities are already in deficit](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/a-third-of-universities-in-deficit).

Britain is ageing. [With no net migration, there would be 641 people aged 65 or over for every 1,000 of working age by 2074, against 525 on the main projection](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/old-age-ratio-2074). While [migrants grow old too, which reverses much of their early gain](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains), the alternative is fewer workers carrying more pensioners, sooner.

### Immigration is a small part of why homes cost so much

[A review for the government's advisers found immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise). [Housebuilding peaked in 2019-20](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-peaked-before-migration), [net migration not until 2023](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-peaked). And [net migration has fallen by four-fifths](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), yet [rents are still rising](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rents-are-still-rising).

Migration does add demand. [Rent growth more than halved](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell) as [numbers fell](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), and [migration is just over half of expected household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth). While both are true, they are a reason to build more homes, not to shut the door.

### Building more homes needs migrant workers

[Immigration is more likely to raise prices where planning rules hold back building](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/local-versus-regional-effects), and [migrants are almost half of London's construction workers](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migrants-in-london-construction). Fixing the planning rules would let Britain build. Cutting migration would take away many of the people who do the building.

### Immigration moved wages only a few percent, so cutting it would do little for people here

[The government's advisers estimate that 24 years of EU immigration cut the lowest wages by about 5% and raised wages from the median up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/small-wage-effects). While [care workers, and adult dependants on the health and care route, cost more than they pay over a lifetime](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/care-route-net-cost), that is a reason to choose carefully. It is no case against the workers who pay their way.

[Output per person has barely grown since 2019, and Canada and Germany did worse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike). [The watchdog's scenarios put migration's effect on output per person at under 1%, up or down](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small). Migrants also start businesses: [in one count of a hundred fast-growing firms, over half had a founder born abroad](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/foreign-born-founders).

### Keep the door open, and spend the gains on homes and services

[Freedom to work and trade together](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) is how countries grow rich. Britain should keep its doors open to people who come to work and study. It should spend the gains on homes, transport and services, so that everyone shares in the growth.
