Britain has taken in more people than it can house, and its people are no richer for it. A government's first duty is to its own people, so Parliament should cut immigration hard and keep it down.

### Britain has just had the biggest wave of immigration on record

More people moved to Britain in each year from 2021 to 2024 than in any earlier year since the record began in 1964. In those four years, net migration added about 2.5 million people. Arrivals have fallen since, but 2025 was still higher than any year before 2018. Migration is now almost all of the country's population growth.

### The extra people made the economy bigger, but not the people richer

Since 2019, output per person has barely moved, even as the economy grew. Extra migrants add to the size of the economy. But in the budget watchdog's scenarios, they change output per person by under 1%, up or down. If buildings and equipment do not keep up, extra migrants leave it lower, and public investment does not grow straight away when the population does. While Canada and Germany did worse, which shows migration is not the only cause, a bigger economy is still not a richer people.

### Homes have not kept up with the extra households

Migration accounts for just over half of the extra households England expects. Yet England is adding fewer homes each year than the households projected. About a third of private renters were born abroad. While immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices, in countries like Britain it raises rents by more, and rent is what people without a home of their own pay.

### Low-paid routes bring people who cost more than they pay in

Care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost the public purse more than they pay over their lives. So do people who come to join a partner who is British or settled here. Skilled workers outside health and care pay in far more. While the average resident is a lifetime cost too, adding people at a cost does not help those already here. And while lower migration raises borrowing within five years, that window ends before the costs that come as migrants grow old.

### The fall in numbers rests on rules a future government can undo

Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths from its peak, as the rules for work and study visas tightened. A future minister can loosen those rules again. Promises have failed before: since the 2010 pledge of "tens of thousands", net migration has fallen below 100,000 only in 2020, during Covid. Later Conservative and Labour pledges named no number at all.

### So Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait to settle

Most people want immigration reduced, and a country should have a say over who joins it. So Parliament should set a binding yearly cap on net migration in law, and close the low-paid routes that cost more than they bring in. It should also lengthen the wait for settlement, the right to stay for good. About 1.6 million people are expected to settle by 2030, and once settled, they can claim benefits on the same terms as British citizens.
