---
name: "Mass immigration has left Britain poorer per head and short of homes: cut it hard and keep it down"
summary: "Recent immigration [made the economy bigger without making its people richer](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew), and [added households faster than homes were built](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). The fall in numbers rests on rules a future government could undo; Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait for settlement."
order: 1
---
Britain has just had [more immigration than at any time on record](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/gross-immigration-is-far-above-any-historical-norm-even-after-two-years-of-falling): [net migration over 2021–24 added about 2.5 million people](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net), [almost all of the country's population growth](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-is-almost-all-population-growth). Over the same years [total GDP grew while GDP per head barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew). A bigger economy that is no richer per person is no gain to the people already living in it.

The reason is simple enough. In the Office for Budget Responsibility's scenarios, [extra migrants leave GDP per person lower if the stock of capital does not keep up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [public investment does not respond quickly to a larger population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust). The OBR calls [the overall effect on output per person small and of uncertain sign](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [other large economies stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike). Rapid population growth has bought nothing measurable per head, which is reason enough to stop it.

Housing shows the cost most plainly. [Migration accounts for just over half of the household growth the ONS projects for England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth), while [building has run below projected household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). As net migration fell, [rent growth dropped to less than half its 2024 peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell). Immigration explains [only about 4–6% of the three-decade rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), but the same review finds [rent effects at least as large as price effects](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects), and [recent migrants mostly rent privately](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migrants-concentrated-in-private-renting), competing with young Britons for the same flats.

Who comes matters as much as how many. [Skilled Workers are a large net gain, but care workers and Health and Care dependants are lifetime net costs](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost). [The average resident is a net cost too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/average-resident-is-a-lifetime-net-cost), but that is no reason to add more people at the same cost. [Lower migration raises borrowing over the OBR's five-year horizon](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), but [that window leaves out the costs that come as migrants age](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/five-year-window), and [lower migration lifts public spending per person](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/lower-migration-raises-spending-per-person), which is what residents actually receive.

[Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), largely through [tighter scrutiny of employers and tighter visa rules](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/why-immigration-fell) that a minister can relax. Promises have not held before: [“tens of thousands” was met only during Covid](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/tens-of-thousands-met-only-in-covid), and [later pledges named no number](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/later-pledges-unquantified). [Most people want immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and [democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) means that wish should bind.

A government's [first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people). Parliament should set a binding annual cap on net migration, close the low-paid routes that cost more than they contribute, and lengthen the wait for settlement before [the 1.6 million expected to qualify by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million) make today's costs permanent.
