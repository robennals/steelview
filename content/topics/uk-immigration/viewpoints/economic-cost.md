Britain has taken in more people than it can house, and its people are no richer for it. [A government's first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people). Parliament should cut immigration hard and make sure it stays down.

### Britain has just had more immigration than at any time on record

[More people moved to Britain in recent years than at any time since the record began in 1964](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/record-immigration-2021-to-2024). [In just four years, net migration added about 2.5 million people](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net). [It now makes up almost all of the country's population growth](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-is-almost-all-population-growth).

### Output per person has barely moved since 2019

[Output per person barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/gdp-per-head-barely-grew), even as [total output grew](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew). A bigger economy that is no richer per person does nothing for the people already here.

The government's budget watchdog describes how this can happen. [Extra migrants leave output per person lower if buildings and equipment do not keep up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/capital-does-not-adjust). And [much public investment is fixed up to the end of a spending period, so it does not respond straight away to the population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust). While [the watchdog's scenarios put the effect on output per person at under 1%, up or down](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [Canada and Germany stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike), that is still no gain. Millions more people have added almost nothing per head.

### Migration is just over half of England's expected new households, and building has fallen short

[Migration accounts for just over half of the extra households expected in England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth), and [building has fallen short of projected household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). [Only 17% of recent migrants own their home](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/recent-migrants-rarely-own), and [about a third of private renters are born abroad](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migrants-concentrated-in-private-renting). So they compete with other renters for the same flats. [As net migration fell](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), [rent growth dropped to less than half its peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell).

While [immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), [in countries like Britain its effect on rents is larger than on prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects). Rent is what young people without a home of their own actually pay.

### Low-paid routes leave costs that last for decades

Who comes matters as much as how many. [Skilled workers are a large gain to the public purse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-large-net-gain), but [care workers and the adult family members of health and care workers cost more than they pay over their lives](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/care-route-net-cost). While [the average resident is also a lifetime cost](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/average-resident-is-a-lifetime-net-cost), that is no reason to add more people at the same cost.

While [lower migration raises government borrowing within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), that window ends long before [the costs that come as migrants grow old](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains). [Lower migration also raises public spending per person](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/lower-migration-raises-spending-per-person), and that is what residents actually receive.

### Net migration has fallen, but on rules a minister can undo

[Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths from its peak](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all). But [work visas began to fall as officials checked employers more closely](#fact-work-and-study-routes/scrutiny-began-the-fall), and [net migration kept falling as visa rules tightened](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/why-immigration-fell). A future minister can undo both. Promises have failed before. [Net migration has fallen to the promised "tens of thousands" only in 2020, during Covid](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/tens-of-thousands-met-only-in-covid). [Later Conservative and Labour pledges named no number](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/later-pledges-unquantified).

### Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait to settle

[Most people want immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and a country should have [a say over who joins it](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership). That wish should bind future governments. Parliament should set a binding yearly cap on net migration and close the low-paid routes that cost more than they bring in. It should also lengthen the wait for settlement, the right to stay for good. [About 1.6 million people are expected to settle by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million). Once they settle, today's costs become permanent.
