Britain has taken in more people than it can house, and its people are no richer for it. [A government's first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people). Parliament should cut immigration hard and make sure it stays down.

### Britain has just had the biggest wave of immigration on record

[More people moved to Britain in each year from 2021 to 2024 than in any year since the record began in 1964](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/record-immigration-2021-to-2024). [In those four years, net migration added about 2.5 million people](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net).

[Migration now makes up almost all of the country's population growth](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-is-almost-all-population-growth).

### Britain's economy grew, but its people are barely any richer

Output per person grew for decades. But [since 2019 it has barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/gdp-per-head-barely-grew), even as [the economy as a whole grew](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew). So the people already here are, on average, no better off.

The government's budget watchdog shows how this can happen. In its scenarios, [extra migrants leave output per person lower if buildings and equipment do not keep up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/capital-does-not-adjust). And [much public investment does not respond straight away to a bigger population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust). While [the watchdog puts the effect on output per person at under 1%, up or down](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [Canada and Germany stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike), its figures still promise no real gain per head.

### Migration is expected to add half of England's new households, and building falls short

[Migration accounts for just over half of the extra households expected in England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth). Yet [England is adding fewer homes each year than the households projected](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). So homes are not keeping up with the households that migration adds.

[Only 17% of recent migrants own their home](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/recent-migrants-rarely-own), and [about a third of private renters were born abroad](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migrants-concentrated-in-private-renting). [As net migration fell](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all), [rent growth dropped to less than half its peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell). While [immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), [in countries like Britain it raises rents more than prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects). Rent is what young people without a home of their own pay.

### Low-paid routes bring people who cost more than they pay in

[Skilled workers outside health and care are a large gain to the public purse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-large-net-gain). But [care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost more than they pay over their lives](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/care-route-net-cost). Who comes matters as much as how many.

While [the average resident is also a lifetime cost](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/average-resident-is-a-lifetime-net-cost), that is no reason to add more people at the same cost. And while [lower migration raises government borrowing within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), that window ends long before [the costs that come as migrants grow old](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains). [Lower migration also raises public spending per person](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/lower-migration-raises-spending-per-person), which is what residents receive.

### The fall in numbers rests on rules a future government can undo

[Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths from its peak](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all). But [net migration for work and study fell as visa rules tightened](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/why-immigration-fell). A future minister can loosen those rules again.

[Work visas began to fall as officials checked employers more closely](#fact-work-and-study-routes/scrutiny-began-the-fall), and checks can be relaxed. Promises have failed before. [Since the 2010 pledge, net migration has reached the promised "tens of thousands" only in 2020, during Covid](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/tens-of-thousands-met-only-in-covid). [Later Conservative and Labour pledges named no number](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/later-pledges-unquantified).

### So Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait to settle

[Most people want immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and a country should have [a say over who joins it](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership). So Parliament should set a binding yearly cap on net migration. It should close the low-paid routes that cost more than they bring in.

It should also lengthen the wait for settlement, the right to stay for good. [About 1.6 million people are expected to settle by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million). [Once settled, they can claim most of the benefits that people on temporary visas cannot](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/settlement-unlocks-benefits).
