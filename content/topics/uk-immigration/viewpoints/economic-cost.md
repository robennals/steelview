Britain has taken in more people than it can house, and its people are no richer for it. [A government's first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people). Parliament should cut immigration hard and make sure it stays down.

### Britain has just had more immigration than ever before

[More people moved to Britain in recent years than at any time on record](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/gross-immigration-is-far-above-any-historical-norm-even-after-two-years-of-falling). Net migration is the number who arrive minus the number who leave. [In just four years, it added about 2.5 million people](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net). [It now makes up almost all of the country's population growth](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-is-almost-all-population-growth).

### The economy grew, but people did not get richer

[Total output grew, but output per person barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew). A bigger economy that is no richer per person does nothing for the people already here.

The government's budget watchdog explains how this happens. [Extra migrants leave output per person lower if buildings and equipment do not keep up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small). And [public investment is fixed years ahead, so it does not grow with the population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust). While [the watchdog judges the overall effect on output per person to be small, either way](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [other large economies stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike), that is still no gain. Millions more people have bought nothing measurable per head.

### Newcomers compete with young Britons for scarce homes

[Migration accounts for just over half of the extra households expected in England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth), and [building has fallen short of household growth](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). [Recent migrants mostly rent, and about a third of private renters were born abroad](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migrants-concentrated-in-private-renting). So they compete with young Britons for the same flats. [As net migration fell, rent growth dropped to less than half its peak](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-growth-fell-as-migration-fell).

While [immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), [its effect on rents is at least as large as on prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects). Rent is what young people without a home of their own actually pay.

### Low-paid routes leave costs that last for decades

Who comes matters as much as how many. [Skilled workers are a large gain to the public purse, but care workers and the adult family members of health and care workers cost more than they pay over their lives](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost). While [the average resident is also a lifetime cost](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/average-resident-is-a-lifetime-net-cost), that is no reason to add more people at the same cost.

While [lower migration raises government borrowing over the next five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), [that window leaves out the costs that come as migrants grow old](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/five-year-window). [Lower migration also raises public spending per person](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/lower-migration-raises-spending-per-person), and that is what residents actually receive.

### The fall in numbers will not last on its own

[Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths from its peak](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all). But [it fell as officials checked employers more closely and tightened visa rules](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/why-immigration-fell), and a future minister can undo both. Promises have failed before. [Net migration fell to the promised "tens of thousands" only during Covid](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/tens-of-thousands-met-only-in-covid). [Later pledges named no number at all](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/later-pledges-unquantified).

### Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait to settle

[Most people want immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and a country should have [a say over who joins it](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership). That wish should bind future governments. Parliament should set a binding yearly cap on net migration and close the low-paid routes that cost more than they bring in. It should also lengthen the wait for settlement, the right to stay for good. [About 1.6 million people are expected to qualify by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million). Once they settle, today's costs become permanent.
