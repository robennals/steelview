Britain has taken in more people than it can house, and its people are no richer for it. A government's first duty is to its own people. Parliament should cut immigration hard and make sure it stays down.

### Britain has just had the biggest wave of immigration on record

[More people moved to Britain in each year from 2021 to 2024 than in any year since the record began in 1964](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/record-immigration-2021-to-2024). [In those four years, net migration added about 2.5 million people](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/four-years-gross-and-net). [Migration now makes up almost all of the country's population growth](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/migration-is-almost-all-population-growth).

### Britain's economy grew, but its people are barely any richer

Output per person grew for decades. But [since 2019 it has barely moved](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/gdp-per-head-barely-grew), even as [the economy as a whole grew](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/total-gdp-grew). So the people already here are, on average, no better off. In the budget watchdog's scenarios, [extra migrants leave output per person lower if buildings and equipment do not keep up](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/capital-does-not-adjust), and [much public investment does not respond straight away to a bigger population](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/investment-does-not-adjust). While [the watchdog puts the effect on output per person at under 1%, up or down](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/obr-per-head-effect-small), and [Canada and Germany stalled too](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/g7-stalled-alike), its figures still promise no real gain per head.

### Migration is expected to add half of England's new households, and building falls short

[Migration accounts for just over half of the extra households expected in England](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/migration-share-of-household-growth). Yet [England is adding fewer homes each year than the households projected](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/building-below-household-growth). So homes are not keeping up with the households that migration adds. While [immigration explains only a small part of the long rise in house prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/immigration-explains-4-6-percent-of-house-price-rise), [in countries like Britain it raises rents more than prices](#fact-immigration-housing-and-public-services/rent-effects). Rent is what young people without a home of their own pay.

### Low-paid routes bring people who cost more than they pay in

[Skilled workers outside health and care are a large gain to the public purse](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-large-net-gain). But care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost more than they pay over their lives. Who comes matters as much as how many. While the average resident is also a lifetime cost, that is no reason to add more people at the same cost. And while [lower migration raises government borrowing within five years](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/lower-migration-raises-borrowing), that window ends long before [the costs that come as migrants grow old](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/old-age-reverses-gains).

### The fall in numbers rests on rules a future government can undo

[Net migration has fallen by about four-fifths from its peak](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/net-migration-is-not-unusual-at-all). But [net migration for work and study fell as visa rules tightened](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/why-immigration-fell). A future minister can loosen those rules again. Promises have failed before. [Since the 2010 pledge, net migration has reached the promised "tens of thousands" only in 2020, during Covid](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/tens-of-thousands-met-only-in-covid).

### So Parliament should set a binding cap and lengthen the wait to settle

[Most people want immigration reduced](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/public-preferences), and a country should have a say over who joins it. So Parliament should set a binding yearly cap on net migration, and close the low-paid routes that cost more than they bring in. It should also lengthen the wait to settle. [About 1.6 million people are expected to settle by 2030](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/forecast-1-6-million), and [once settled, they can claim most of the benefits that people on temporary visas cannot](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/settlement-unlocks-benefits).
