Britain should welcome people who want to work, contribute and build a life here. The [freedom to cooperate and exchange](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) is worth protecting: when someone wants to offer their skills and someone else wants to employ them, there should be a good reason to stand in their way.

Immigration brings benefits we have reason to value. [Skilled workers are projected to contribute more in taxes than they receive in public spending over their lifetimes](#fact-skilled-worker-fiscal-gain-concentrated/the-route-as-a-whole-is-clearly-positive). That contribution can help pay for services we all use. And the contribution is also the work itself: [doctors, nurses and care workers are among the occupations most reliant on people born outside Europe](#fact-health-and-care-relies-on-migrant-workers/care-and-health-professional-roles-have-the-highest-non-eu-born-shares-of-any-occupat). Caring for someone matters even when the job does not command a high salary.

Welcoming newcomers should go together with protecting people already struggling. [Research finds small overall effects on UK-born workers’ wages, with some losses among lower-paid workers](#fact-wage-effects-small-and-uneven/what-is-established-is-a-small-unevenly-distributed-effect). Those losses deserve attention. Giving [priority to the worst off](#principle-priority-to-the-worst-off) means enforcing decent pay and working conditions, and helping workers improve their prospects. It does not follow that we should close opportunities to everyone else.

Housing needs the same practical approach. [Immigration adds to housing pressure where building cannot respond](#fact-housing-cost-effect-positive-but-small/the-direction-is-not-in-doubt). Welcoming more people therefore carries a responsibility to provide more homes and infrastructure. A government that chooses immigration should also make room for it, with investment reaching the places taking in newcomers.

There are real choices about who comes and how quickly. [Arrivals remain high by historical standards despite falling from their peak](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/gross-immigration-is-far-above-any-historical-norm-even-after-two-years-of-falling), and [some lower-paid routes are projected to cost the public finances over a lifetime](#fact-care-worker-route-fiscally-negative/finding). We should weigh those costs against the work people do and fund the commitments we make. The right response is a welcoming policy with a credible plan for growth: let people contribute, protect those who bear the costs, and build the capacity to share the benefits.
