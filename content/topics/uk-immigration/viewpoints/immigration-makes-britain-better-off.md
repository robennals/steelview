Britain has often been at its best when it has [drawn in people with the nerve to leave home and the ambition to make something new](#needed). Its strength is not that it has frozen an old way of life in place. It is that it has [remained unusually open to experiment, enterprise and ideas from elsewhere](#needed). The [freedom to cooperate and exchange](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) is part of that inheritance.

People who arrive bring more than a labour-market category. [They start companies, make discoveries, practise professions and pass ideas between Britain and the rest of the world](#needed). Some will be obvious successes on arrival; others will not. [A country that admits only people whose value can be predicted from a salary or a qualification will miss people whose enterprise and creativity become clear only after they have been given a chance](#needed).

This is why a welcoming policy should not be limited to the already wealthy. [Skilled migration is projected to make a substantial net contribution to the public finances](#fact-skilled-worker-fiscal-gain-concentrated/the-route-as-a-whole-is-clearly-positive), but public finance is only one measure of what a person can add. [Health and care rely heavily on workers born outside Europe](#fact-health-and-care-relies-on-migrant-workers/care-and-health-professional-roles-have-the-highest-non-eu-born-shares-of-any-occupat). Their work makes possible the lives and careers of other people, even where a spreadsheet cannot capture its full value.

Nor should fear of general economic harm dominate the case. [Research finds small overall effects on UK-born workers’ wages, though lower-paid workers can lose out](#fact-wage-effects-small-and-uneven/what-is-established-is-a-small-unevenly-distributed-effect). Those workers deserve protection: better pay, stronger bargaining power and real opportunities to advance. Giving [priority to the worst off](#principle-priority-to-the-worst-off) means addressing a concentrated harm directly, rather than closing the country to people who could create opportunities for everyone.

Welcoming people also requires doing the practical work. [Immigration adds to housing pressure where building does not keep up](#fact-housing-cost-effect-positive-but-small/the-direction-is-not-in-doubt). Britain should build homes, fund infrastructure and make it easier for newcomers and established residents to share in growth. The failure to do that is a failure of government, not proof that openness itself was a mistake.

There are good reasons to improve individual routes, and [arrivals remain high by historical standards](#fact-immigration-against-the-long-run/gross-immigration-is-far-above-any-historical-norm-even-after-two-years-of-falling). But Britain should not let a desire for control turn into a suspicion of outsiders. A confident country welcomes people with something to offer, gives unexpected talent room to emerge, and lets the best of many cultures become part of a changing British story.
