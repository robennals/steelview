Immigration is not good or bad in bulk. It depends on who comes and what they do. Britain should choose people for what they contribute, and make the right to stay for good something they earn.

### The gain comes from who comes, not how many

On average, skilled workers outside health and care each add hundreds of thousands of pounds to the public purse over their lives. Care workers, and the adult family members of health and care workers, cost more than they pay. Even the gain is concentrated: among skilled workers outside health and care, the top tenth of earners provide almost two-fifths of it. And for lower earners, a temporary stay is better for the public purse than a permanent one.

### When Britain loosened the rules, low-paid visas surged

In 2020 the skill bar for a work visa was cut to A-level standard, and in 2022 care workers were added. Work visas more than doubled in two years, and at the peak, health and care visas went to more family members than workers. The Home Office then cancelled a record number of employers' licences to hire migrants, with adult social care among the worst sectors. The record is in numbers: with four times as many licensed employers, the share cancelled is back to its mid-2010s level.

### Care has coped as overseas recruitment fell, so far

Since overseas recruitment ran down, care vacancies have not risen, and the vacancy rate is the lowest in ten years. That is even though care-worker visas collapsed in late 2023, once officials checked employers more closely. While the British share of care jobs is still falling, and care pay sits just above the legal minimum, the answer is to raise pay and recruit at home, not to reopen the route.

### Every route must be used for its purpose

In 2025, almost two in five people claiming asylum had held a visa, up from about one in eight in 2021. Claims from people on the graduate visa more than doubled in a year. A visa granted to study or work should be used to study or work.

### Settlement should follow contribution

In work, family-visa holders and the dependants of health and care workers earn well under half what skilled workers on the main visa earn, and about half of adult skilled-worker dependants were not working at all. Settlement opens up the benefits that people on temporary visas cannot claim, so it should go to those who have paid in. Hundreds of thousands would not yet meet the proposed rule that they be in work, and a longer route gives them time to.

### So select hard, police each route, and make settlement earned

Britain should keep the higher skill and salary bars, and police employers and routes for their purpose. Settlement should take ten years as standard, and less for those who earn and pay in the most. That should apply to everyone not yet settled, since the rules that apply are those in force when a person applies, but people already settled should keep their status.

While a ten-year standard wait would make Britain an outlier among rich countries, that is a fair price on the lower-paid routes. The routes for the most talented are a small slice of work visas, and they should grow.
