Immigration is not good or bad in bulk. It depends on who comes and what they do. Britain should choose people for what they contribute, and make the right to stay for good something they earn.

### The biggest gain comes from skilled workers outside health and care

The government's migration advisers have worked out what each group adds to or takes from the public purse over a lifetime. Skilled workers outside health and care add hundreds of thousands of pounds each. But care workers and adult dependants on the health and care route cost money. The gain is concentrated: among skilled workers outside health and care, the top tenth of earners provide almost two-fifths of it. And for lower earners, a temporary stay is better for the public purse than a permanent one.

### When Britain loosened the rules, low-paid visas and abuse surged

In 2020 the skill bar for a work visa was cut to A-level, and in 2022 care workers were added. At the peak, health and care visas went to more family members than workers. The Home Office cancelled a record number of employers' licences to hire migrants, with adult social care among the worst sectors. While cancellations, as a share of employers with licences, are only back to their mid-2010s level, that is because the number of those employers roughly quadrupled.

### Care has coped without overseas recruits so far

Since overseas recruitment ran down, care vacancies have not risen, and the vacancy rate is the lowest in ten years. That is even though care-worker visas collapsed in late 2023, once officials checked employers more closely. While the British share of care jobs is still falling, and care pay sits just above the legal minimum, the answer is to raise pay and recruit at home, not to reopen the route.

### Every route must be used for its purpose

In 2025, almost two in five people claiming asylum had held a visa, up from under a third before 2020. Claims from people on the graduate visa more than doubled in a year. A visa granted to study or work should be used to study or work.

### Settlement should follow contribution

Settlement gives a person most of the benefits that people on temporary visas cannot claim. Those who contribute earn a share, and a government's first duty is to its own people, who pay for what settlement unlocks.

Hundreds of thousands would not yet meet the proposed rule that they be in work. A longer route gives them time to. While a ten-year standard wait would make Britain an outlier among rich countries, that is a fair price on the lower-paid routes. High earners would keep a short path.

### So select hard, police each route, and make settlement earned

Britain should keep the higher skill and salary bars, and police employers and routes for their purpose. Settlement should take ten years as standard, and less for those who earn and pay in the most.

The routes for the most talented are a small slice of work visas, and they should grow.
