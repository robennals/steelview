Britain should decide what it wants immigration to achieve, then design the rules around those purposes. [People come to study, work and seek protection](#fact-how-people-actually-arrive/study-is-the-largest-single-reason-anyone-moves-to-britain). A student taking a university course, a nurse taking a job and a family seeking safety raise different questions. Treating every arrival as an identical addition to a target tells us little about whether the policy is succeeding.

For work migration, contribution should matter. [The projected gains to the public finances from skilled migration are concentrated among higher earners](#fact-skilled-worker-fiscal-gain-concentrated/chart-earnings-concentration). That gives Britain a reason to welcome people with valuable skills and strong earning prospects. The [freedom to cooperate and exchange](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) should make us reluctant to block a useful working relationship merely to lower a headline total.

But salary cannot be the only test. [Health and care are particularly reliant on workers born outside Europe](#fact-health-and-care-relies-on-migrant-workers/care-and-health-professional-roles-have-the-highest-non-eu-born-shares-of-any-occupat). [Care workers are projected to cost more in public spending than they pay in taxes](#fact-care-worker-route-fiscally-negative/finding), yet [that calculation leaves out wider benefits of the care they provide](#fact-care-worker-route-fiscally-negative/it-counts-fiscal-flows-only). If we want to admit people for socially valuable work, we should explain why and be willing to fund the choice. Low pay does not make useful work worthless.

The rules can change who comes. [Opening and then restricting the care-worker route produced a dramatic rise and fall in visa grants](#fact-care-worker-route-opened-then-closed/one-rule-change-accounts-for-much-of-both-the-rise-and-the-fall-in-the-headline-numbe). We should use that power deliberately. Each route should have a clear purpose, realistic expectations about settlement and public costs, and regular scrutiny of whether it delivers what was promised.

That scrutiny should be democratic. [Public preferences differ substantially between immigration routes](#fact-immigration-is-salient-and-objection-varies-by-route/the-public-does-not-hold-one-view-of-immigration-it-sorts-by-route), so [democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) requires a more useful choice than being for or against immigration as a whole. Set out whom we intend to welcome, what they can contribute and what we owe them in return. Success should mean an immigration system that serves those purposes, even when that calls for expanding one route and restricting another.
