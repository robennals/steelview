---
name: We need the right kind of immigration, not simply less of it
summary: "Choose immigration for what it contributes. Welcome skills and useful work, honour the purpose of each route, and judge success by more than the number of arrivals."
order: 1
---
Britain should judge immigration by what it contributes, not by whether the headline number rises or falls. [People arrive for study, work and protection](#fact-how-people-actually-arrive/study-is-the-largest-single-reason-anyone-moves-to-britain), and those routes ask different things of the country. A system that treats a scientist building a new industry, a student, a care worker and a person seeking safety as interchangeable cannot make sensible choices about any of them.

The best work routes [bring skills Britain needs, create high-value work and strengthen its ability to compete](#needed). They should also make it easier for newcomers to join a common national life: [English, an expectation of obeying the law, and a serious commitment to living here as fellow citizens all matter for integration](#needed). The [freedom to cooperate and exchange](#principle-gains-from-open-exchange) gives us a positive reason to welcome people whose talent and enterprise can make the country stronger.

There is evidence that the composition of migration matters. [The projected fiscal gains from skilled migration are concentrated among higher earners](#fact-skilled-worker-fiscal-gain-concentrated/chart-earnings-concentration). This is not a reason to value people by their salary alone; useful work can matter beyond a tax calculation. But it is a reason to be selective, especially when [a route serves domestic demand for low-paid labour and leaves costs for public services and local communities](#needed).

The care-worker route shows why that distinction cannot be wished away. [Health and care rely heavily on people born outside Europe](#fact-health-and-care-relies-on-migrant-workers/care-and-health-professional-roles-have-the-highest-non-eu-born-shares-of-any-occupat), while [care workers are projected to cost more in public spending than they contribute in tax](#fact-care-worker-route-fiscally-negative/finding). Those workers do valuable jobs and the fiscal model does not capture every benefit of their work. Yet [dependence on large-scale overseas recruitment can substitute for retaining British staff, improving management and pay, and using technology](#needed). That is a harder question than simply how many visas to issue.

[Opening and then restricting the care-worker route changed visa grants dramatically](#fact-care-worker-route-opened-then-closed/one-rule-change-accounts-for-much-of-both-the-rise-and-the-fall-in-the-headline-numbe). Britain can therefore choose a different mix. I would make it easier for people with scarce skills in export-oriented industries to come, while expecting English and genuine integration. I would reduce routes that mainly import low-paid labour to meet needs Britain should address at home. Protection for people in danger remains a separate duty, but [its scope and practical consequences have changed with newer interpretations of asylum](#needed) and should be openly debated rather than folded into an undifferentiated immigration total.

[Democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) means giving the public that real choice. The aim is neither to shut Britain off nor to accept every form of immigration on the same terms. It is to welcome the people and routes that make the country more capable, cohesive and prosperous, while reducing those that do not.
