---
name: People who came lawfully on a path to settlement should be allowed to finish it
summary: "[About 2.2 million people](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/path-2-2-million) built their lives around a five-year path to settlement that the state set out. Change the terms for people who have not yet come, but let those already on the path finish it."
order: 4
---
[About 2.2 million people were on a five-year path to settlement at the end of 2024](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/path-2-2-million). They took jobs, moved families, enrolled children in school and [paid thousands of pounds in fees and surcharges](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/surcharge-matches-average-use) on terms the state set out. Changing those terms halfway through tells them, and everyone deciding whether to come, that Britain's word is worth nothing.

The state has bound itself this way before. When the Home Office toughened the rules for highly skilled migrants already in the country, [the High Court held that the scheme had made “a clear representation” and that changing it was unfair](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/high-court-struck-down-mid-route-change-2008). The Home Affairs Committee found it [“common” for the Home Office to protect people already on a route](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/transitional-protection-precedent). For Hong Kong's British Nationals (Overseas), the route was framed as [“a historic and moral commitment”](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/guidance-said-may-be-able-to-apply). The skilled-worker guidance tells applicants only that they [“may be able to apply”](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/guidance-said-may-be-able-to-apply), and [there is precedent for changing rules mid-route](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/transitional-protection-precedent). But [rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say) in the sense people reasonably took them, and nobody uproots a family for “may” unless they trust the state to mean it.

Keeping the promise also keeps people Britain wants. [Skilled Worker main applicants earn a median £56,600 and Health and Care main applicants £30,900](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/earnings-by-route), and the government's advisers model [skilled main applicants who stay as worth far more to the public finances than those who leave](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/gain-from-top-earners-and-stayers). Not everyone pays their way: [around half of adult Skilled Worker dependants were not working](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/non-working-groups), and [care workers are a net cost over a lifetime](#fact-immigration-economy-and-public-finances/skilled-workers-gain-care-workers-cost). That is an argument for setting different terms for future arrivals, not for breaking them for people already here. And they have paid: [indefinite leave costs £3,226 against a processing cost of £310](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/ilr-fee-ten-times-cost), on top of [a health surcharge paid every year in addition to tax](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/surcharge-matches-average-use).

A ten-year wait would [make the UK an outlier among high-income countries](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/ten-year-baseline-outlier); whether that drives skilled people elsewhere [is not established](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/behaviour-evidence-limited). Insecure status itself does harm. In the Windrush scandal, [people entitled to live here were detained, removed and denied work, housing and services](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/windrush-record). Reopening the status of lawful residents risks repeating that.

The public draws the line in the same place. [Removing settlement from people who hold it is opposed 58% to 29%](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/revoking-opposed-58-29), while [ending it for new arrivals splits the country evenly, and majorities want to keep it for people in full-time work and NHS staff](#fact-settlement-and-migrant-status/ending-for-new-arrivals-splits). Parliament may set any terms it likes for people who have not yet come. Everyone who arrived on a five-year path should be able to finish it on the terms they were given.
