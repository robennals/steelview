---
name: Priority to the worst off
heldBy: [open-and-welcoming, restore-control, honour-the-refugee-obligation, keep-the-place-recognisable]
---
A policy should be judged first by what it does to the people with the least
bargaining power, not by its effect on the average. Averages hide distribution,
and a change that makes a country richer while making its poorest residents worse
off has not obviously improved anything.

This principle is shared and its application splits hard, because the viewpoints
disagree about who the worst off are. One reading counts the low-paid British
worker and the tenant in an area where nothing is being built — the people with
least ability to move away from a change they did not choose. Another counts the
care worker on a sponsored visa who cannot leave an abusive employer, or the
person waiting three years for a decision. A third notes that on any global
measure the worst off are the people the policy excludes entirely, and that
whether they count at all is the real question.
