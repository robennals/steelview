---
name: Gains from open exchange
heldBy: [the-right-kind-of-immigration-not-less-of-it, immigration-makes-britain-better-off]
---
When people, goods and ideas can move to where they are most useful, more is
produced than when they cannot, and both sides of the exchange are usually better
off. This is the oldest well-evidenced claim in economics and it does not stop
applying at a border. It is also not unlimited: the gains are real but they are
averages, they can be captured unequally, and they can be swamped by the costs of
adjusting too fast.

Those who hold this principle differ on how much of the gain is conditional. One
reading is that the exchange is beneficial roughly wherever it is voluntary.
Another is that it is beneficial where it is priced honestly, and that a labour
market with a subsidised floor is not an open exchange but a distorted one.
