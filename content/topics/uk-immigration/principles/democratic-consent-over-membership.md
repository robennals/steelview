name: Democratic consent over membership

Decisions that change who belongs to a political community should be made by that
community, openly, and by people who can be removed for making them badly. This is
not a claim about who deserves to belong — it is a claim about where the authority
to decide sits. A government that changes the composition of the electorate
through administrative discretion, without ever putting the choice to a vote, has
done something different in kind from a government that argues for a policy and
wins.

Every viewpoint on this page holds this, and they differ on what it licenses. For
some it implies that a settled population may set a number, or a rate, and expect
it to be honoured. For others it implies only that the reasons must be given in
public and defended at an election — and that a majority preference does not by
itself extinguish obligations already incurred to particular people.
