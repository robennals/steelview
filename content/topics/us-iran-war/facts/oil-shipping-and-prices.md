## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-brent-monthly}

### Brent nearly doubled by the April peak and rose again in the autumn {#oil-shipping-and-prices--brent-nearly-doubled}

Brent went from $71.32 a barrel on the last trading day before the strikes to $138.21 in early April. [1](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-1)

After falling in June and July, it climbed again to $135.51 in early October. [1](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-1)

The latest daily price, $123.10 on 27 October, was still nearly three-quarters above the prewar level. [1](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-1)

### The 2026 peak is below the 2008 record in money terms {#oil-shipping-and-prices--below-2008-nominal}

Brent's daily record is $143.95, set in July 2008. [1](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-1) Its high in 2022 was $133.18. [1](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-1)

Monthly averages in 2026 also stayed below the 2008 peak (chart). [21](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-21)

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-wti-monthly}

### US crude rose less than Brent {#oil-shipping-and-prices--wti-rose-less}

WTI, the US benchmark, went from $66.96 before the strikes to $114.58 at its April peak. [2](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-2) In proportion, its rise was smaller than Brent's.

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-real-crude-price}

### Adjusted for inflation, crude stayed below the 1981, 2008 and 2022 peaks {#oil-shipping-and-prices--below-earlier-real-peaks}

In October 2026 dollars, the refiner price of imported crude peaked at $98.85 in May 2026 (chart). [23](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-23)

That is well below January 1981, July 2008 and June 2022 in the same series, and above the 1974 and 1990 levels. [23](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-23) EIA's estimates for September and October 2026 are close to May's, so the autumn may set a slightly higher 2026 peak.

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-gasoline-monthly}

### Petrol rose by about half, and has fallen only slightly {#oil-shipping-and-prices--pump-prices-up-by-half}

The weekly US average for regular rose from $2.937 a gallon just before the war to $4.500 in mid-May. [3](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-3)

In the latest week it was $4.269, below the record of $5.006 set in mid-2022. [3](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-3)

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-cpi-september-2026}

### Gasoline prices drove most of the rise in inflation {#oil-shipping-and-prices--gasoline-cpi-up-a-third}

BLS reports gasoline up 34.2 percent over the year to September, accounting for most of the energy index's rise. [4](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-4) Prices excluding food and energy rose far less (chart).

### CBO attributes about half a point of extra inflation to the war {#oil-shipping-and-prices--cbo-inflation-attribution}

CBO estimates headline PCE inflation in early 2027 will be 0.5 percentage points higher than it projected before the war, and core inflation 0.3 points higher. [5](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-5)

BLS does not attribute the rise, and the Federal Reserve's September statement cites only "geopolitical developments". [4](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-4) [6](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-6)

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-hormuz-transits}

### Tracked traffic through Hormuz fell to almost nothing within days {#oil-shipping-and-prices--hormuz-traffic-near-zero}

PortWatch counted 57 ships on the day of the first strikes and none four days later. [25](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-25)

Monthly averages have stayed far below any earlier month since PortWatch's records began in 2019 (chart).

The strait normally carries oil equal to about 20% of world consumption. [8](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-8)

### The June reopening was brief {#oil-shipping-and-prices--june-reopening-brief}

Traffic picked up after the June memorandum, peaking at 51 ships on 24 June, then fell back after the blockade resumed in July (chart). [25](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-25) [18](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-18)

### Other sources report much more traffic than the tracking counts {#oil-shipping-and-prices--tracking-versus-other-reports}

CENTCOM says more than 3,000 vessels passed through with US support in six months, and that Gulf partners exported more than 1.5 billion barrels since summer. [12](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-12) Kpler, via the Washington Post, reports Gulf exports approaching prewar daily averages. [13](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-13)

EIA warns that ship-tracking signals in the strait have been "especially unreliable" since February. [26](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-26) Recorded transits are therefore a floor, and the true level is unsettled.

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-hormuz-oil-flows}

### Oil through the strait fell by more than three-quarters by the second quarter {#oil-shipping-and-prices--hormuz-flows-collapsed}

EIA puts oil flows in the second quarter of 2026 at under a quarter of their 2025 level, and LNG flows near zero (chart). [26](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-26)

EIA estimates production shut in across the region peaked at 10.9 million barrels a day in May and fell to 4.8 million in September. [7](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-7)

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-chokepoints-september-2026}

### Hormuz is the outlier among the world's chokepoints {#oil-shipping-and-prices--hormuz-alone-collapsed}

In September, most of the 28 chokepoints PortWatch tracks were close to their 2025 traffic (chart). [25](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-25) Only the Kerch Strait, near zero, ran lower than Hormuz.

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-iran-oil-exports-2026}

### Iran's exports collapsed after the first blockade began {#oil-shipping-and-prices--iranian-exports-collapsed}

Vortexa data put Iran's exports in May at about 209,000 barrels a day, the lowest since early 2020 (chart). [14](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-14) The first blockade had begun in mid-April. [14](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-14)

ISW/CTP judges the spring blockade had "a limited but notable effect" on Iran's willingness to keep fighting. [17](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-17)

## Observations {#oil-shipping-and-prices--observations-blockade-tallies}

### CENTCOM reports enforcing the blockade by force {#oil-shipping-and-prices--blockade-enforced}

CENTCOM reports turning around 135 ships and disabling four by 10 October, and says enforcement will continue against "any vessels trying to run the blockade". [11](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-11) [10](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-10) These figures have no independent check.

### US forces are also destroying ships {#oil-shipping-and-prices--us-also-destroying-vessels}

CENTCOM's early-October releases say US forces destroyed 13 commercial vessels in one, and 10 IRGC-linked tankers in the other. [10](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-10) [11](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-11)

The two releases use different categories, and CENTCOM does not explain the difference. The 1 September War Powers letter reported strikes on Iranian state oil tankers. [19](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-19)

### Attacks on shipping continued in October {#oil-shipping-and-prices--shipping-attacks-october}

Officials told Bloomberg of 23 recorded attacks on vessels in or near Hormuz in the first two weeks of October, at least five hitting engine rooms. [13](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-13) The report, relayed by ISW/CTP, does not say who carried out each attack.

## The 1980s Tanker War {#oil-shipping-and-prices--tanker-war}

### The last Gulf shipping war disrupted far less traffic {#oil-shipping-and-prices--tanker-war-disrupted-little}

The Strauss Center says the 1980s Tanker War "failed to disrupt more than two percent of ships" in the Gulf even at its height, and real oil prices fell through the decade. [15](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-15)

Tracked Hormuz traffic in 2026 has fallen much further than that (Hormuz chart). [25](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-25)

## About this data {#oil-shipping-and-prices--about}

**What this measures.** Crude oil spot prices (Brent, the world benchmark, and WTI, the US one), the real price US refiners paid for imported crude, US retail petrol, consumer prices, and ships and oil passing through the Strait of Hormuz, as of late October 2026.

**Frequency.** The price charts use EIA's monthly averages over each full published range, because the daily and weekly series have thousands of points. Daily and weekly values are quoted in the observations.

**How confident to be.** Prices are measured, not estimated. Hormuz counts and flows rest on ship tracking that EIA calls unreliable since February, and CENTCOM's tallies are claims by one side in the war.

**Causes.** A price rising after an event does not show the event caused all of it. CBO is the only source here that attributes inflation to the war. [5](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-5)

**Commonly mistaken for.** A record in money terms is not a record in real terms: adjusted for inflation, 2026's crude prices were below several earlier peaks. [23](#source-oil-shipping-and-prices-23) For the war's cost to the US government, see [the war's human and financial costs](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war).
