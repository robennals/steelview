## Observations {#iran-actions-abroad--observations-confirmed-attacks}

### Iran-aligned militias attacked US forces more than 200 times before this war {#iran-actions-abroad--attacks-on-us-forces-before-the-war}

The Washington Institute's tracker confirmed 223 attacks on US and coalition bases in Iraq and Syria between October 2023 and December 2024 (chart). [17](#source-iran-actions-abroad-17)

The State Department counted "more than 100" by the end of 2023 alone. [9](#source-iran-actions-abroad-9) These attacks predate the February 2026 war.

### The attacks came in a burst and then nearly stopped {#iran-actions-abroad--attacks-came-in-a-burst}

About four in five of the confirmed attacks fell in the first four months, to January 2024 (chart). [17](#source-iran-actions-abroad-17) From February to mid-July 2024 the tracker confirmed only a handful, during what it calls a "temporary cessation". Attacks resumed at a lower rate from August.

### Before October 2023 there had been a long pause {#iran-actions-abroad--pause-before-october-2023}

The State Department says the October 2023 attacks "came after an 18-month general pause in attacks in Iraq and a six-month pause in attacks in Syria" (marked on the chart). [9](#source-iran-actions-abroad-9)

The pauses show the militias' attacks start and stop. The sources do not say whether Iran ordered the pauses or the restarts.

### The State Department found no sign Iran knew of 7 October in advance {#iran-actions-abroad--no-foreknowledge-of-october-7}

It says "there is no evidence that Iran knew about Hamas's October 7 attacks on Israel in advance", while its support "enabled Hamas to execute the attack". [9](#source-iran-actions-abroad-9)

## Observations {#iran-actions-abroad--observations-yemen-panel-matched-materiel}

### Most Houthi weapons the UN inspected resemble Iranian materiel {#iran-actions-abroad--houthi-materiel-similar-to-iranian}

The UN Panel found strong similarities to Iranian materiel in more inspected items than to any other source (chart). It says the Houthis receive "technical assistance, training, weapons and financial support" from Iran, Iraqi armed groups and Hezbollah. [10](#source-iran-actions-abroad-10)

### The Panel reports resemblance, not proven supply {#iran-actions-abroad--similar-not-proven-supplied}

The finding is that items are "similar" to those Iran develops or operates, not that each was shipped from Iran. [10](#source-iran-actions-abroad-10) Similar items are also operated by armed groups in Iraq and Lebanon.

## Observations {#iran-actions-abroad--observations-court-record}

### Argentina's highest criminal court held Iran-backed Hezbollah responsible for two bombings {#iran-actions-abroad--argentine-court-buenos-aires-bombings}

In April 2024 the Cassation Court ruled that both Buenos Aires bombings were carried out by Hezbollah "sponsored by the government of Iran", and blamed then-president Rafsanjani and other officials. [1](#source-iran-actions-abroad-1) Iran denies involvement; no one has been tried in person.

### US courts have sentenced men hired to kill a critic of the regime on US soil {#iran-actions-abroad--us-convictions-iran-directed-plots}

Three men have been sentenced for plots against Masih Alinejad that DOJ says were directed by the Iranian government and the IRGC (table). [4](#source-iran-actions-abroad-4) [5](#source-iran-actions-abroad-5) In Belgium an Iranian diplomat was convicted of plotting to bomb an opposition rally. [3](#source-iran-actions-abroad-3)

### The US cases here are plots, and the Trump case is only a charge {#iran-actions-abroad--us-plots-were-foiled}

Every US case in the table is a plot or conspiracy; none records a completed killing. [4](#source-iran-actions-abroad-4) [5](#source-iran-actions-abroad-5) [6](#source-iran-actions-abroad-6) [7](#source-iran-actions-abroad-7)

The alleged tasking against Donald Trump appears only in a 2024 criminal complaint against a man who remains in Iran. [6](#source-iran-actions-abroad-6)

### A man charged over the AMIA bombing was later Iran's interior minister {#iran-actions-abroad--amia-indictee-held-office}

Ahmad Vahidi, former Quds Force chief, is among the officials an Argentine judge charged in September 2026. [2](#source-iran-actions-abroad-2) He was serving as interior minister in March 2024, when he announced that year's election turnout. [16](#source-iran-actions-abroad-16)

## Observations {#iran-actions-abroad--observations-state-sponsors}

### Iran has been on the US terrorism-sponsor list since 1984 {#iran-actions-abroad--state-sponsor-since-1984}

Iran is the longest-listed state on the list after Syria (table). [8](#source-iran-actions-abroad-8) Whether Syria has since been removed could not be checked, because state.gov refused automated requests.

### The State Department calls Iran the leading state sponsor {#iran-actions-abroad--leading-state-sponsor}

Its 2023 report calls Iran "the leading state sponsor of terrorism". It says Iran gave Hezbollah "hundreds of millions of dollars" and "thousands of rockets, missiles, and small arms" since 2006. [9](#source-iran-actions-abroad-9) This is the US government's own assessment, with no published accounting behind the dollar figure.

## Observations {#iran-actions-abroad--observations-plot-counts}

### Most recent plots in Europe targeted Iranian dissidents {#iran-actions-abroad--plots-mostly-target-opponents}

Of the plots in Europe from 2021 to 2024 in Levitt's dataset, most targeted Iranian dissidents, including Farsi-language journalists (table). [12](#source-iran-actions-abroad-12) Across the whole 1979–2021 dataset, dissidents were the largest single target group, ahead of Jews or Israelis. [11](#source-iran-actions-abroad-11)

### The pace of plotting has varied, with a lull after 9/11 {#iran-actions-abroad--plot-pace-varies}

Levitt finds a gap in Iranian plotting of "about 23 months" after the September 2001 attacks, and very few plots in Europe in the following decade. [11](#source-iran-actions-abroad-11)

Plotting then rose: more than half of all his cases fall in 2011–2021 (table). [11](#source-iran-actions-abroad-11)

### These counts come from one author, and they grow with the dataset {#iran-actions-abroad--plot-counts-one-author}

The dataset more than doubled between the 2022 and 2024 publications (table). The 2022 study covered assassination, surveillance and abduction plots; the 2024 map adds intimidation. [11](#source-iran-actions-abroad-11) [12](#source-iran-actions-abroad-12)

Levitt works at a think tank whose analysts generally favour a hard line on Iran. Neither publication says how many plots were foiled and how many carried out.

### MI5 reports a rising Iranian threat in the UK, by its own unaudited count {#iran-actions-abroad--mi5-uk-plots-and-arrests}

MI5 says it tracked more than twenty potentially lethal Iran-backed plots in the year to October 2025, and that Iran-linked operations led to more than fifty UK arrests in the following twelve months (table). [14](#source-iran-actions-abroad-14) [13](#source-iran-actions-abroad-13) Neither figure comes with cases that can be checked.

### MI5 says the IRGC was behind the spring 2026 arson attacks {#iran-actions-abroad--irgc-behind-arson-front}

MI5 says a wave of arson against Jewish communities in the UK and Europe was claimed by a front group, and "behind this front sat members of the IRGC". [13](#source-iran-actions-abroad-13) It also reports "Iranian ambitions to come at harder targets, like military sites". [13](#source-iran-actions-abroad-13)

### The targets MI5 names are mostly opponents, media and Jewish sites {#iran-actions-abroad--mi5-targets-opponents}

MI5 describes Iran using "criminal proxies to try to intimidate oppositionists, Iranian media organisations, and Israeli sites". [13](#source-iran-actions-abroad-13) In 2025 fourteen countries jointly condemned "Iran's efforts to kill, kidnap and harass in Europe and North America". [14](#source-iran-actions-abroad-14)

## About this data {#iran-actions-abroad--about}

**What this measures.** Court outcomes, US designations, one tracker of attacks on US forces, one UN weapons inspection and counts of plots from one researcher and from MI5. Each has its own counter, named beside every figure.

**What it does not measure.** No source counts plots that were foiled against those carried out. Houthi attacks on shipping are not shown because ACLED's counts sit behind a login. The Global Terrorism Database, which could give a longer record, needs registration and was not used.

**How confident to be.** Court findings and convictions are the firmest evidence here. The State Department reports and MI5 statements are official assessments without published evidence; the Washington Institute and Levitt are independent but hawk-leaning. The militia tracker counts only attacks it could confirm, so it is a floor.

**Commonly mistaken for.** The militia attacks are often cited as one continuous campaign; the chart shows a four-month burst and a long lull. A charge such as the Shakeri complaint is often reported as if proven.

**Dates.** As of late October 2026. The attack tracker stops on 3 December 2024; Levitt's counts run to October 2024; MI5's latest statement is 13 October 2026; court outcomes are dated in the table.
