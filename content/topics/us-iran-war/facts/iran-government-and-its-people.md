## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-presidential-turnout}

### The two lowest presidential turnouts are the last two elections {#iran-government-and-its-people--turnout-record-lows}

Turnout fell to 48.8 percent in 2021 and to 40 percent in the 2024 first round, each a record low since 1979 (chart). [21](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-21)

Parliamentary turnout fell the same way, to the lowest since the revolution in 2024 (chart below). [24](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-24) Earlier swings were large: turnout was highest in 1997 and 2009 (chart). [33](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-33)

### In 2024 voters elected the one moderate in the field {#iran-government-and-its-people--reformist-backed-president-2024}

After Raisi's death, Masoud Pezeshkian, "the sole moderate in an original field of four", won the runoff with 53.7 percent of the vote. [22](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-22)

Turnout rose to 49.8 percent in the runoff, about ten points above the first round. [22](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-22) [21](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-21) He was one of only six candidates the Guardian Council approved (next chart). [23](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-23)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-candidates-share}

### The Guardian Council allows very few registrants to run {#iran-government-and-its-people--vetting-approves-few}

From 2001 to 2021, about one in a hundred or fewer of those who registered for president were allowed to run (chart). [34](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-34) [20](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-20) In 2021 none of the 40 women who registered was approved. [20](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-20)

In 2024 the share was higher, six of "more than 80", but well-known figures such as Ali Larijani and Mahmoud Ahmadinejad were barred. [23](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-23)

### Parliament is vetted too, and a reformist parliament was purged in 2004 {#iran-government-and-its-people--majlis-vetting}

In January 2004 the Guardian Council rejected 3,600 parliamentary candidates, including dozens of sitting members (timeline below). About a thousand were reinstated after a third of the Majlis resigned. [18](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-18)

In 2024 it barred a smaller share of sitting members who sought re-election. [19](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-19)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-ihrngo-executions}

### Executions in 2025 were the most since 1989 {#iran-government-and-its-people--executions-most-since-1989}

IHRNGO recorded at least 1,639 executions in 2025, the highest number since 1989 (chart). [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35) Its count has risen every year since 2020.

Amnesty's separate count for 2025 is higher still (chart below). [25](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-25)

### Executions rose fastest under Pezeshkian {#iran-government-and-its-people--executions-rose-under-pezeshkian}

The monthly rate under Pezeshkian is more than double the rate under Raisi, by IHRNGO's count (chart below). [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

IHRNGO describes "a consistent upward trend ... regardless of the president in power". [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

### A fall after 2015 followed a drug-law change that has since been undone {#iran-government-and-its-people--drug-law-fall-reversed}

Executions fell sharply from 2016 to 2020 (chart). IHRNGO links the decline to the 2017 drug-law amendment, which narrowed the death penalty for drug offences. [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

It says the amendment "has been reversed in practice" since 2021: drug executions averaged 26 a year in 2018–2020 and reached at least 795 in 2025. [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

### Executions surged after the June 2025 war {#iran-government-and-its-people--executions-rose-after-june-2025-war}

IHRNGO says "a new surge in executions began in the months after the 12-day war" and that 60% of 2025's executions came in the second half of the year. [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

It finds executions "tend to increase when authorities fear social unrest and when the attention of the international community is focused on other geopolitical issues". [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35) The UN mission called October 2025's total "the highest monthly total recorded in at least 25 years". [4](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-4)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-hrana-annual}

### A third counter shows the same rise {#iran-government-and-its-people--hrana-annual-rise}

HRANA counts from October to October. Its latest year, to October 2026, was its highest in ten reporting years (chart). [36](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-36)

Like IHRNGO, it shows a low around 2018–19 and a rise every year since.

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-hrana-monthly-total}

### Total executions fell sharply in the first months of the war {#iran-government-and-its-people--executions-fell-in-first-war-months}

HRANA recorded hundreds of executions a month from November 2025 to February 2026, then its lowest full month in March 2026, when the strikes began (chart). [37](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-37)

The monthly total recovered after the ceasefire but stayed well below its pre-war level through September. HRANA does not say why it fell.

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-hrana-monthly-political}

### Political executions peaked during and just after the war {#iran-government-and-its-people--political-executions-peaked-april-may}

Executions on political and security charges ran the other way: HRANA's peak months were April and May 2026, with 29 between them (chart). [37](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-37)

### Half the political executions were tied to the January protests {#iran-government-and-its-people--january-protest-executions}

Of the political and security executions HRANA recorded in its latest year, 35 were "in connection with the January 2026 (Dey 1404) protests". [37](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-37)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-gdp-growth}

### The economy grew in the deal's first year and shrank after the US left {#iran-government-and-its-people--growth-in-deal-years}

Growth was strongest in 2016, the year the nuclear deal took effect, and turned negative in 2018 and 2019 after the US withdrew (chart). [38](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-38) Inflation was at its lowest in decades in 2016 and 2017 and then climbed (next chart). [39](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-39)

The timing fits sanctions relief and their return, but these series do not separate sanctions from oil prices or other causes.

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-majlis-turnout}

### Parliamentary turnout also hit a record low in 2024 {#iran-government-and-its-people--majlis-turnout-low}

The March 2024 parliamentary election drew 41 percent, "the lowest participation since Iran's 1979 Islamic Revolution", just below 2020's pandemic-year 42 percent. [24](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-24)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-january-2026-deaths}

### HRANA confirmed 7,007 deaths in the January 2026 crackdown, most of them protesters {#iran-government-and-its-people--january-2026-crackdown-toll}

HRANA's 7,007 counts every confirmed death in its 50-day record, protesters and others (chart). [1](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-1)

It is a floor: 11,744 more cases were still under review when it was published. [1](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-1)

The government counted 3,117 dead by late January, including people it called "rioters". [4](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-4)

The UN mission found the crackdown amounted to "crimes against humanity of murder, imprisonment, torture". [4](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-4)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-amnesty-executions-2025}

### Iran carried out most of the world's recorded executions in 2025 {#iran-government-and-its-people--iran-top-recorded-executioner}

Amnesty recorded at least 2,159 executions in Iran, 80% of all it recorded worldwide (chart). [25](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-25) China is not included: Amnesty believes it executed thousands but cannot count them.

### Saudi Arabia, a US partner, was second {#iran-government-and-its-people--saudi-second-executioner}

Saudi Arabia recorded the next-highest total, and per resident it executed at about two-fifths of Iran's rate (both charts). [25](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-25) [27](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-27) Together the two account for 93% of recorded executions. [25](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-25)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-iran-execution-counters}

### Counters agree on the direction but not the 2025 level {#iran-government-and-its-people--counters-differ}

IHRNGO and Amnesty were close for 2023 and 2024, but about 500 apart for 2025 (chart). [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35) [25](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-25) Both count only cases they can confirm, so each is a minimum; they are never added together or merged here.

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-executions-per-president}

### The execution rate rose under every recent president {#iran-government-and-its-people--execution-rate-by-president}

By IHRNGO's count, the average monthly rate rose from Ahmadinejad to Rouhani to Raisi, then more than doubled under Pezeshkian (chart). [35](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-35)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-freedom-house-2026}

### Iran is among the least free countries, alongside Saudi Arabia {#iran-government-and-its-people--freedom-house-iran-and-saudi}

Freedom House scores Iran one point above Saudi Arabia, out of 100 (chart). [7](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-7) [8](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-8)

Both are rated "Not Free" and are among the 20 lowest-scoring countries it rates. [9](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-9)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-gamaan-preferred-system}

### Few respondents prefer the Islamic Republic {#iran-government-and-its-people--few-prefer-islamic-republic}

In GAMAAN's June 2026 online sample, about 13% chose the Islamic Republic, down about 7 points on the previous year (chart). [12](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-12) Monarchy and republic each drew more, and about a quarter had no clear alternative.

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-gamaan-war-views}

### Respondents split on whether the strikes were aggression {#iran-government-and-its-people--strikes-seen-as-aggression}

42% in GAMAAN's sample called the US–Israeli action "military aggression against Iran", while about 40% called it "humanitarian intervention" (chart). [11](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-11) Most opposed Iran building nuclear weapons.

### Most were glad to see the regime weakened {#iran-government-and-its-people--satisfaction-at-weakening}

About 60% "felt satisfaction at the weakening of the Islamic Republic during the war", and only about 14% approved of its response to the January protests. [11](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-11)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-crackdown-deaths}

### Each of the last three protest waves was put down with lethal force {#iran-government-and-its-people--crackdowns-2019-2022-2026}

Amnesty documented 304 deaths in November 2019, most of them in two days. [6](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-6)

The UN mission counted at least 551 protesters killed in 2022–23. [7](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-7) The January 2026 toll is many times either (table).

### There is no verified count for 2009 {#iran-government-and-its-people--no-verified-2009-count}

No primary source for deaths in the 2009 post-election crackdown was found, so it is left blank rather than filled from secondary reports. See Data still needed.

### Counts grew as cases were confirmed {#iran-government-and-its-people--counts-are-floors}

HRANA's confirmed total rose by more than half in a month while thousands of cases stayed under review (table). [2](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-2) [1](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-1)

Its published list names 4,555 people, against 3,038 on the government's list. [5](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-5)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-arrests}

### Repression widened after the June 2025 war {#iran-government-and-its-people--arrests-after-june-2025}

In the three months after the June 2025 war, Amnesty and Human Rights Watch counted over 20,000 arrests, including "dissidents, human rights defenders, journalists" and foreign nationals; the police's own figure was about 21,000. [10](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-10) At least nine men were executed on political or espionage charges. [10](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-10)

### The January 2026 crackdown brought more than 50,000 arrests {#iran-government-and-its-people--arrests-january-2026}

HRANA counted 53,777 arrests during the crackdown, including hundreds of children and school students. [1](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-1)

## Observations {#iran-government-and-its-people--observations-openings-and-reversals}

### The harsher hijab law was shelved, with the Leader's assent {#iran-government-and-its-people--hijab-law-shelved}

In December 2024 the Supreme National Security Council put the new hijab law "on hold"; its decisions "require the Supreme Leader's signature". [13](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-13) In May 2025 the Speaker said he had been told in writing not to enact it. [14](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-14)

What was halted is the new law's enactment. The existing hijab rules stayed, and Freedom House records arrests in December 2025 over unveiled women at a marathon. [7](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-7)

### Earlier openings were reversed while reformists held the presidency {#iran-government-and-its-people--earlier-openings-reversed}

Under Khatami, hardliners closed sixteen reformist publications in a week in 2000, and in 2004 the Guardian Council purged reformist candidates for parliament (timeline). [17](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-17) [18](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-18) The November 2019 killings came under Rouhani. [6](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-6)

### The new Leader is the old Leader's son {#iran-government-and-its-people--hereditary-succession}

On 8 March 2026 the Assembly of Experts named Mojtaba Khamenei "the third leader", by "the decisive vote of its members", with no tally published. [15](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-15) [16](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-16) Iran International reports he was chosen "under pressure from the Revolutionary Guards"; that is a single outlet's claim. [15](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-15)

## Subtleties {#iran-government-and-its-people--subtleties}

### Moderation and repression ran side by side {#iran-government-and-its-people--moderation-and-repression-together}

The 2024 election and the shelved hijab law are the clearest signs of moderation in these sources. In the same period executions rose fastest and the January 2026 crackdown was the deadliest here. A reader who takes only one set of facts gets a one-sided picture.

### The war's first months cut both ways {#iran-government-and-its-people--war-cuts-both-ways}

Total executions dropped when the bombing began, while political executions and arrests rose. "The war weakened repression" and "the war hardened it" each use one half of the same HRANA data.

## About this data {#iran-government-and-its-people--about}

**What this measures.** Turnout, candidate vetting, executions, protest deaths, arrests, expert freedom scores and one online survey. Every count of killing or execution is a confirmed minimum by a named counter: IHRNGO, HRANA and Amnesty each use their own method and period, so each has its own chart.

**What it does not measure.** No source measures how hijab rules are enforced on the street, or how united the regime's security forces are. Executions and deaths in the war itself are in [the war's human and financial costs](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war).

**How confident to be.** Iran does not let independent monitors work, and most executions are unannounced: IHRNGO says under 7% were officially announced in 2025. True numbers are likely higher than every count here.

**The survey.** GAMAAN reached its sample online, almost all through the Psiphon circumvention tool, and weighted it to literate residents aged 15 and over. Its effective sample size after weighting is about 6,211. [12](#source-iran-government-and-its-people-12) People who use such tools may differ from those who do not; no offline survey was found to check this.

**How it compares.** Amnesty's execution counts and Freedom House's scores cover every country they rate, and both charts show the full set. Turnout and vetting have no comparable cross-country measure here.

**Dates.** As of late October 2026. Execution counts run to 8 October 2026 (HRANA) and December 2025 (IHRNGO, Amnesty); crackdown counts stop on 23 February 2026; the survey was fielded 20–27 June 2026.
