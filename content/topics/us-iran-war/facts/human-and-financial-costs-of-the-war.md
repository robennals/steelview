## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-us-wounded}

### Wounded rose with each round of fighting {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--wounded-track-fighting}

Wounded came in two surges, in March and in July, when US strikes resumed (chart). [23](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-23) More than half of all the wounded were recorded after the July split into a second category. [8](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-8)

Few were wounded in the quieter months of May, June and August, and a smaller rise followed in September. [23](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-23)

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-us-deaths}

### Half of the US deaths were not from hostile action {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--half-of-deaths-non-hostile}

Of the US deaths DCAS records by late October, half were non-hostile, mostly Air Force accidents in March (chart). [23](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-23)

Reports in early October gave a total of 21; DCAS has since added one more non-hostile death, in October. [8](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-8) [23](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-23)

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-iranian-deaths-by-counter}

### At least 1,701 Iranian civilians were killed by early April, by HRANA's minimum count {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--civilian-minimum-by-april}

HRANA counted 1,701 civilians killed by the April ceasefire, including at least 254 children. [1](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-1) It counts incident by incident, takes minimums, and warns of undercounting.

### By Hengaw's count, most of those killed were military {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--most-deaths-military-by-hengaw}

Hengaw counts 6,620 military personnel killed, mostly Revolutionary Guards and army. Its civilians are 13.5% of its total. [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2)

Its method is not published, and it also alleges that military units moved into civilian buildings. [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2)

### The counters disagree on the split more than on the scale {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--counters-disagree}

All three counts put deaths in the thousands by spring. HRANA's civilians are slightly under half of its total; Hengaw's are about one in seven (chart). [1](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-1) [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2)

The government's count has no split at all, so it cannot settle the question. [3](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-3)

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-displacement-and-population}

### WHO estimates 3.2 million people displaced inside Iran {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--displaced-millions}

That is about one in every 29 people in Iran (chart). [3](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-3) [4](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-4) WHO calls the figure an estimate and does not say how many have returned home.

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-cost-estimates}

### Official estimates have risen from $25 billion to over $40 billion {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--cost-38-to-47-billion}

CBO put the cost at about $38 billion by August, and the Pentagon at $43.6 billion by early September (chart). [11](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-11) [9](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-9)

AP reports a later Pentagon figure of $47.1 billion, without its as-of date. [22](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-22) Most of the Pentagon's September figure is replacing munitions and equipment, not operating costs. [9](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-9)

### More fighting would add $2–3 billion a month, by CBO's estimate {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--monthly-cost-going-forward}

CBO estimates each further month at $2 billion at the low intensity of May and June, and $3 billion at July's level. [11](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-11)

CBO notes that DoD did not answer its requests for information, so it worked from public reports. [11](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-11)

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-us-deaths-earlier-wars}

### US deaths are a small fraction of the Iraq war's {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--us-deaths-small-share-of-iraq}

The Iran war's US deaths are about one two-hundredth of Operation Iraqi Freedom's (chart). [23](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-23) [16](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-16)

CBO attributes the war's lower cost to "relatively few U.S. forces" being involved compared with Iraq and Afghanistan. [11](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-11) The comparison is of totals: Iraq ran for years, this war for eight months.

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-war-costs-for-scale}

### So far the war has cost a small fraction of Iraq's appropriations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--cost-small-against-iraq}

The Pentagon's latest figure is about a seventeenth of the $815 billion appropriated for Iraq through FY2014 (chart). [22](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-22) [17](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-17) Iraq's appropriations built up over 13 years.

## Observations {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--observations-iraq-estimates-vs-appropriations}

### Iraq cost far more than the White House forecast {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--iraq-overran-estimates}

In 2002 the White House budget director put the war at $50–60 billion. [18](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-18)

Congress appropriated $815 billion for Iraq through FY2014, more than thirteen times the top of that range. [17](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-17)

Even the high estimate the budget director called "very, very high" was a quarter of the outcome (chart). [18](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-18)

| Counter | Covers | Deaths | Civilians | Method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HRANA | 28 Feb – 7 Apr | civilian, military and unclassified listed separately | 1,701 [1](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-1) | Incident by incident, minimum values; excludes state-media totals |
| Hengaw | 28 Feb – 8 Apr | 7,650 [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2) | 1,030 [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2) | Not published |

### No count covers the fighting after April except the government's {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--no-count-after-april}

HRANA's and Hengaw's counts end at the April ceasefire. [1](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-1) [2](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-2) After that, only the authorities' totals and Airwars' incident tallies exist. [3](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-3) [5](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-5)

Airwars tracked 267 civilian-harm incidents before the ceasefire and 34 more after strikes resumed in June. [5](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-5) WHO notes the authorities have yet to publish cumulative figures. [3](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-3)

### Other reports reuse HRANA's figures {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--airwars-uses-hrana}

Airwars' one-month report of at least 1,443 civilians killed is HRANA's count, not a separate one. [6](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-6)

## About this data {#human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war--about}

**What this measures.** Deaths, injuries and displacement in Iran, US military casualties, and estimates of the war's cost to the Defense Department, as of late October 2026. Each figure carries the date it runs to: Iranian independent counts stop in April, costs run to September or later, US casualties to 28 October.

**What it does not measure.** Deaths in Israel, Iraq, Lebanon and the Gulf states, costs to the wider US economy (see [oil, shipping and prices](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices)), and Iran's own costs.

**US casualty data.** DCAS publishes its tables through a JSON interface behind its web app; the series sums its two categories. The July split means a month's casualties appear in one category or the other, so neither category alone is a complete record.

**How confident to be.** The independent Iranian counts are minimums from organisations working without access to the country. The government count has no split and is relayed by WHO without checking. Cost estimates differ in what they include; none is an audited figure.

**Commonly mistaken for.** The $1.6 trillion figure for "the Iraq war" is all post-9/11 operations; Iraq's share was $815 billion. [17](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-17) The Brookings estimate of $150–300 billion is often called prewar, but was made in August 2003. [18](#source-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war-18)
