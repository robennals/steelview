name: Iran could not be allowed a nuclear weapon, and its record showed what it would do under one
summary: "Iran had brought itself within reach of a bomb, and [no inspector has seen its 60% uranium since June 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for). Its [record of attacks and plots abroad](#fact-iran-actions-abroad/argentine-court-buenos-aires-bombings) shows what it would do once shielded by a weapon. Stopping that was worth a war."
order: 1
---
[By US intelligence estimates, Iran had brought itself within a week of enough weapons-grade uranium for a bomb](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/close-to-weapons-grade-before-strikes), and [its government has spent decades sponsoring attacks on people it considers enemies](#fact-iran-actions-abroad/argentine-court-buenos-aires-bombings). A regime with that record could not be allowed the one weapon that would put it beyond punishment. Ending the danger was worth a war, and [safety from catastrophic threats](#principle-safety-from-catastrophic-threats) is the reason.

Start with what Iran was doing. [It is the only state without nuclear weapons producing uranium enriched to 60%](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/only-non-weapon-state-at-60). [Its 60% stock more than doubled in the seven months before the June 2025 strikes](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/sixty-percent-growth).

Before those strikes, [US intelligence judged it was probably less than a week from enough weapons-grade uranium for a bomb, with enough material for more than a dozen](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/close-to-weapons-grade-before-strikes).

Iran did not earn the benefit of the doubt. [The IAEA found an undeclared weapons-relevant programme that ran to the early 2000s, hidden uranium, and sites Iran sanitised](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/past-weapons-work).

[Inspectors' access narrowed step by step, long before any war](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/access-narrowed-in-steps), and [the IAEA's Board sent the case to the UN Security Council](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/board-referral-with-abstentions). Today [the last estimate of the 60% stock was mostly verified, but no inspector has seen it since](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for).

It is true that [in 2025 US intelligence judged Iran was not building a weapon](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/ata-2025-not-building), and that [the IAEA found no ongoing structured programme that May](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-ongoing-programme-2025).

But a state that had brought its material within a week of weapons grade did not need a programme in place; it needed only a decision. Those judgments described intent at one moment. The capability was what needed to end.

What Iran would do behind a bomb is no mystery. [Argentina's highest criminal court held Iran-backed Hezbollah responsible for the 1992 and 1994 Buenos Aires bombings](#fact-iran-actions-abroad/argentine-court-buenos-aires-bombings). [US courts have sentenced men hired through the Revolutionary Guards to kill a critic of the regime on American soil](#fact-iran-actions-abroad/us-convictions-iran-directed-plots).

[Iran-aligned militias attacked US forces more than 200 times between October 2023 and December 2024](#fact-iran-actions-abroad/attacks-on-us-forces-before-the-war). Each of those acts was done by a state that could still be punished. A bomb would change that.

Pressure without force had not stopped it. In one study, [sanctions stopped only three of 22 programmes already under way, though their threat deterred others from starting](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/nonproliferation-sanctions-three-of-22). Fears that one bomb sets off many have usually proved wrong, since [past predictions of rapid spread did not come true](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/predicted-cascades-did-not-happen).

Even so, [Saudi Arabia's crown prince said his country would follow an Iranian bomb](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/saudi-2018-pledge). The choice was never between war and a peaceful Iran. It was between acting while the material was still in reach of bombs and inspectors, and facing an Iran that no one could deter.
