Under the Constitution, a President cannot take the country to war alone. [Congress has never voted to approve this war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/votes-all-to-end-the-war), so it is against the law. It should stop unless Congress votes for it.

### Only Congress can declare war, and it has not approved this one

[The Constitution gives Congress, not the President, the power to declare war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/congress-declares-war), [and the administration did not seek its approval before the 60-day mark](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/no-authorisation-sought). [Every vote Congress has held on the war that the record shows was on ending it, and none was on approving it](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/votes-all-to-end-the-war). The rule exists so that [the people, through their representatives, decide](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) whether Americans are sent to kill and die.

### Congress has voted to end the war

[The House voted three times to end the war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/house-passed-three-resolutions). [The Senate passed one of those votes](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/senate-adopted-one-resolution). Voters wanted a say too: [most said the President should have asked Congress first](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/most-wanted-congress-asked).

### The President reported new strikes as new actions

[The White House told Congress the fighting had ended, then new strikes followed](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/terminated-letter-then-new-strikes). [The first blockade was still turning ships back weeks later](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/first-blockade-into-may). Saying the fighting had ended let it claim to meet [the law's 60-day requirement to withdraw forces](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/sixty-day-requirement). [It then reported each new strike as a new action](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/separate-actions).

### Presidents have almost never started the 60-day clock

[From 1975 to 2017, presidents almost never formally started the 60-day clock](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/only-one-report-started-the-clock). While [they have used force abroad hundreds of times without a declaration of war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances), that does not make it lawful. [The main binding attempts since 2011 to end a military action have all failed](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/binding-attempts-failed).

A rule still counts when it is often broken. [Rules should mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say), and years of ignoring this one are a reason to enforce it now.

### Congress should vote to approve this war or end it

While [the votes so far may not bind the President](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/concurrent-resolutions-not-binding), they still matter. [Research shows Congress shapes how far wars go and how long they last](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/congress-as-constraint).

Now Congress should hold a vote that does count. It should either approve the war, with clear goals and limits, or end it. Members who support the war should be willing to vote for it. Until they do, the war has no legal basis.
