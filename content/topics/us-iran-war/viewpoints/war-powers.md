[The Constitution gives Congress, not the President, the power to declare war, and Congress has not authorised this one](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances). Whatever the case against Iran, a war started and continued by one person's decision is unlawful, and it should end until Congress votes for it.

The rule is old and was meant to bind. [Congress has declared war eleven times, covering five wars](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances). It has not declared or authorised this one. [Democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership) means the people's representatives decide whether their sons and daughters are sent to kill and die.

Congress has spoken against the war. [A House majority voted to end it three times, from June to September](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/house-passed-three-resolutions). [The Senate adopted one of those resolutions in June, so both chambers passed it](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/senate-adopted-one-resolution). [Most voters said in March that the President should have asked Congress first](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/most-wanted-congress-asked).

The administration answered with a label. [On 1 May it told Congress that hostilities had terminated, while the blockade ran on, and from June it reported at least five new military actions](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/terminated-letter-then-new-strikes). [Each letter treats the strikes as a new, separate action](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/separate-actions). That is how a 60-day limit is defeated: by restarting the count each time.

The objection is that every president does this. That is true. [Presidents have used force abroad hundreds of times without a declaration](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances), and [only one War Powers report since 1975 has started the 60-day clock](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/only-one-report-started-the-clock).

[Every binding attempt since 2011 to end an operation has failed or been vetoed](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/binding-attempts-failed). But a habit of breaking a rule does not repeal it. [Rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say), and a long record of presidents ignoring the war power is a reason to enforce it now.

The votes did not force the President's hand. [The resolutions were concurrent resolutions, which do not bind him in practice](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/concurrent-resolutions-not-binding). They still matter. [Research finds Congress shapes when wars start, how far they go and how long they last, even without binding votes](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/congress-as-constraint).

Congress should therefore do what the Constitution asks of it. It should pass a joint resolution that either authorises this war, with stated aims and limits, or ends it. Members who believe in the war should be willing to vote for it. Until they do, the war has no lawful basis.
