Under the Constitution, a President cannot take the country to war alone. [Congress has never voted to approve this war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/votes-all-to-end-the-war), so it is against the law. It should stop unless Congress votes for it.

### Only Congress can declare war, and it has not approved this one

[The Constitution gives Congress, not the President, the power to declare war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/congress-declares-war). [Every vote Congress has held on the war, as far as the record shows, was on ending it](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/votes-all-to-end-the-war), and [the administration did not seek Congress's approval before the 60-day mark](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/no-authorisation-sought). [The President says he acted as Commander in Chief, in defence of allies, and filed his reports "consistent with" the War Powers law](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/president-cites-commander-in-chief). But commanding the forces is not choosing war. The rule exists so that the people, through their representatives, decide whether Americans are sent to kill and die.

### When Congress has voted, it has voted to end the war

[After narrowly rejecting three resolutions, the House passed three to end the war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/house-passed-three-resolutions), and [the Senate passed one of those resolutions](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/senate-adopted-one-resolution). So both chambers have voted to end it. Voters wanted a say too: [most said the President should have asked Congress first](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/most-wanted-congress-asked).

### The war has run past the law's 60-day limit, whatever the White House calls it

[Two months in, the White House told Congress the fighting had ended. Then new strikes followed](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/terminated-letter-then-new-strikes). It reported each new strike as a new action, yet [the first blockade was still turning ships back weeks after that letter](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/first-blockade-into-may). [The War Powers law sets a 60-day limit on using force without Congress's approval](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/sixty-day-requirement), and one war does not become many by giving each strike a new start date.

### Presidents have long sidestepped this rule, which is why it must be enforced now

[From 1975 to 2017, presidents almost never formally started the 60-day clock](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/only-one-report-started-the-clock). While [presidents have used force abroad hundreds of times without a declaration of war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances), that does not make it lawful. A rule still counts when it is often broken: rules should mean what they say.

### So Congress should vote either to approve this war or to end it

Congress should hold a vote that counts. It should either approve the war, with clear goals and limits, or end it. Members who support the war should be willing to vote for it. Until they do, the war has no legal basis.

While [the votes so far may not bind the President](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/concurrent-resolutions-not-binding), they still matter. [Research shows Congress shapes how far wars go and how long they last](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/congress-as-constraint).
