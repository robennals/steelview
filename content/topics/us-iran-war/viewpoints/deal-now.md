Whatever one thought of starting this war, it has given the United States leverage: [for the first time in 20 years, ISIS sees no route for Iran to weapons-grade uranium](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-route-to-weapons-grade). The time to turn that into a verified deal is now, before the advantage wears away and before the next round of fighting makes a deal harder.

That gain will not last. [ISIS declined to say how long it holds, because no one outside Iran knows which centrifuges survived](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-route-to-weapons-grade), and every month without inspectors gives Iran time to repair what it can.

Force cannot solve the problem that remains. [The IAEA's last estimate of the 60% stock was mostly verified, but no one has seen it since June 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for). Bombs cannot find uranium that may be buried or moved. Only inspectors on the ground can, and only a deal will let them in.

Deals with Iran have worked before. [While the 2015 deal held, Iran's stock stayed under its limit](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/deal-era-compliance). [In June 2026 Iran signed a pledge not to make nuclear weapons](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/mou-no-weapons-clause). [Libya gave up its programme in 2003 and accepted inspections](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/libya-2003-with-inspections), and [threats combined with face-saving rewards make reversal most likely](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/threats-plus-rewards).

A deal is also on the table. [Talks continued through October, with Qatar and others pressing a plan](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/talks-continue-in-october) in which Iran reopens Hormuz and the US lifts its blockade. That is the right trade to start from, with nuclear limits and inspection added.

Waiting is costly. [Tracked traffic through Hormuz fell to almost nothing within days of the war](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/hormuz-traffic-near-zero), [attacks on shipping continued in October](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/shipping-attacks-october), and [US forces are destroying ships to enforce the blockade](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/us-also-destroying-vessels).

Each incident risks a wider war. [CBO estimates each further month at $2–3 billion](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war/monthly-cost-going-forward). [War only as a last resort](#principle-war-only-as-a-last-resort) means taking a workable settlement when one is offered.

The last agreement failed fast. [The June memorandum broke down within weeks, with Iranian attacks on ships and US strikes in reply](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/mou-broke-down), and [Iran worked to rebuild its missiles and drones throughout the ceasefire, in ISW/CTP's assessment](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/iran-reconstituting-missiles).

[Sanctions stopped few programmes already under way](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/nonproliferation-sanctions-three-of-22). That is why a new deal must rest on inspection, not on promises, and why the blockade should be lifted in stages as inspectors confirm each step.

The US should offer to end the blockade and stop strikes in return for inspectors at every site, an accounting of the uranium, and no enrichment while it is verified. [A government's first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people), and a verified settlement serves them better than an open-ended war.
