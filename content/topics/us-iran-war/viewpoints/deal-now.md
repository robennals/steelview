Whatever you think of the war, it has given the US the upper hand over Iran. The US should use it now to get a nuclear deal that inspectors can check, before that advantage fades.

### The war has set Iran back, but the setback may not last

[For the first time in 20 years, a leading group of nuclear experts sees no way for Iran to make bomb-grade uranium](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-route-to-weapons-grade). But [they cannot say how long that will last](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-route-to-weapons-grade). Every month without inspectors gives Iran time to rebuild.

### Only inspectors can find the missing uranium, and Iran must let them in

[No inspector has seen Iran's 60% uranium since June 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for). It may have been buried or moved, and bombs cannot find it. Inspectors on the ground could, but only if Iran lets them in, and pressure alone will not get them in: [sanctions have rarely stopped a nuclear programme once it has started](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/nonproliferation-sanctions-three-of-22).

### Deals with inspectors have held programmes back before

[Under the 2015 deal, Iran's uranium stock stayed under its limit until about a year after the US left](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/deal-era-compliance). [Libya gave up its nuclear programme and let inspectors in](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/libya-2003-with-inspections). [Countries are most likely to give up a programme when threatened sanctions come with face-saving rewards](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/threats-plus-rewards). And [in June 2026 Iran signed a promise not to make nuclear weapons](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/mou-no-weapons-clause).

### Every month of standoff risks a wider war and costs billions

[Tracked shipping through the Strait of Hormuz fell almost to nothing when the war began, and has stayed far below normal](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/hormuz-traffic-near-zero). [Attacks on ships have continued](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/october-escalation), and [US forces are destroying ships to enforce the blockade](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/us-also-destroying-vessels). Any of these clashes could start a wider war, and [each extra month of fighting costs billions of dollars](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war/monthly-cost-going-forward). While [other sources report far more ships than tracking shows](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/tracking-versus-other-reports), the attacks go on either way.

### A new deal must be checked step by step, not trusted

While [the June memorandum broke down within three weeks](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/mou-broke-down), that calls for checks, not giving up. [Experts say Iran used the ceasefire to rebuild its missiles and drones](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/iran-reconstituting-missiles), so trust is not enough. The US should lift the blockade step by step, as inspectors confirm that Iran has opened every site, shown where its uranium is and stopped enriching.

### So the US should trade its blockade for limits inspectors can check, now

[Qatar is pushing a plan: Iran reopens the Strait of Hormuz and the US ends its blockade](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/qatari-plan). With nuclear limits and inspections added, the US should take that deal while it still has the upper hand. [War should be the last resort](#principle-war-only-as-a-last-resort), and [a government's first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people): a deal that can be checked serves Americans better than endless war.
