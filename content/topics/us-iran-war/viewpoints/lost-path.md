Iran was dangerous, but talks and inspections had kept it in check before. Choosing war instead was a mistake. War should be the last resort, and this one was not.

### The war has killed thousands of Iranians who had no say in it

One group counted at least 1,701 civilians killed by early April, at least 254 of them children, and the outside counts stop in April, so they miss any deaths since. Millions more have been forced from their homes. These people had no say in their government's choices, and the people who suffer most should count the most.

### The 2015 deal kept Iran's uranium in check until the US walked away

Iran's uranium stock stayed under the 2015 deal's limit until about a year after the US left the deal, then rose far above it, so the deal held Iran back for as long as it lasted. Its most dangerous uranium came after the deal was gone: it began building up uranium enriched to 60% in 2021. Even then the talking went on. US–Iran talks were under way in Geneva two days before the strikes, so the war threw away the chance to restore the deal.

### Iran was not about to build a bomb

In 2025, UN nuclear inspectors found no credible sign of an active weapons programme, and US intelligence judged that Iran was not building a bomb. While Iran was the only country without nuclear weapons making 60% uranium, that was a danger to watch closely, not a reason for war. There was still time for inspections and talks.

### The war has blinded the inspectors, and bombs cannot do their job

Since the war began, inspectors have visited only one site in Iran, a power plant, and no inspector has seen Iran's 60% uranium since June 2025. While access had narrowed in steps since 2021, that came after the deal fell apart, and a new deal could reverse it. Experts see no way for Iran to make bomb-grade uranium for now, but give no estimate of how long that will last, and past strikes on nuclear sites slowed programmes only a little.

### Keeping Iran in check never needed Iran to become friendly

Earlier openings in Iran were reversed, and executions rose fastest under the moderate president. But the deal never depended on Iran becoming freer. It depended on inspectors being able to watch. There were even some signs of change: in 2024 voters chose the one moderate candidate, and a harsher headscarf law was put on hold, most likely with the Supreme Leader's consent.

### So the US should stop bombing and go back to inspections

The US should stop the bombing for good. Then it should rebuild what it threw away: inspectors on the ground, and a deal that gives Iran a reason to let them in.

The war also lacks outside support. Oman's foreign minister said the UN never approved it. Britain, France and Germany did not join the strikes.
