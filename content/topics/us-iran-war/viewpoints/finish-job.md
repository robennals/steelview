Whatever one thought of starting this war, stopping halfway is the worst way to end it. It would leave an angry Iran with its best uranium hidden and its missiles being rebuilt. The US should keep up the pressure until inspectors confirm nothing dangerous is left.

### Stopping now would leave Iran's most dangerous uranium out of sight

The job is not done while Iran's best uranium is missing. When the first strikes began in June 2025, Iran held 440.9 kg of uranium enriched to 60%. No inspector has seen it since, and the UN agency cannot say how much is left or where it is. Iran says it is buried under rubble. But Iran has hidden weapons work before, so its word is not enough. Until inspectors find the uranium, or see that it was destroyed, the danger remains.

### Every pause has given Iran time to rearm

A pause has not been an end. Both sides fired during the April ceasefire, and the agreement signed in June broke down within three weeks. Meanwhile, experts at two US research groups judge, Iran worked to rebuild its missiles and drones, and its new defence minister plans to rebuild them inside mountains. In October the top US intelligence official reportedly warned that Iran was in the advanced stages of planning attacks on US targets in the region.

### The blockade is pressure Iran feels

Pressure is working where it bites. Iran's oil exports collapsed after the US blockade began in April, to the lowest level since early 2020. The blockade is the strongest card the US holds. Lifting it before inspectors are back would throw that card away for a promise, and the last agreement lasted three weeks.

### Pressure has ended nuclear programmes when it was kept up and came with a way out

Kept-up pressure has made countries give up nuclear programmes. Iraq's ended only after a war and years of close inspection, and Libya gave up its programme in 2003 and let inspectors in. Countries give up a programme most often when they face sanctions, with force on the table, and are offered face-saving rewards. While bombing alone has forced a country to give in only about a third of the time, that is a reason to pair the pressure with a way out, not to stop it.

### Backing down now could make Iran and its allies bolder

How this war ends will teach Iran and others what the US will do. Researchers disagree on whether backing down invites new challenges, but some find that what a country did in the past is closely connected to the challenges it faces later. With the worst weapons at stake, that risk is too serious to take. America's friends are already under fire: in October the Houthis in Yemen fired missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia.

### So the US should keep up the pressure until inspectors confirm nothing is left

The goals should be limited and clear. Iran stops enriching uranium, shows where its uranium is, and lets inspectors see every site. Overthrowing Iran's government is not the aim: when outsiders impose a new government, the risk of civil war more than doubles, and demanding a change of leader makes bombing less likely to work.

The war should end with inspectors in place, by force or by a deal Iran accepts under pressure. The cost so far is a small part of the Iraq war's. While most Americans disapprove of the war, clear goals would show them how it ends. Stopping short leaves the hardest part to the next president.
