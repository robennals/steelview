The United States is already at war with Iran, and stopping halfway is the worst way out. A half-finished war leaves a bombed, humiliated regime with [uranium no one has seen since June 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for) and every reason to want a bomb more than before. Having started, the US should finish removing Iran's capacity to threaten it and its allies.

The nuclear danger has not gone. [The IAEA's last estimate of Iran's 60% uranium was mostly verified, but no one has seen it since June 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for). [Iran's foreign minister says it is under the rubble](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/stock-unaccounted-for). That may be true, but it is the word of a government that [hid an undeclared weapons-relevant programme and sanitised the sites](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/past-weapons-work).

Iran used the pause to rearm. [In ISW/CTP's assessment it worked to rebuild its missiles and drones from the April ceasefire on, and its new defence minister plans to rebuild in mountains](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/iran-reconstituting-missiles). [The intelligence chief warned on 23 October, as reported, that Iran is in the advanced stages of planning attacks on US targets](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/dni-warning-as-reported).

History shows what ending a programme takes. [Iraq's programme ended after a war and years of intensive inspection](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/iraq-ended-after-war-and-inspection), not after a strike and a pause. [Air-power coercion succeeds about a third of the time, more often against military targets](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/air-coercion-about-a-third), which is where pressure should stay.

Credibility matters too. [Some research finds that a state that backs down faces more challenges later, though other work disagrees](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/credibility-split). [Safety from catastrophic threats](#principle-safety-from-catastrophic-threats) is worth some risk of being wrong about that. And [loyalty to allies](#principle-loyalty-to-allies) counts: [Gulf partners came under fire again in October, as attacks hit shipping and the Houthis fired on Saudi Arabia](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/october-escalation).

Finishing is not toppling. [Imposed regime change more than doubles the risk of civil war](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/imposed-regime-change-and-civil-war), and [demanding a new leader makes bombing less likely to work](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/leadership-demands-lower-success). The aim should be limited and stated: no enrichment, the uranium found or proved destroyed, and missile production stopped.

The record is not one-sided. [The scholars who found strikes delay programmes say the conditions behind past successes are absent for Iran](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/past-success-conditions-absent-for-iran). [Both sides used force during the ceasefire](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/both-sides-fired), and [most Americans disapprove of the war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/majority-disapproves). Those are reasons to set a clear end point. They are not reasons to stop before reaching it.

So the next phase, whether by force or by a deal Iran accepts under pressure, should end with inspectors on the ground and nothing left to find. Stopping short leaves the hardest part to whoever governs next.
