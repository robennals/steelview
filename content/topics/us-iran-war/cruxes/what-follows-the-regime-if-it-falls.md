The research is discouraging for anyone who wants outsiders to bring the regime down. [Imposed regime change more than doubles the risk of civil war](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/imposed-regime-change-and-civil-war), [democracy has followed only where conditions already favoured it](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/democracy-only-in-favourable-conditions), and [most covert US attempts failed](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/covert-attempts-failed). But [the datasets were not built on a case like Iran](#fact-outside-attempts-to-change-hostile-regimes/fit-to-iran).

If Iran is like the cases in the record, helping the regime fall is likely to make Iranians worse off, and the regime-change case must rest on the hope that Iran is different. If Iran's history and society make it an exception, that case gains real strength. The evidence that would settle it does not yet exist; what would move people is how a transition actually unfolded, if one began.
