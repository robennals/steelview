The evidence runs both ways. [Voters elected the one moderate in the field in 2024](#fact-iran-government-and-its-people/reformist-backed-president-2024), and [a harsher hijab law was shelved with the Leader's assent](#fact-iran-government-and-its-people/hijab-law-shelved). But [executions rose fastest under that president](#fact-iran-government-and-its-people/executions-rose-under-pezeshkian), [earlier openings were reversed while reformists held the presidency](#fact-iran-government-and-its-people/earlier-openings-reversed), and [the new Leader is the old Leader's son](#fact-iran-government-and-its-people/hereditary-succession).

If the openings were the start of a real change, the case for containment gains force, because a slower path had something to wait for. If they were cosmetic, the argument that Iran could be left to change from within loses its footing, and the case against the regime itself stands. Better data on how decisions are made inside the regime, and whether the reformist president had power over security and the courts, would settle much of this.
