[Rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say) and [safety from catastrophic threats](#principle-safety-from-catastrophic-threats) collide here. [The Constitution gives Congress the power to declare war](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/declarations-and-instances), yet [presidents have almost never started the 60-day clock themselves](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/only-one-report-started-the-clock), and [every binding attempt since 2011 to end an operation has failed](#fact-decisions-law-and-diplomacy/binding-attempts-failed).

Someone who puts lawful process first should oppose the war until Congress authorises it, whatever they think of Iran. Someone who puts the threat first can accept a war Congress did not vote for. Evidence cannot settle which comes first; it can only show how often the rule has been set aside, and whether Congress acts when it disagrees.
