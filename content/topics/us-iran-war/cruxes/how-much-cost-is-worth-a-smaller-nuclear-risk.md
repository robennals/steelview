---
question: "How much American cost is worth paying to reduce the chance of an Iranian bomb?"
kind: tradeoff
divides: [nuclear, america-first, finish-job]
positions:
  - viewpoint: nuclear
    holds: "An Iranian bomb would be a permanent danger to Americans and their allies. Even a large cost now is small beside that."
  - viewpoint: america-first
    holds: "The cost falls on American families and soldiers, for a risk that intelligence did not judge imminent. The war has to justify itself to the people paying for it, and it has not."
  - viewpoint: finish-job
    holds: "The cost has been modest by the standard of past wars, and stopping halfway would waste what has already been paid."
---
[Safety from catastrophic threats](#principle-safety-from-catastrophic-threats) and [a government's first duty to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people) pull apart here. [Official estimates of the war's cost have risen to $47.1 billion](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war/cost-38-to-47-billion), and [petrol rose by about half](#fact-oil-shipping-and-prices/pump-prices-up-by-half). Yet [US deaths are a small fraction of the Iraq war's](#fact-human-and-financial-costs-of-the-war/us-deaths-small-share-of-iraq), and [Iran had been within a week of weapons-grade uranium, by US estimates](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/close-to-weapons-grade-before-strikes).

Evidence can say how large each cost is. It cannot say how much a reduction in a nuclear risk is worth. Someone who gives the risk great weight will accept costs far above today's; someone who puts the cost to Americans first will want the war to end. A sharp rise in costs or casualties, or clear evidence that the risk has gone, would move people on either side.
