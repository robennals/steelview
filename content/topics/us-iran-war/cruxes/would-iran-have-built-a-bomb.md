The evidence on capability is strong and on intent is thin. [By US estimates, Iran was less than a week from enough weapons-grade uranium for a bomb before the June 2025 strikes](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/close-to-weapons-grade-before-strikes). Yet [the IAEA found no ongoing structured weapons programme in May 2025](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/no-ongoing-programme-2025), and [US intelligence judged that year that Iran was not building one](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/ata-2025-not-building). [The 2026 assessment says nothing either way](#fact-iran-nuclear-programme/ata-2026-silent).

If Iran was close to deciding, the case for striking before it could act is strong, and the containment argument loses its main support. If it was not, the war took a large risk to stop something that was not happening, and containment looks like the safer bet. The answer cannot be known from public evidence. What would move people is a declassified intelligence judgment, or Iranian records, showing whether a decision was being prepared.
