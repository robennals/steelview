claim: >-
  Since 2021 most people offered a UK safe and legal (humanitarian) route have
  come through two country-specific schemes: Ukraine (290,044 out-of-country
  visas since March 2022) and Hong Kong BN(O) (187,552 since January 2021).
  Almost no Ukrainians or Hong Kongers have crossed the Channel by small boat.
  For the main small-boat nationalities in the year to June 2026 (Eritreans,
  Sudanese, Somalis and Iranians), the largest route open to them was refugee
  family reunion, which peaked at 19,258 grants in 2024 and was paused on 4
  September 2025. The UK Resettlement Scheme had no quota for new referrals in
  2025/26 and reopened on 28 September 2026, with arrivals expected "in the
  hundreds at first".

Safe and legal routes are the ways a person can be given permission to come
to the UK for protection before travelling, instead of claiming asylum after
arriving. Since 2021 most such permissions have gone through two schemes for
specific countries. The UK has granted 290,044 Ukraine visas since March 2022
and 187,552 visas on the BN(O) route to people from Hong Kong since January 2021
[3](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-3)
[5](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-5). Almost no Ukrainians or Hong Kongers
crossed the Channel by small boat in the same years
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40). The main small-boat nationalities had
far smaller routes. For Eritreans, Sudanese, Somalis and Iranians the largest
was refugee family reunion, which let a refugee's partner and children join
them. It was paused on 4 September 2025
[14](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-14)
[18](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-18). The UK Resettlement Scheme, which
had no quota for new referrals in 2025/26, reopened on 28 September 2026 with
a yearly cap [22](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-22)
[24](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-24)
[26](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-26).

These are counts side by side. They show which nationalities had a route.
They do not show what a route causes, or how many people wanted one and had
none (see Data still needed).

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-all-routes}

### Two schemes for specific countries account for most grants since 2021 {#safe-and-legal-routes--two-country-schemes-dominate}

From 2010 to 2020, out-of-country grants ran between about five and thirteen
thousand a year, starting at 5,603 in 2010. They jumped when the BN(O) route
opened on 31 January 2021 and the Ukraine schemes in March 2022, peaking at
273,413 in 2022 [8](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-8)
[6](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-6)
[38](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-38). Ukraine grants peaked at 209,706 in
2022 and BN(O) grants at 86,439 in the year to March 2022, then both fell
[4](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-4) [5](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-5).
The Ukraine Family Scheme closed to new applications on 19 February 2024
[7](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-7). Out-of-country grants fell to 48,814 in
2025, and to 38,267 in the year to June 2026, 29% fewer than the year before
[38](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-38) [2](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-2).

### Grants are not arrivals, and extensions are not new people {#safe-and-legal-routes--grants-are-not-arrivals}

The Home Office headline counts 124,930 grants on these routes in the year
to June 2026, but 69% went to people already in the UK extending their leave
[1](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-1). This chart leaves extensions out. Visa
grants also overstate arrivals: Ukraine arrivals peaked at 110,000 in 2022,
against 209,706 visas granted [9](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-9)
[4](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-4).

### Resettlement peaked with the Afghan evacuation and has fallen since {#safe-and-legal-routes--resettlement-peaked-with-afghanistan}

Before 2021, resettlement was mostly Syrians: 22,157 people came through the
Vulnerable Persons and Vulnerable Children's schemes between 2014 and 2021
[13](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-13). It stopped in mid-2020 for the
pandemic [36](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-36). The Afghan Resettlement
Programme has resettled 39,058 people since 2021
[11](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-11), but its schemes closed to new
applicants or referrals in July 2025, with existing applications still being
processed [12](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-12). In the year to June 2026,
3,840 people were resettled, 48% fewer than a year earlier; 87% (3,352) were
on the Afghan programme and 13% (488) on the UK Resettlement Scheme and the
Mandate Scheme [10](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-10).

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-family-and-resettlement}

### Family reunion became the largest route open to any nationality, then was paused {#safe-and-legal-routes--family-reunion-paused}

Refugee family reunion lets the partner and children (aged 17 and under) of a
refugee in the UK join them, if they were part of the family before the
refugee fled [18](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-18). It ran at 4,113 to 7,456
grants a year from 2010 to 2022, then rose to 9,320 in 2023, 19,258 in 2024
and 18,577 in 2025 [37](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-37). The Home Office
links the rise to the large number of people granted refugee status in the
same period [15](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-15). The route was paused on 4
September 2025, as a "temporary pause" pending a review of family rules
[14](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-14)
[21](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-21). The Immigration Rules still describe
it as "closed to new applications pending a review"
[20](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-20). Grants in the year to June 2026 were
12,888, 38% lower, and still include applications made before the pause
[16](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-16)
[19](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-19).

### The UK Resettlement Scheme took no new referrals in 2025/26 {#safe-and-legal-routes--ukrs-no-new-referrals}

The UK Resettlement Scheme began in February 2021 and has resettled 4,589
refugees, from 1,269 in its first year to 537 in 2025
[45](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-45)
[13](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-13)
[39](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-39). In September 2025 the Home Office
confirmed "there is currently no 2025/2026 quota for new referrals to the
UKRS", with arrivals continuing only for people already accepted
[22](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-22).

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-boats-against-routes}

### Safe-route grants have exceeded small-boat arrivals in every year since 2021, by less each year {#safe-and-legal-routes--routes-against-boats-over-time}

In 2018 to 2020 there were 10,608 small-boat arrivals in total; from 2021 the
yearly figure has been between 29,000 and 46,000
[47](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-47). Out-of-country safe-route grants were
above that every year from 2021, peaking at 273,413 in 2022
[38](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-38). By 2025 the two were close, as the
Ukraine and BN(O) schemes shrank. Most of the grants went to nationalities
that were not crossing by boat (see the next chart).

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-routes-by-nationality}

### Nationalities with a large safe route almost never came by boat {#safe-and-legal-routes--nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat}

From 2018 to June 2026, 12 Ukrainians and no Hong Kong nationals were
recorded arriving by small boat, against 290,044 Ukraine visas and 187,552
visas on the BN(O) route [3](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-3)
[5](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-5)
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40). Both schemes were for people from one
place: the Ukraine Sponsorship Scheme for Ukrainians and their families with
a UK sponsor, and BN(O) for BN(O) passport holders resident in Hong Kong
[6](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-6) [8](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-8).

### For most boat nationalities, family reunion was the largest route {#safe-and-legal-routes--family-reunion-was-the-main-route-for-boat-nationalities}

For Eritreans, Sudanese, Iranians, Ethiopians and Yemenis, more than nine in
ten safe-route grants from 2018 to June 2026 were family reunion visas, and
for Somalis about two-thirds; resettlement was small and the Ukraine and
BN(O) schemes did not apply
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40)
[42](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-42)
[43](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-43). Family reunion depends on someone in
the family already holding refugee status in the UK
[18](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-18), and it is the route that was paused
in September 2025 [14](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-14). Eritreans, Sudanese,
Afghans, Somalis and Iranians were the five largest small-boat nationalities
in the year to June 2026, two-thirds of all arrivals
[30](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-30).

### Afghans and Syrians had a large route and also crossed in large numbers {#safe-and-legal-routes--afghans-and-syrians-had-both}

Afghans had the largest resettlement route (the Afghan Resettlement
Programme, 39,058 people since 2021) and were still among the largest
small-boat nationalities over the period
[11](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-11)
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40). Syrians had both resettlement and
family reunion at scale, and also crossed by boat in large numbers. A route
did not stop crossings where the route was small relative to the number of
people who wanted to come, or where it was open only to some of them; these
data cannot say which.

### Some large boat nationalities had almost no safe route {#safe-and-legal-routes--some-boat-nationalities-had-no-route}

Albanians, Iraqis, Vietnamese and Turkish nationals crossed by boat in large
numbers and received far fewer safe-route grants than boat arrivals;
Albanians received almost none
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40)
[41](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-41)
[42](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-42)
[43](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-43). The data do not say whether they would
have qualified for a route.

## Subtleties {#safe-and-legal-routes--subtleties-routes-by-nationality}

### A side-by-side count is not a test of cause {#safe-and-legal-routes--not-a-test-of-cause}

Ukrainians and Hong Kongers had a large route and did not cross by boat. That
pattern fits the view that routes take people off boats. It is also
consistent with other differences between these groups and the boat
nationalities, which these data do not measure. Afghans had a large route and crossed anyway.
No source here tests what a route does to crossings.

### Small-boat counts miss some people, and nationality is not always recorded {#safe-and-legal-routes--small-boat-counts-miss-some-people}

The small-boat figures count people detected arriving, not those who arrived
undetected or were stopped in France [33](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-33).
Some recent arrivals have no nationality recorded yet; they sit in the
residual. One further route, the UK/European Applicant Transfer Scheme set up
under the UK-France agreement [23](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-23), is not
part of the Home Office's safe and legal routes tables
[34](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-34) and is not shown.

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-routes-by-nationality-latest}

### After the pause, boat arrivals far exceeded safe-route grants for Eritreans, Sudanese, Somalis and Iranians {#safe-and-legal-routes--after-the-pause}

In the year to June 2026 small-boat arrivals were 33,374, 23% fewer than the
year before [29](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-29). Eritreans were the largest
group at 5,875; Sudanese arrivals rose 11% to 4,191 and Somali arrivals 76% to
3,901 [31](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-31)
[32](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-32). For each of these, and for Iranians,
safe-route grants were a fraction of boat arrivals, and most were
family reunion visas on applications made before the pause
[19](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-19)
[40](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-40)
[43](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-43). Afghans, Syrians and Yemenis received
more safe-route grants than they had boat arrivals, through resettlement
(Afghans) and family reunion. Afghanistan, Yemen, Sudan, Iran and Syria were
the top family reunion nationalities, at 17%, 13%, 11%, 11% and 9% of grants
[17](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-17).

## Observations {#safe-and-legal-routes--observations-reopening}

### Resettlement is reopening, with a yearly cap {#safe-and-legal-routes--resettlement-is-reopening}

On 28 September 2026 the Home Secretary reopened the UK Resettlement Scheme
to new referrals, with around 1 in 4 expected to be Afghan women and girls
and Palestinian refugees [24](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-24)
[25](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-25). Three new sponsorship routes
(community, student and work) are to follow, with first arrivals on the
community and student routes expected in autumn 2027
[28](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-28). People on these routes get a five-year
path to settlement, which the government describes as "by design, more
generous" than for those who arrive by small boat
[27](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-27).

### The planned scale is hundreds at first, rising to low thousands {#safe-and-legal-routes--planned-scale}

The government expects arrivals across resettlement and the three new routes
"to number in the hundreds at first", growing "into the low thousands as
levels of illegal migration fall", under a cap set each year
[26](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-26). For scale, there were 33,374
small-boat arrivals and 12,888 family reunion grants in the year to June 2026
[29](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-29)
[16](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-16). Family reunion remains closed
[20](#source-safe-and-legal-routes-20).
