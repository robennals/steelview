## Observations {#offending-by-nationality--observations-conviction-rates-asylum-nationalities}

### No statistics record whether an offender claimed asylum {#offending-by-nationality--no-asylum-status-data}

There is no published data on whether people convicted of crimes are asylum seekers, refugees or have any other immigration status. Several nationalities with high shares of asylum seekers, such as Iranians, Iraqis and Somalis, were among the 15 most common in prison in December 2025, but the data do not show whether those convicted had claimed asylum. [5](#source-offending-by-nationality-5)

### Adjusted for age and sex, Afghans were convicted of sex offences at seven times the British rate {#offending-by-nationality--afghan-sex-offence-rate}

In 2020–22, after adjusting for age and sex, Afghan nationals were convicted of sex offences at seven times the rate of British citizens, people from the Middle East and north Africa at about three times and sub-Saharan Africans at about twice. [1](#source-offending-by-nationality-1) [2](#source-offending-by-nationality-2) The Economist called its figure "still uncertain", saying it "may be skewed by a few serial offenders" and by reporting and detection rates. [2](#source-offending-by-nationality-2)

### Across all offences, Iraqi, Afghan and Iranian citizens were convicted at four to six and a half times the British rate {#offending-by-nationality--asylum-nationalities-all-offences}

Per head of population in 2021, Iraqi citizens were cautioned or convicted at 6.5 times the British rate, Afghans at 4.2 times, Iranians at 4.0 times and Syrians at 1.4 times, not adjusted for age or sex. [4](#source-offending-by-nationality-4) The Census showed most people born in Afghanistan had become British citizens by 2021, and they count as British here. [4](#source-offending-by-nationality-4)

## Subtleties {#offending-by-nationality--subtleties-conviction-rates-asylum-nationalities}

### Nationality is not immigration status {#offending-by-nationality--nationality-is-not-status}

Naturalised citizens are recorded as British, and country of birth is not recorded. [8](#source-offending-by-nationality-8) An Afghan or Iraqi citizen convicted of a crime may have claimed asylum, come on another visa, or have no status at all. [5](#source-offending-by-nationality-5)

### The detailed figures are withheld, and the Ministry is appealing an order to publish them {#offending-by-nationality--detailed-figures-withheld}

The Ministry of Justice refused a request for convictions by nationality and offence group; in June 2026 the Information Commissioner ruled that it must release them, withholding only counts under five. [6](#source-offending-by-nationality-6) The Ministry has appealed the ruling to a tribunal; it says it already publishes a "range of statistical breakdowns" on foreign offenders. [7](#source-offending-by-nationality-7)
