Polling on the ECHR and on removals is unusually sensitive to wording. The
same public that chooses "remain" over "withdraw" also backs replacing
"the current European system of human rights laws" when the question offers
faster deportation, so each result below is shown with the question asked.
[1](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-1) [7](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-7)

## Observations {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--observations-echr-membership}

### Remain has led withdraw since 2016 {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--remain-has-led-since-2016}

48% backed withdrawal in 2013 and 41% in July 2014, against 35% and 38% for
remaining. By 2016 the order had flipped, 42% to 35%. YouGov recorded 51% to
27% in June 2025 and 46% to 29% in October 2025, with 24% unsure.
[14](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-14) [5](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-5) [2](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-2) [3](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-3)
A Savanta poll for Amnesty International, reported in November 2025, found
48% for staying and 26% for withdrawing.
[8](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-8)

### Conservative and Reform voters lean towards leaving {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--conservative-and-reform-voters-lean-to-leaving}

In June 2025, 54% of Conservative voters and 72% of Reform voters favoured
leaving. In October 2025, 72% of Reform voters and 44% of Conservative voters
did, against 82% of Labour voters who wanted to remain.
[13](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-13) [4](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-4)

## Observations {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--observations-removals}

### The answer depends on whether the question offers a deportation power or alternatives {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--wording-moves-the-answer}

A question that does not name the ECHR but promises to "promptly deport
illegal migrants" drew 54% support, while offering middle options cut
outright support for leaving to 16%.
[7](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-7) [6](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-6)

### Removal of small-boat arrivals splits the public {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--small-boat-removal-splits-the-public}

In September 2023, 38% chose immediate removal and 43% case-by-case
decisions; 63% of 2019 Conservative voters chose removal and 71% of 2019
Labour voters chose case by case.
[9](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-9) [10](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-10)

### Support for removal targets small-boat arrivals far more than legal asylum seekers {#public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr--removal-support-targets-small-boat-arrivals}

In August 2025, 45% backed requiring large numbers of recent migrants to
leave. Counted across all adults, that came to 38% who included small-boat
asylum seekers and 16% who included asylum seekers who came through the
legal process. [11](#source-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr-11)
