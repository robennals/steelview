title: Channel Crossings and Irregular Arrivals
claim: >-
  About 193,000 people were detected crossing the Channel to the UK in small boats
  from 2018 to 2025, peaking at 45,774 in 2022, and small boats have been the main
  detected illegal route since 2020. Crossings fell sharply in 2026 — 47% below the
  same point of 2025 by 9 September — while irregular crossings into the EU fell by
  almost a third; days with good crossing weather did not fall in step, and no published
  source separates the effect of UK and French policy from the wider European fall.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-arrivals}

### About 193,000 people crossed from 2018 to 2025 {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--crossings-since-2018}

The Migration Observatory counts around 193,000 people detected reaching the UK in small boats from 2018 to the end of 2025. [1](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-1) Adding the 16,300 who arrived in 2026 up to 9 September takes the total past two hundred thousand. [3](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-3) Crossings were small until 2020: 10,608 across 2018–2020 together. [2](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-2)

### The peak year was 2022, and 2025 was close behind {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--the-peak-year-was-2022}

The Home Office's official count peaks at 45,774 in 2022. Since 2021, every year has had between 29,000 and 46,000 arrivals. [2](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-2) In 2025 around 41,500 people crossed, 13% more than in 2024 and the second-highest year. [1](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-1)

### Arrivals fell sharply in 2026 {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--arrivals-fell-sharply-in-2026}

By 9 September 2026, arrivals were 47% below the same point of 2025 and 27% below 2024. [3](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-3) On the 1 January–22 October line in the chart, 2026 is the lowest year since 2021. In the year to June 2026 the official count was 33,374, 23% lower than the year before. [2](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-2) Frontex, counting people who reached the UK plus people stopped from leaving France, puts Channel attempts in January–September 2026 down 43%. [4](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-4)

### What caused the 2026 fall is not settled {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--what-caused-the-2026-fall-is-not-settled}

Several candidate causes are documented, and none has been measured on its own:

- **Good crossing weather did not fall in step with arrivals.** Arrivals per good-weather day fell sharply (see the weather chart). [24](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-24)
- **French preventions fell roughly in step with arrivals.** The share of people stopped stayed near a third (see the preventions chart). [25](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-25)
- **Irregular crossings into the EU fell too, by almost a third in January–September.** Most people who cross the Channel travel through the EU first. [19](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-19) [21](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-21)
- **UK policy changed.** Changes included enforcement against smugglers, returns to France and tighter asylum rules. Returns to France were about 4% of arrivals between September 2025 and June 2026. [27](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-27)

The Migration Observatory concludes that UK policy and other factors probably both contributed, but that "it is difficult to isolate the effect of each factor". [5](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-5)

## Subtleties {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--subtleties-arrivals}

### Compare the same months of each year {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--compare-the-same-months-of-each-year}

Crossings are concentrated in the warmer months, and the Home Office advises against comparing short periods or different times of year. [18](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-18) That is why the chart has a second line counting only 1 January to 22 October: the 2026 point is a part year, and setting it against whole earlier years would overstate the fall.

### Detected arrivals are not every entry {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--detected-arrivals-are-not-every-entry}

These are people detected arriving. The Home Office warns that detection rates differ by route and that detection counts should not be read as the number of people entering. [7](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-7) Small-boat arrivals are not the same as asylum claims either: many people who claim asylum arrived on a visa.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-boats}

### Fewer boats, more people in each {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--fewer-boats-more-people-in-each}

The number of boats has fallen faster than the number of people. In the year to June 2026, 511 boats arrived, 33% fewer than the year before, and the average boat carried 65 people. That compares with 56 a year earlier and 7 in 2018. [29](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-29) A single boat carried a record 230 people in August 2026. The Migration Observatory suggests that seizures of boats and engines may be one reason. It also reports a shift towards departures from Belgium in the first half of 2026. [28](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-28)

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-nationality-people}

### A few nationalities make up most arrivals {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--a-few-nationalities-make-up-most-arrivals}

From 2018 to 2025, people from six countries made up 65% of arrivals: Iran (16%), Afghanistan (14%), Iraq (10%), Eritrea (10%), Albania (8%) and Syria (8%). [14](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-14)

### The mix has shifted towards the Horn of Africa and Sudan {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--the-mix-has-shifted}

Eritreans were the largest group in the year to June 2026, with 5,875 arrivals. Sudanese arrivals rose 11% to 4,191, and Somali arrivals rose 76% to 3,901. [16](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-16) [15](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-15) Syrian arrivals fell by 67% between 2024 and 2025, which the Home Office attributes to the fall of the Assad regime. [17](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-17) Albanian arrivals rose and fell within 2022 (see the Albanian chart below).

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-route-people}

### Boats grew as other detected routes shrank, but did not simply replace them {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--boats-did-not-simply-replace-lorries}

From 2018 to 2025, detections by every other method fell: inside the UK (many of them people who came in lorries or on ferries), at UK ports, and on flights without proper documents. Small-boat arrivals rose by far more, so total detections more than tripled. Small boats have been the main detected method since 2020. The Home Office says the fall in other methods was "likely in part" due to COVID-19. [6](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-6) By the year to June 2026, detections inside the UK were 77% below their 2019 peak. [8](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-8)

The Migration Observatory says tighter security at French ports and the Channel Tunnel after 2014 is likely to have contributed to the rise of the boat route. [9](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-9) Two limits apply. Detection rates differ by method, so the lines cannot be added up to a true total of people entering. [7](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-7) And no official series covers the years before 2018, when the switch would have begun.

### Before 2018, the published figures are attempts stopped in France {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--before-2018-the-figures-are-attempts-stopped-in-france}

Home Office figures released under freedom of information show nearly 164,000 attempts at clandestine entry stopped at border controls in France and Belgium from 2010 to 2015. [10](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-10) Most of them came in 2015, with 84,000 by Full Fact's count. [11](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-11) Motor Transport reported 81,000 foiled attempts at the French ports in 2015 and more than 56,000 in 2016. [13](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-13) The two 2015 figures come from different press reports and may cover different sets of controls. These are attempts, not people: one person can be stopped many times. [12](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-12) They therefore cannot be compared with the post-2018 counts of people arriving.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-monthly-people}

### Crossings are seasonal {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--crossings-are-seasonal}

Arrivals are lowest in mid-winter and highest from late spring to early autumn. The Home Office links this to crossing conditions. [18](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-18) From January to September 2026, every month except February was below the same month of 2025.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-europe-crossings}

### Irregular crossings into the EU have also fallen {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--crossings-into-the-eu-have-also-fallen}

Frontex recorded just over 91 000 irregular crossings into the EU in January–September 2026, almost a third fewer than a year earlier. Frontex attributes the fall to cooperation with partner countries and to preventive measures in departure states. [19](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-19) The Migration Observatory cites Frontex figures showing EU crossings fell 26% in 2025 and another 37% in the first half of 2026. It expects this to reduce Channel crossings "with a lag", because most people who cross the Channel travel through the EU first. [21](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-21) The UK's 2025 rise came while EU crossings were already falling, so the two do not move in lockstep.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-europe-change}

### The UK's fall is within the range of European routes {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--the-uk-fall-is-within-the-european-range}

Over January–August, the UK's fall was larger than the EU total's and smaller than the falls on the Central Mediterranean, Western African and eastern land-border routes. Frontex's January–September release gives the Central Mediterranean down 56% and the Western African route down 45%. The Western Mediterranean rose 26%. [20](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-20) The comparison shows that the UK's fall happened during a wider European one. It cannot show whether the two share a cause.

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-red-days}

### Weather explains little of the 2026 fall {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--weather-explains-little-of-the-2026-fall}

From January to August 2026 the Met Office judged 110 days likely for crossings ("red days"). That is about a tenth fewer than the 123 in 2025, and more than in any year from 2020 to 2024. Over the same months, the average number of people arriving on a red day fell 38%, from 232 to 144, and was 43% below the 2022 peak of 251. [24](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-24) The Home Office says 2026 had more favourable crossing conditions than almost any year since 2020. [23](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-23) The Migration Observatory also judges weather "unlikely to have had a significant impact" over periods this long. [22](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-22)

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-prevented-people}

### French preventions fell roughly in step with arrivals {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--french-preventions-fell-in-step-with-arrivals}

In the weeks to 18 October, the number of people the French authorities report stopping fell by nearly half compared with the same weeks of 2025, slightly more than arrivals fell. The share of people stopped stayed close to a third in both years. The share of events stopped rose, from a little over half to about three in five. Fewer, fuller boats would produce that pattern. The counts are French operational estimates. "Events" also include finds of equipment and arrests of facilitators, not only boats. [25](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-25)

### Interceptions at sea are not counted {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--interceptions-at-sea-are-not-counted}

France changed its maritime law to allow boats to be intercepted at sea in limited circumstances, but it is unclear how often this has happened. No count is published. [26](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-26)

## Observations {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--observations-albania-people}

### Albanian arrivals collapsed before the returns deal {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--albanian-arrivals-collapsed-before-the-deal}

The UK and Albania signed their returns agreement on 13 December 2022. Albania already accepted removals from the UK before then. [31](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-31) Albanian arrivals had already fallen: from around 9,000 in July–September 2022 to around 1,000 in October, 166 in November and 28 in December. [30](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-30) The fall was specific to Albanians: arrivals of other nationalities rose in the last quarter of 2022. [32](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-32) The deal therefore cannot explain the initial collapse.

### Albanian arrivals have stayed low since {#channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals--albanian-arrivals-have-stayed-low}

Albanian arrivals have stayed under a thousand a year since 2023, while arrivals from other countries recovered. Several things changed together after 2022, and the Migration Observatory says the deal "could be a major driver" of the continuing low numbers. [30](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-30) The government prioritised Albanian asylum cases, and returns to Albania rose sharply. Albania accounts for 76% of all small-boat returns. [33](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-33) The share of Albanian claims granted at first decision fell from almost half in 2021–2022 to 3% in 2024. [34](#source-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals-34) No source separates the effect of the deal from faster processing, more refusals and more returns.
