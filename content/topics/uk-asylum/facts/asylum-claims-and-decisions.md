Asylum claims in the UK reached about 105,000 people in 2024, the highest since records began in 1979, and about 101,000 in 2025. [3](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-3) Per head of population that was about 15 claims per 10,000 residents in 2025, mid-table in the EU+ and level with the EU-wide rate of 1.5 per thousand. [10](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-10) [7](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-7) The share of claims granted at initial decision fell from a record 76% in 2022 to 42% in 2025, [15](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-15) [16](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-16) and an appeal is allowed in about two in five cases heard. [26](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-26)

These figures count people who claim asylum inside the UK. They do not count resettled refugees, people on humanitarian visa routes such as those for Ukrainians, or people who enter illegally and never claim. Grant rates exclude withdrawn claims, so they describe decided claims, not all claims.

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-people}

### 2002 peak, then a long low, then a rise from 2021 {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--claims-over-time}

Claims peaked at around 103,000 in 2002, when most claimants came from Somalia, Afghanistan, Iraq and the former Yugoslavia, then ran between 22,000 and 46,000 a year until 2021. [2](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-2) [4](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-4) They rose from 2021, passed the 2002 peak and reached a rolling-year high of 109,662 in the year to September 2025. [2](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-2) The Migration Observatory links the rise since 2022 to conflict in the Middle East and instability in the Horn of Africa and South Asia, and notes that not everyone from an unstable country qualifies for asylum. [4](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-4)

### Claims are now falling {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--claims-now-falling}

85,891 people claimed asylum in the year to June 2026, 21% fewer than the year before. [1](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-1) Claims across the EU fell faster in 2025, by 27%. [8](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-8)

### About half of claimants arrived illegally; two in five had held a visa {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--routes-before-claiming}

In the year to June 2026, 52% of people claiming asylum came by illegal entry routes, mostly small boats, and 41% had held a visa or other permission to be in the UK. [5](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-5) [6](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-6) Asylum claims are therefore not a count of illegal arrivals, and illegal arrivals are not a count of claims.

## Subtleties {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--subtleties-people}

### People and cases are different counts {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--people-and-cases}

One claim can cover dependants. The headline counts people; grant rates are calculated on main applicants, one per case. The 2001 figure is main applicants only, because dependants enter the data in 2002. [37](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-37)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-per-head}

### Per head, UK claims rose to the EU average {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--uk-per-head-now-at-eu-average}

In every year from 2014 to 2024 the UK received fewer claims per head than the EU27 as a whole. The gap closed as UK claims rose and EU claims fell: in 2025 the UK received about 15 claims per 10,000 residents and the EU 1.5 per thousand, the same rate. [10](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-10) [7](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-7) Germany, which received the most claims per head of the large countries in 2015–2016 and again in 2023, fell below the UK in 2025.

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-grant-rate}

### The initial grant rate rose to a peak in 2022 and has since fallen back {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--grant-rate-over-time}

The grant rate was low in the 2000s, rose through the 2010s and reached a record 76% in 2022, partly because more claims came from nationalities usually granted at high rates and partly because rates rose within nationalities. [15](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-15) It fell to 42% in 2025 and 38% in the year to June 2026, against a rolling-year peak of 77% in the year to September 2022. [16](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-16) [11](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-11) The Home Office says grant rates move with the nationality mix, conditions in claimants' countries, and which cases are prioritised for decision. [12](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-12)

### The Nationality and Borders Act raised the standard of proof {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--standard-of-proof}

Claims made from 28 June 2022 must show persecution "on the balance of probabilities", a higher bar than before. The Migration Observatory says the fall "may be linked" to this change; no published analysis measures how much it contributed. [17](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-17)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-cohort-grant-rate}

### Appeals and reconsiderations raise the final grant rate {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--final-outcomes-after-appeal}

Following each year's claims to their latest outcome, the grant rate for claims made in 2007–2022 ended on average 20 percentage points above the initial rate, and between 8 and 29 points above it in every year. [22](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-22) [23](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-23) For claims made in 2010–2019, the latest-outcome grant rate was between about half and three-quarters.

### For recent claims the final picture is not yet known {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--recent-cohorts-incomplete}

For claims made since 2023, the latest-outcome rate is barely above the initial rate. That reflects missing data, not a finding: the Home Office could not load appeals data for this release, and appeals now take over a year. [25](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-25) [24](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-24)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-cohort-outcomes}

### Withdrawals rose sharply for claims made in 2021–2024 {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--withdrawals}

Withdrawn claims, which end without a decision, rose with the claims made from 2021. In 2023 the government widened the grounds on which a claim can be treated as withdrawn, though withdrawals were already rising. [30](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-30) In the year to June 2026, 16,265 people had their claims withdrawn, 79% of them implicitly, usually for missing an interview or other step. [29](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-29) A withdrawal is not a finding that the claimant was or was not a refugee.

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-appeals-allowed}

### Two in five asylum appeals are allowed {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--two-in-five-appeals-allowed}

39% of asylum and protection appeals determined in April–June 2026 were allowed. [26](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-26) The allowed share rose from about a quarter in 2007/08 to about half in 2021/22–2022/23, and has fallen back since, to 39% in 2025/26. The appeals counted here were decided in that year and may concern claims refused years earlier.

### The appeals queue has grown {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--appeals-backlog}

The open asylum appeals caseload grew from 6,319 at the end of June 2022 to 87,450 at the end of March 2026, and an asylum appeal now takes 72 weeks on average to clear. [28](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-28) [27](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-27)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-grant-rate-by-nationality}

### Grant rates differ widely by nationality {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--grant-rates-by-nationality}

In the year to June 2026, 92% of Sudanese and 84% of Eritrean claims decided were granted, against 11% for Bangladeshis, 10% for Syrians and 1% for Indians. [13](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-13)

### Rates fell within most nationalities, steeply for some {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--within-nationality-falls}

The Syrian grant rate fell from 98% to 10% in a year after the fall of the Assad regime, and the Pakistani rate from 46% to 25%. [14](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-14) Of the ten nationalities with most decisions in 2025, only Sudan was granted at a higher rate than in 2022. [17](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-17)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-grant-rate-decomposition}

### Most of the 2022–2025 fall came within nationalities, not from a changed mix {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--why-the-grant-rate-fell}

Applying each nationality's 2022 grant rate to the 2025 mix of decisions gives a rate about seven points below 2022's. Applying 2025 rates to the 2022 mix gives a rate close to 2025's. So the shift towards lower-grant nationalities such as Pakistanis, Bangladeshis and Indians explains a minority of the fall; lower rates for the same nationalities, notably Afghans, Syrians, Turks and Vietnamese, explain most of it. This calculation cannot say why rates fell within a nationality: a higher standard of proof, changed country conditions and a different mix of cases decided are all candidates. [12](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-12) [17](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-17)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-small-boat-outcomes}

### Most decided small-boat claims were granted {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--small-boat-claims-mostly-granted}

Of 178,845 people who arrived by small boat in 2018–2025 and claimed asylum, 79,589 had been granted at initial decision and 53,305 refused, a grant rate of 60% on cases. [18](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-18) [19](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-19) Rates vary by nationality: 86% for Eritreans who arrived in 2025, 34% for Afghans. [21](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-21)

### 2023 and 2024 arrivals were refused more often than granted, and many claims ended without a decision {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--2023-2024-arrivals-granted-under-half}

For people who arrived in 2023 and 2024, refusals slightly outnumbered grants at initial decision; for 2020–2022 and 2025 arrivals, grants were the clear majority. Across all arrivals, 33,413 claims, or 18.7%, were withdrawn or closed administratively without a decision on the merits. [20](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-20) [18](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-18) Withdrawals were concentrated among 2022 arrivals. These figures exclude appeals.

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-recognition-2025}

### The EU grant rate fell too, to about the UK's level {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--eu-grant-rate-similar}

39% of first-instance decisions across the EU were positive in 2025, and 21% of final decisions after appeal or review. [31](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-31) The UK rate on the same people-based measure was similar. In 2024 the UK's rate was 47% against the EU's 51%; the Migration Observatory notes that overall rates mostly reflect which nationalities claim where. [34](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-34)

## Observations {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--observations-recognition-by-nationality}

### For the same nationality, UK and EU rates differ in both directions {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--uk-and-eu-by-nationality}

The UK has granted higher rates than the EU for most of its main nationalities since 2018, but grants Afghans far less often: 51% against 81% in 2024, [33](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-33) and in 2025 the EU granted 73% of Afghans. [32](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-32) In 2025 the UK also granted Somalis and Syrians at lower rates than the EU, and Iranians, Sudanese and Vietnamese at higher ones.

## Subtleties {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--subtleties-per-head-2025}

### The UK's European rank depends on what is counted {#asylum-claims-and-decisions--rank-depends-on-measure}

The UK ranks thirteenth of 32 on first-time applicants per head in 2025 in the chart above. The Migration Observatory puts it twelfth for 2025; [10](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-10) the Home Office puts it sixteenth for the year to March 2026, counting all applicants in other countries, including repeat applications. [9](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-9) By total number the UK is fifth on every measure. [9](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-9) [10](#source-asylum-claims-and-decisions-10)
