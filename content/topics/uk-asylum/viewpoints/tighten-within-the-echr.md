name: "Stop the crossings and speed up removals by changing British law, without leaving the ECHR"
summary: "What stops removals is mostly [late claims and legal challenges, not human-rights claims](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims), and British law can fix those. Britain should clear its queues, narrow family-life claims by statute and tie protection to continuing danger, while keeping the Convention and [the European cooperation that depends on it](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation)."
order: 3
---
A refusal should mean leaving, or [rules do not mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say). What stands in the way is mostly of Britain's own making, and British law can fix it. Among people in detention facing removal, [late asylum claims, medical reports, modern-slavery referrals and judicial reviews are the commonest obstacles, and human-rights applications are a small share](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims). [Fresh asylum claims and judicial reviews were the commonest reasons seats went empty on the Jamaica charters](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/charter-reasons). Among foreign offenders, [appeals won on human-rights grounds alone equal about 3% of deportations](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/human-rights-wins-about-3-percent).

Clearing queues works. [The first-decision backlog is down 77% from its peak, and hotel use down 71%](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/backlog-and-hotels-below-peaks), and [asylum support fell from £4.7 billion to £4.0 billion a year](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/support-costs-about-4bn-a-year). [Hotels held about a third of people but took three-quarters of the cost](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/hotels-third-of-people-three-quarters-of-cost), so closing them is where the savings lie.

The queue has [moved into the appeals system, which now takes well over a year per case](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/queue-moved-to-appeals). That is why the Immigration and Asylum Bill [replaces the tribunal with a new appeals authority and rewrites how judges weigh family life](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/immigration-and-asylum-bill). [Its impact assessment models about 11,700 more Article 8 refusals a year, while calling that neither a target nor a forecast, and expects many of those refused not to leave](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/the-bill-sets-no-target). [The 2014 reforms cut offenders' human-rights wins by three-quarters before they climbed back](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/article-8-reforms-cut-then-faded), so a statute alone is not enough. It needs judges who apply it. [All 46 Council of Europe states have now agreed that the Strasbourg court should need "strong reasons" to override national courts on family life](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/chisinau-declaration). [The declaration is not binding, and restates the court's own case law](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/the-declaration-is-non-binding), but it gives British judges a reading every member state has signed.

Fast decisions with returns keep a route shut. [Albanian arrivals have stayed low since 2023 as Albanian claims were prioritised, nearly all refused at first decision, and returns rose](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/albanian-arrivals-have-stayed-low), even though [the first collapse came before the returns deal](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/albanian-arrivals-collapsed-before-the-deal). [Asylum-related returns are now at a record](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/asylum-related-returns-at-record). Protection should last as long as the danger does: [the Syrian grant rate fell from 98% to 10% in a year after Assad fell](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/within-nationality-falls), which is the case for [30-month protection, renewed only if return is still unsafe](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/uk-2026-changes). Temporary status will not get refugees into work, since [less-secure status has not raised employment elsewhere in Europe](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/less-secure-status-no-employment-gain); its purpose is to match protection to need.

No one policy has stopped the boats. [Crossings fell sharply in 2026](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/arrivals-fell-sharply-in-2026), but [the fall sits within a wider European one](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/the-uk-fall-is-within-the-european-range), and [the France returns pilot moved about 4% of arrivals before it ended](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/france-pilot-about-4-percent). That argues for pressing on every stage at once, not for one dramatic act.

Leaving the ECHR would fix few of the measured obstacles. [The Refugee Convention's ban on returning refugees to danger would remain](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains), [the EU could end law-enforcement and criminal-records cooperation](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation), and [whether withdrawal breaches the Good Friday Agreement is disputed](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/good-friday-agreement-is-disputed). Britain also keeps an [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger): [Sudanese and Eritrean claimants are granted in the great majority of cases](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/grant-rates-by-nationality). Speed up decisions and appeals, narrow Article 8 in statute, remove those refused, and keep the Convention.
