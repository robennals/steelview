---
name: "Britain owes refuge to people fleeing persecution: safe routes, fair hearings and the right to work"
summary: "[Most people who cross the Channel and receive a decision are granted protection](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted). Britain should give refugees safe ways to come, decide their claims fairly and let them work while they wait, instead of paying for deterrence schemes like Rwanda, which [sent no asylum seekers at all](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/rwanda-cost-and-relocations)."
order: 2
---
Britain has an [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger), and most people who cross the Channel are found to need protection. [Most decided claims by small-boat arrivals since 2018 were granted at first decision](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted), and [the largest groups now crossing, Eritreans and Sudanese, are granted in the great majority of cases](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/grant-rates-by-nationality). Britain is not taking an outsized share either: it [receives about as many claims per head as the EU average](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/uk-per-head-now-at-eu-average).

First decisions are not the last word. [Two in five asylum appeals are allowed](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/two-in-five-appeals-allowed), and [for claims made in 2007–2022, appeals and reconsideration raised the final grant rate by about 20 points on average](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/final-outcomes-after-appeal). Grant rates have fallen since 2022, and [mostly within nationalities rather than from a change in who claims](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/why-the-grant-rate-fell). Some of that reflects real change, such as [the fall of the Assad regime in Syria](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/within-nationality-falls); some may reflect [the higher standard of proof introduced in 2022](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/standard-of-proof). [Arrivals in 2023 and 2024 were refused more often than granted at first decision](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/2023-2024-arrivals-granted-under-half), which is a reason to hear their appeals properly. Replacing the tribunal with [an authority whose adjudicators need not be legally qualified, under a Chair the Home Secretary appoints](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/immigration-and-asylum-bill) moves the other way. [Rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say), and the Refugee Convention's promise means nothing without a fair hearing.

Safe routes take people off boats. [Hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians and Hong Kongers came on visas, and almost none by boat](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat). For [Eritreans, Sudanese and Iranians, the main route was family reunion, which was paused in 2025](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/family-reunion-was-the-main-route-for-boat-nationalities), and [since then their boat arrivals have far exceeded the grants they receive](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/after-the-pause). [Afghans had a large route and still crossed](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/afghans-and-syrians-had-both), so a route works only if it is big enough and open to the people who need it. [The reopened resettlement scheme expects hundreds of arrivals at first](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/planned-scale). That is too small.

Deterrence has cost a great deal for little. [Britain spent £715 million on the Rwanda partnership and the Illegal Migration Act, and sent four volunteers and no asylum seekers](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/rwanda-cost-and-relocations). [Australia's offshore system has cost more than A$13 billion](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/offshore-cost-per-year), and [18 of the people it sent offshore had died by 2021](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-outcomes). Australia's arrivals did [fall to almost none after it turned boats back](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks), but [turnbacks depend on the country of departure cooperating](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/turnbacks-need-departure-country), and [the UK–France returns pilot moved about 4% of arrivals before it ended](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/france-pilot-about-4-percent). The 2026 fall in crossings [came during a wider European fall](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/the-uk-fall-is-within-the-european-range).

People waiting should be allowed to work. [Work bans lower refugees' employment for years afterwards](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-bans-reduce-employment), while [shorter bans show only a modest association with where people claim](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-rights-modest-association). [Making status less secure has not raised refugees' employment in Denmark, Germany or Sweden](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/less-secure-status-no-employment-gain).

People refused after a fair hearing should leave, and [most issues raised in detention to stop removal were eventually refused](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused), so some claims are made to delay. The answer is quick, fair decisions. [The obstacles to removal are mostly late claims and judicial reviews, not human-rights claims](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims). Leaving the ECHR [would leave the Refugee Convention's ban on return in place](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains) and [could cost Britain its law-enforcement cooperation with the EU](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation). Britain should reopen family reunion, build routes on the scale of the need, let people work while they wait, and fund hearings that get decisions right.
