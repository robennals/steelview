Britain has an obligation to people in danger. Britain should give refugees safe ways to come, fair hearings, and the right to work while they wait.

### Most people crossing the Channel are found to need protection

[Most small-boat arrivals who got a decision were granted protection](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted). [So were most Eritreans and Sudanese](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/sudanese-and-eritreans-mostly-granted), [the largest groups now crossing](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/eritreans-and-sudanese-largest-boat-groups). By Britain's own decisions, then, most would face danger if sent back. Britain has an obligation to people in danger.

### Many refusals are overturned on appeal, so appeals must stay fair

[Since 2007, between a quarter and a half of asylum appeals each year have succeeded](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/two-in-five-appeals-allowed). A wrong refusal can send someone back to danger. Yet [ministers plan a new appeals body whose decision-makers need not be lawyers](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal), with [its chair appointed by the Home Secretary](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal). While [grant rates at first decision have fallen since 2022](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/grant-rate-over-time), some of that reflects real change: [after Syria's government fell, Syrians' grant rate dropped from 98% to 10% in a year](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/syrian-grant-rate-fell). [A 2022 law also raised the standard of proof](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/standard-of-proof), which may explain some of the rest. A higher bar makes fair appeals matter more.

### Where a safe route existed, people could come without a boat

[Hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians and Hong Kongers got visas, and almost none came by boat](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat). Some of the largest groups who cross by boat had almost no safe route. A safe route gives people in danger a way in without a smuggler. [For the groups who do cross, boat arrivals far outnumbered safe-route grants in the year to June 2026](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/after-the-pause), a year when family reunion was paused. For Eritreans, Sudanese and Iranians, the main safe route was joining family here. A route does not stop every crossing: Afghans had a large route and still crossed. But [the reopened scheme and its new routes plan for only hundreds of people at first](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/planned-scale), far fewer than cross by boat.

### Britain's deterrence scheme failed at great cost

[Britain spent £715 million on the Rwanda scheme and a related law](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/rwanda-cost-and-relocations), and [sent no asylum seekers there](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/no-asylum-seekers-sent). So Britain paid for a deterrent that never ran.

[Australia's offshore camps cost billions](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/offshore-cost-per-year), and [18 people sent there had died by 2021](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/eighteen-had-died). While [Australia's arrivals fell to almost none after it turned boats back](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks), [handing people back at sea needs the country they left from to take them](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/turnbacks-need-departure-country).

### Work bans hurt refugees and do little to deter claims

[Studies across Europe find that work bans leave refugees less likely to have a job for years afterwards](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-bans-reduce-employment). [The one study of where people choose to claim finds that bans are not a strong deterrent](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/authors-say-bans-not-a-strong-deterrent). So a ban holds refugees back without keeping many people away.

While [shorter bans are linked to slightly more claims](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-rights-modest-association), the study's authors judge the link too small to make bans a strong deterrent.

### So Britain should open safe routes, decide claims fairly, and let people work

This meets Britain's duty to people in danger, and gives them a way in without a smuggler. People refused after a fair hearing should then leave.

While [over four in five challenges raised in detention in 2017 failed](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused), the answer is quick, fair decisions. [In 2019, most challenges were asylum claims, medical reports, slavery referrals and court challenges, not human-rights applications](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims). Leaving the European Convention on Human Rights [would not remove the Refugee Convention's ban on sending refugees back to danger](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains), and [could cost Britain police cooperation with the EU](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation).

Britain should reopen family reunion, build routes to match the need, let people work, and fund hearings that get decisions right.
