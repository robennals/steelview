Britain has an [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger). Britain should give refugees safe ways to come, in numbers it chooses, fair hearings, and the right to work while they wait.

### Most people crossing the Channel have been found to need protection

[Of small-boat arrivals since 2018 who got a decision, most were granted protection](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted). [So were most Eritreans and Sudanese in 2025](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/sudanese-and-eritreans-mostly-granted), [now the largest groups crossing](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/eritreans-and-sudanese-largest-boat-groups). By Britain's own decisions, most would face danger if sent back.

While [arrivals in 2023 and 2024 were refused slightly more often than granted](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/2023-2024-arrivals-granted-under-half), part of the fall in grants reflects real change: [after Syria's government fell, Syrians' grant rate dropped from 98% to 10% in a year](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/syrian-grant-rate-fell).

### Britain has closed the safe routes most of them could use

[Family reunion, joining a refugee already here, became the largest route open to any nationality, and was then paused](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/family-reunion-paused). [The UK Resettlement Scheme took no new referrals in 2025/26](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/ukrs-no-new-referrals). That leaves the people now crossing little other way in.

[For Eritreans, Sudanese and Iranians, family reunion had been the main safe route](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/family-reunion-was-the-main-route-for-boat-nationalities). [In the year to June 2026, boat arrivals far exceeded safe-route grants for all three](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/after-the-pause). [Some of the largest groups who cross had almost no safe route at all](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/some-boat-nationalities-had-no-route).

### A safe route lets Britain choose how many it protects; a boat does not

[Hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians and Hong Kongers came on visas, and almost none came by boat](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat). Britain set those numbers, and no one paid a smuggler. [The reopened resettlement scheme has a yearly cap](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/resettlement-is-reopening).

Safe routes do not mean fewer people come. [Afghans had the largest resettlement route and still crossed](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/afghans-large-route-still-crossed), and [comparing routes with boats side by side is not a test of cause](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/not-a-test-of-cause). The case is that Britain, not smugglers, decides who comes and how many.

### Many refusals are overturned on appeal, so appeals must stay fair

[Since 2007, between a quarter and a half of asylum appeals each year have succeeded](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/two-in-five-appeals-allowed). A wrong refusal can send someone back to danger. Yet [ministers plan a new appeals body whose decision-makers need not be lawyers](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal).

[Counting appeals and second decisions, the share of claims finally granted rose by 20 points on average for claims made in 2007–2022](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/final-outcomes-after-appeal). [The new body's chair would be appointed by the Home Secretary](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal). [A 2022 law also raised the standard of proof](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/standard-of-proof). A higher bar makes fair appeals matter more, if [rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say).

### Britain paid £715 million for a deterrent that sent no asylum seekers

[Britain spent £715 million on the Rwanda scheme and a related law](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/rwanda-cost-and-relocations), and [sent no asylum seekers there](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/no-asylum-seekers-sent). So Britain paid for a deterrent that never ran.

[Australia's offshore camps cost billions](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/offshore-cost-per-year), and [18 people sent there had died by 2021](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/eighteen-had-died). While [Australia's arrivals fell to almost none after it turned boats back](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks), [handing people back at sea needs the country they left from to take them](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/turnbacks-need-departure-country).

### Work bans hurt refugees and do little to deter claims

[Studies across Europe find that work bans leave refugees less likely to have a job for years afterwards](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-bans-reduce-employment). [The one study of where people choose to claim finds that bans are not a strong deterrent](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/authors-say-bans-not-a-strong-deterrent). So a ban holds refugees back without keeping many people away.

While [shorter bans are linked to slightly more claims](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-rights-modest-association), the study's authors judge the link too small to make bans a strong deterrent.

### So Britain should open capped safe routes, decide claims fairly, and let people work

This meets Britain's duty to people in danger, in numbers Britain chooses, without a smuggler. People refused after a fair hearing should then leave.

Leaving the European Convention on Human Rights [would not remove the Refugee Convention's ban on sending refugees back to danger](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains), and [could cost Britain police cooperation with the EU](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation). Britain should reopen family reunion, set resettlement to match the need, let people work, and fund hearings that get decisions right.
