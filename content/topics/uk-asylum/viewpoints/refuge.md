Britain has an [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger). Britain should give refugees safe ways to come, fair hearings, and the right to work while they wait.

### Most people crossing the Channel are found to need protection

[Most small-boat arrivals who got a decision were granted protection](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted), as are [most Eritreans and Sudanese](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/sudanese-and-eritreans-mostly-granted). By Britain's own decisions, most would face danger if sent back, and Britain has an [obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger).

[Eritreans and Sudanese are the largest groups now crossing](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/eritreans-and-sudanese-largest-boat-groups).

### Many refusals are overturned on appeal, so appeals must stay fair

[Since 2007, between a quarter and a half of asylum appeals have been allowed each year](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/two-in-five-appeals-allowed), and a wrong refusal can send someone back to danger. Yet ministers plan [an appeals body whose adjudicators need not be lawyers](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal).

[Appeals and reconsiderations raised final grant rates an average 20 points for 2007–2022 claims](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/final-outcomes-after-appeal). The new body would have [a chair the Home Secretary appoints](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/bill-replaces-the-tribunal). If [rules must mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say), the Refugee Convention's promise needs a fair hearing.

While [grant rates have fallen since 2022](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/grant-rate-over-time), some of that reflects real change, as in [Syria, where the rate fell from 98% to 10% in a year](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/syrian-grant-rate-fell), and [a 2022 law raised the bar of proof](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/standard-of-proof), which may explain some of the rest. That makes fair appeals matter more.

### Where a safe route existed, people could come without a boat

[Hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians and Hong Kongers got visas, and almost none came by boat](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat), while [some of the largest groups crossing had almost no safe route](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/some-boat-nationalities-had-no-route). A route gives people in danger a way in that needs no smuggler.

For the groups who do cross, [boat arrivals far exceeded safe-route grants in the year to June 2026](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/after-the-pause), which took in a pause in family reunion.

[For Eritreans, Sudanese and Iranians, the main safe route was joining family here](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/family-reunion-was-the-main-route-for-boat-nationalities). While [Afghans had a large route and still crossed](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/afghans-large-route-still-crossed), that may be because routes were too small or open only to some. [The reopened scheme and its new routes expect only hundreds at first](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/planned-scale).

### Britain's deterrence scheme failed at great cost

[Britain spent £715 million on the Rwanda scheme and a related law](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/rwanda-cost-and-relocations), and [sent no asylum seekers](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/no-asylum-seekers-sent). That money would do more good spent on routes and hearings.

[Australia's offshore camps cost billions](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/offshore-cost-per-year), and [18 people sent there had died by 2021](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/eighteen-had-died). While [Australia's arrivals fell to almost none after it turned boats back](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks), [handing people back at sea relies on the departure country working with Australia](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/turnbacks-need-departure-country).

### Letting people work helps them, and draws only modestly more claims

[Work bans lower refugees' employment for years afterwards](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-bans-reduce-employment), and [the study's authors judge that bans are not a strong deterrent](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/authors-say-bans-not-a-strong-deterrent). A ban holds refugees back without keeping many people away.

While [shorter bans show a modest link with where people claim](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/work-rights-modest-association), the authors do not find that bans stop people coming.

### So Britain should open safe routes, decide claims fairly, and let people work

That meets Britain's duty to people in danger and gives them a way in that needs no smuggler. People refused after a fair hearing should then leave.

While [most issues raised against removal led to no positive decision](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused), the answer is quick, fair decisions. [In 2019 the issues raised were mostly asylum claims, medical reports, slavery referrals and legal challenges, not human-rights applications](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims). Leaving the European Convention on Human Rights [would keep the Refugee Convention's ban on return](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains) and [could cost Britain EU police cooperation](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation).

Britain should reopen family reunion, build routes to match the need, let people work, and fund hearings that get decisions right.
