More than [two hundred thousand people have crossed the Channel by small boat since 2018](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/crossings-since-2018), and almost none could have reached Britain [without first passing through another safe country](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/uk-law-ties-article-31-to-the-first-safe-country). Parliament decided in 2022 that such a person has not "come directly" from danger. The law should follow that through: someone who enters illegally should not be able to claim asylum here, and should be removed. The Refugee Convention [forbids sending a refugee back to danger, but does not by itself oblige Britain to decide every claim, and the EU applies its own first-country rule](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/article-31-is-about-penalties).

Today a refusal rarely ends in departure. [About half of refused claimants since 2010 are recorded as leaving](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/about-half-of-refused-claimants-recorded-leaving), and [refusals now far outrun removals in any year](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/refusals-outrun-returns). The claim made at the last moment is why. [Late asylum claims and judicial reviews are the commonest obstacles for people in detention](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims), yet [most of the issues raised there are eventually refused](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused); [fresh asylum claims emptied more seats on the Jamaica charters than anything else](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/charter-reasons). Then come the appeals: [the tribunal's queue has grown more than fourteen-fold since 2022, and an appeal takes over a year](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/queue-moved-to-appeals).

Many who cross are refugees, and [most decided small-boat claims have been granted at first decision](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted). But [the same nationalities are now refused far more often than in 2022](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/why-the-grant-rate-fell), and [arrivals in 2023 and 2024 were refused more often than granted](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/2023-2024-arrivals-granted-under-half). Britain should protect the refugees it chooses to resettle, not whoever reaches a beach.

Changing the law inside the Convention has been tried. [After the 2014 Act, appeals that foreign offenders won on human-rights grounds fell by three-quarters, then climbed back within three years, alongside a Supreme Court judgment that let them appeal before deportation](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/article-8-reforms-cut-then-faded). Lord Wolfson advised that [new limits inside the ECHR would probably be litigated to failure, that leaving it is lawful, and that the Refugee Convention can stay](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/wolfson-withdrawal-possible-refugee-convention-unnecessary).

[No one has counted how many removals the ECHR blocks](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-share-not-measured), and [human-rights wins equal about 3% of offender deportations](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/human-rights-wins-about-3-percent). But [Article 3 risk is argued inside asylum claims, so those counts are a floor](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-hidden-in-other-rows). The deeper question is who decides. Under [democratic consent](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership), the rules for removal belong to a Parliament that voters can dismiss. [Remain has led withdraw in polls since 2016](#fact-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr/remain-has-led-since-2016), but [a majority in one poll backed replacing European rights law with British law that allows prompt deportation](#fact-public-opinion-on-asylum-and-the-echr/wording-moves-the-answer); that is the choice to put at an election. [The EU could end law-enforcement cooperation](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation), but that would be its own political choice, and one to negotiate.

Certainty deters. [Australia's boat arrivals fell to almost none once boats were turned back](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks), [within a chain of measures that included a rule that no boat arrival would ever settle](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-cause).

Removal on this scale is a large task: [150,000 a year is about 15 times today's enforced returns](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/150000-about-15-times-enforced-returns). But [most returns are already voluntary, at under a tenth of the cost](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-returns-are-voluntary), and the status quo costs [around £4 billion a year in asylum support](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/support-costs-about-4bn-a-year). A government's [first duty is to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people): bar the claims, remove those who come, and take back the power to do it.
