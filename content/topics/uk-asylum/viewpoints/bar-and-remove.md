Anyone who enters Britain illegally should not be allowed to stay. Britain should refuse their claims, remove them, and leave European human-rights law if it stands in the way.

### People crossing the Channel have already passed through other countries, where British law expects them to claim asylum

[The boats leave from France and Belgium](#fact-channel-crossings-and-irregular-arrivals/boats-leave-from-france-and-belgium), so everyone on them has already been in another country. [Few refugees can reach Britain without first passing through another country](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/few-reach-the-uk-without-passing-through-another-state). [British law expects a refugee who stopped in another country to have sought protection there](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/uk-law-ties-article-31-to-the-first-safe-country). Crossing on to Britain is a choice of where to claim. [One European study finds that what most draws people to a country is people from home who already live there](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/networks-strongest-pull). The Refugee Convention [forbids sending a refugee back to danger](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/article-33-forbids-return), but does not say Britain must decide every claim. So Britain can refuse to hear these claims without sending anyone back to danger.

### People who claim asylum can expect to stay, even if they are refused

[Up to 2013, over a quarter of each year's asylum claims ended with the person recorded as returned](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/fewer-refused-recorded-leaving). [Since 2015, under one in seven have](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/fewer-refused-recorded-leaving). [Even among claims that were refused or withdrawn, under a third have](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/fewer-refused-recorded-leaving). So most people who claim asylum, granted or refused, are still here, as far as the records show. [In 2019, the most common challenge from people held for removal was an asylum claim](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/asylum-claims-commonest-in-detention). [In detentions ending in 2017, over four in five challenges to removal failed](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused), but challenges take time: [an asylum appeal takes over a year on average](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/asylum-appeal-takes-72-weeks).

### Australia's boats stopped once it began turning them back

[From late 2013, Australia turned asylum boats back at sea, and arrivals fell to almost none](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/australia-arrivals-fell-after-turnbacks). [Months earlier, it had banned boat arrivals from ever settling in Australia, and the boats had kept coming](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/offshore-processing-alone). Making the crossing fail worked where the settlement ban had not. [Britain withdrew its own policy of turning boats back, saying it could rarely be done safely in the Channel](#fact-deterrence-and-protection-policies/uk-turnbacks-withdrawn), but removing everyone who crosses would make the crossing fail without turning boats back at sea. While [most small-boat arrivals who get a decision are granted protection](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted), Britain can still protect refugees without the boats, [as it did for Ukrainians and Hong Kongers through safe routes](#fact-safe-and-legal-routes/nationalities-with-safe-routes-rarely-came-by-boat).

### Human-rights claims stop removals, the Home Office says

[The Home Office says people use the right to family life, Article 8, to stop removals. It says 86% of people who made a human-rights claim while held for removal in 2022 were released](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/home-office-says-rights-claims-stop-removals). [Lord Wolfson, advising the Conservative leader, judged that new limits written within the ECHR would likely fail in court](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/wolfson-limits-inside-the-echr-likely-fail). If limits within the ECHR would fail, leaving it is the way to make removals stick. While [foreign offenders who won on human-rights grounds alone equalled only about 3% of those deported](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/human-rights-wins-about-3-percent), [that count misses claims of danger made inside asylum cases](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/counts-miss-article-3-in-asylum-claims), and [no published figure counts the removals the ECHR stops](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-share-not-measured).

### So Britain should bar these claims, remove those who come, and leave the ECHR

If Britain refused these claims and removed those who come, crossing would no longer pay. Leaving the ECHR would let Parliament set those rules. A government's first duty is to its own people.

While [the Conservatives' pledge of 150,000 removals a year would be about 15 times today's enforced returns](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/150000-about-15-times-enforced-returns), [most returns are already voluntary](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-returns-are-voluntary), and [those cost far less](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/voluntary-returns-far-cheaper). [Supporting asylum seekers already costs around £4 billion a year](#fact-asylum-backlog-accommodation-and-cost/support-costs-about-4bn-a-year).
