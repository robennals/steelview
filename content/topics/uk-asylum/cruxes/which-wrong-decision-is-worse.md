---
question: Is refusing a real refugee a worse error than letting someone without a claim stay?
kind: tradeoff
divides: [refuge, bar-and-remove, tighten-within-the-echr]
positions:
  - viewpoint: refuge
    holds: Yes. Sending someone back to persecution can cost a life; a person wrongly allowed to stay costs money. Hearings should be built to avoid the first error even at the price of more of the second.
  - viewpoint: bar-and-remove
    holds: No. Every person allowed to stay without a right to it shows smugglers the route works, which draws more crossings and more deaths at sea. Protection belongs to those Britain resettles by choice.
  - viewpoint: tighten-within-the-echr
    holds: Both matter, and speed reduces both. Quick, careful first decisions and a fast appeal cut wrongful refusals and wrongful stays together.
---
The rates frame the trade-off. [Most decided small-boat claims were granted at first decision](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/small-boat-claims-mostly-granted), [two in five appeals are allowed](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/two-in-five-appeals-allowed), and [appeals have raised final grant rates well above first decisions](#fact-asylum-claims-and-decisions/final-outcomes-after-appeal). On the other side, [most issues raised in detention to stop removal were eventually refused](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/most-issues-refused), and [only about half of refused claimants are recorded leaving](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/about-half-of-refused-claimants-recorded-leaving).

A rule that bars claims from everyone who crosses makes the first error for every refugee among them; a system of long appeals and weak removal makes the second error for many of those refused. [An obligation to people in danger](#principle-obligation-to-people-in-danger) weighs the first error heavily; [a government's duty to its own people](#principle-first-duty-to-its-own-people) weighs the cost and the pull of the second.

Evidence can measure each error rate, but not how much of one to accept to avoid the other. What would move people is knowing how many refused claimants later turn out to be refugees, and how much the prospect of staying draws crossings.
