---
question: How many removals does the ECHR actually block?
kind: assumption
divides: [bar-and-remove, tighten-within-the-echr, refuge]
positions:
  - viewpoint: bar-and-remove
    holds: Far more than the proxies show. Article 3 risk is argued inside asylum claims, cases that cannot be removed are never put forward, and every attempt to narrow its reach in law has been undone in court.
  - viewpoint: tighten-within-the-echr
    holds: A small share directly. Late claims, judicial reviews and slow appeals do most of the damage, and British law can reach those. Article 8 is the part worth narrowing, by statute.
  - viewpoint: refuge
    holds: Few. Most people who resist removal claim asylum under the Refugee Convention, which would still bar return to danger after leaving the ECHR.
---
[No published source counts removals stopped by the ECHR](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-share-not-measured), and Lord Wolfson's finding that it constrains policy [is legal, not numerical](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/wolfsons-finding-is-legal-not-numerical). The proxies point to a small direct share: [human-rights applications were a small fraction of issues raised in detention, far behind asylum claims](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/late-claims-not-human-rights-claims), and [human-rights wins equal about 3% of offender deportations](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/human-rights-wins-about-3-percent). But [Article 3 risk is usually argued inside asylum claims, so those counts are a floor](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-hidden-in-other-rows), and [the 3% is not a block rate](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/ratio-is-not-a-block-rate).

The answer decides whether leaving is worth its costs. If the ECHR blocks few removals, withdrawal would risk [EU law-enforcement cooperation](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/eu-may-end-law-enforcement-cooperation-on-denunciation) for little, and [the Refugee Convention's ban on return would remain](#fact-asylum-law-and-the-echr/refugee-convention-ban-on-return-remains). If it blocks many, inside asylum claims as well as outside them, the case for staying in rests on its other benefits.

The evidence that would settle it is a count the Home Office says [it does not hold](#fact-removals-and-what-stops-them/echr-share-not-measured): of people liable to removal who stayed, how many stayed on ECHR grounds, by article, including Article 3 arguments made within asylum claims.
