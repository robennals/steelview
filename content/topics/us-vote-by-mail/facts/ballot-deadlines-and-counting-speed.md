title: Ballot Receipt Deadlines, Late Ballots and Counting Speed
claimSources: [1, 3, 8, 10, 12, 14]
claim: >-
  This report covers when mail ballots must arrive, how many arrive late, and
  how long states take to count. Fifteen states plus DC count ballots
  postmarked by Election Day that arrive later, down from 21 in 2020. Late
  arrival causes a steady share of rejections, rising in 2026 primaries, and
  the slowest counts are mostly in heavy-mail western states.

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-receipt-deadline-states}

### Grace periods rose in 2020 and are back to their 2016 level {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--back-to-2016-level}

The number of states counting late-arriving ballots peaked in November 2020 and has fallen since (chart). [14](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-14) NCSL expects the count of states requiring Election Day receipt in 2026 to be "roughly the same as it was in November 2016". [14](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-14)

North Dakota, Ohio and Utah moved to Election Day receipt in 2025. Kansas legislated the same change, but a court has blocked it for now (both marked under the chart). [1](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-1)

### Grace periods are not confined to one kind of state {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--grace-states-mixed}

The states still counting postmarked ballots that arrive late include Texas, Alaska, Kansas, Mississippi and West Virginia, as well as California, New York and Illinois. [1](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-1) Windows run from one day in Texas to about three weeks in Washington (next chart).

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-late-share}

### Late arrival is a steady share of rejections {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--late-share-of-rejections}

In every federal general election since 2016, between about one in eight and more than one in four rejected mail ballots was rejected for arriving too late (chart). [7](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-7) [5](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-5) [15](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-15)

In 2024 that was 17.8% of rejections, the third-largest reason after signature problems and "other". [15](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-15) Rejections themselves were 1.2% of returned mail ballots. [3](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-3)

### The low point came when the most states had grace periods {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--2020-low}

Late arrival's share of rejections was lowest in 2020, when the most states counted ballots arriving after Election Day (charts). [5](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-5) [14](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-14) The two series come from different sources, and much else about voting changed in 2020, so the timing alone does not show that grace periods caused the drop.

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-grace-windows}

### Watson's "roughly 30 States" counts military and overseas ballots too {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--fifteen-versus-thirty}

The Supreme Court described Mississippi as "one of roughly 30 States" counting late-arriving ballots. [2](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-2) NCSL's 15 states are those that do so for ordinary domestic mail ballots.

Its separate count for military and overseas voters is "Thirty states and Washington, D.C.", which accept at least some such ballots after Election Day. [1](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-1) Both numbers are right; they count different ballots.

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-hours-to-count}

### Most of the vote is counted within hours {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--most-votes-counted-within-hours}

In 2024 a majority of the national vote was counted in about two hours and three-quarters in about three. [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10) Every state had reported more than 75% of its presidential vote within about five days. [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

### The slowest counts are mostly in heavy-mail western states {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--slowest-states-mostly-heavy-mail}

MIT names Washington, California, Nevada, Arizona, Utah, Colorado and Alaska as having the most left to count after a day. California reported "about a third of its votes" over the month after Election Day. [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

Texas and Florida, each with well over ten million votes, counted almost all of them within about a day. [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

### Mail volume is not the only cause of slow counts {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--slow-states-not-only-mail}

Mississippi, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts and Michigan were also slow to report in 2024. [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10) Among the battleground states, Nevada and Pennsylvania counted far faster in 2024 than in 2020, while Arizona took twice as long (chart). [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

MIT's 2020 study found counts slower where states had "large volumes of mail ballots, prohibited preprocessing mail ballots, and allowed mail ballots to arrive after Election Day". [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-preprocessing-four-hours}

### Early processing speeds the first hours of the count {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--preprocessing-speeds-first-hours}

In 2020, states that let officials prepare mail ballots before Election Day had reported more of their vote four hours after polls closed (chart). [12](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-12) The number of states prohibiting this fell from seventeen in 2020 to seven in 2024. [12](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-12)

### The early-processing gap is gone within a day {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--gap-gone-by-24-hours}

By 24 hours after polls closed, "the gap between states had essentially disappeared". [12](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-12) Arizona and Nevada process mail ballots on receipt and were still among the slowest states in 2024 (table below). [11](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-11) [10](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-10)

### Nevada's late ballots were few against the votes cast and the margins {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--late-ballots-small-in-nevada}

Less than 1 percent of ballots cast in Nevada's two largest counties arrived after Election Day, and Senator Rosen's margins there "far" exceeded them. [9](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-9)

Against mail ballots rather than all ballots the share is larger: the two counties' late ballots alone come to nearly one in fifty of the mail ballots returned statewide. [9](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-9) [3](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-3)

### Late rejections rose in the 2026 primaries {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--late-rejections-rising-2026}

In Wisconsin, Michigan and Washington, more mail ballots arrived too late in 2026 primaries than in the previous comparable election (table). [8](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-8) None of these ballots was counted.

Washington, an all-mail state with a grace period, shows that late rejections happen under postmark rules as well as under Election Day deadlines. [8](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-8) [1](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-1)

### Postal changes bear on both kinds of deadline {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--postal-delays-and-postmarks}

The Postal Service says more than 95% of mail ballots have arrived on time in recent elections. [8](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-8) Since 2024 it postmarks mail at regional centres rather than where it is posted, which the reporting says has "particularly affected" states that rely on postmarks. [8](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-8)

### In Nevada the lead changed on ballots that arrived by Election Day {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--nevada-2024-lead-change}

Senator Rosen trailed on election night in 2024 and took the lead the next night, once about 50,000 Clark County mail ballots received on Election Day were counted. [9](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-9) The late swing came from the time taken to count, not from ballots arriving after Election Day.

## Observations {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--observations-watson}

### The Supreme Court left receipt deadlines to legislatures {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--watson-left-it-to-legislatures}

In *Watson v. RNC*, decided by five votes to four, the Court held that federal election-day statutes do not stop states counting ballots that arrive afterward (marked on the first chart). [2](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-2)

On the arguments that Election Day receipt protects integrity and confidence, it said such "policy arguments are properly directed to legislatures, not courts". [2](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-2)

### The Court recognised a reason a legislature might choose Election Day receipt {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--watson-appearance-of-fraud}

The opinion said legislatures may have set Election Day deadlines because "an election-day deadline avoids the appearance of fraud from late-arriving ballots". [2](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-2) It did not decide whether that view is right: "The question today is not whether requiring ballots to be received by election day is a good or bad idea". [2](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-2)

### Experts rate the US count well {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--experts-rate-count-strong}

The Electoral Integrity Project's expert survey of the 2024 election rated "the counting, results and procedural aspects" strong, while overall US scores fell. [13](#source-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed-13) It measures experts' judgement of the process, not public confidence (see [confidence and disputed results](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results)).

## About this data {#ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed--about}

**What this measures.** State law on when a mail ballot must arrive (NCSL), why returned mail ballots were rejected (EAVS), and how fast votes were reported (MIT, from Edison Research data used by news media).

**What it does not measure.** No national source counts ballots that arrived late and were counted inside a grace period; EAVS records only those rejected. Unofficial reporting speed is not the official canvass, which takes weeks everywhere.

**How it compares.** Deadlines and processing rules are shown for every state NCSL lists. Count speed by hour is published only for seven battleground states; MIT describes the rest in words.

**The Nevada share.** "Nearly one in fifty" divides the two counties' late ballots (about 9,500 + 2,400) by all 669,445 mail ballots returned in Nevada in 2024. Because other counties' late ballots are left out, the true statewide share is at least this large. An unconfirmed figure of about two percent is not used.

**Common mistakes.** NCSL's 15 states and *Watson*'s "roughly 30" are often treated as conflicting; they count different ballots. Late-rejection shares are shares of rejected ballots, not of all ballots.
