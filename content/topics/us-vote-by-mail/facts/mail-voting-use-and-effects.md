title: 'Mail Voting in the US: Who Uses It and Its Effects, 2016–2026'
claim: >-
  This report covers how many Americans vote by mail, who they are, where it
  is offered and what it does to turnout and party. Mail voting peaked in the
  2020 pandemic election and has since settled near three in ten voters, above
  its earlier level, with older voters, Western states and Democrats using it
  most. Studies find it raises turnout slightly and favours neither party.

This report covers who votes by mail and what mail voting changes. How mail ballots are rejected, how fast they are counted, and how often they have been abused are in separate reports.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-cps-method}

### Mail voting peaked in 2020 and fell back {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--mail-share-fell-after-2020}

The share of voters voting by mail jumped in the 2020 pandemic election, when many states eased their rules, and fell back in 2022 and 2024 (chart). [8](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-8) [23](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-23) The EAC's official counts show the same peak, from 43.1% in 2020 to 30.3% in 2024. [1](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-1) [2](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-2)

The EAC found the largest 2020 rises where states newly sent every voter a ballot or dropped excuse requirements. [2](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-2)

### Mail voting is still above its level before the pandemic {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--mail-above-pre-pandemic}

In 2016 and 2018, mail ballots were "approximately one-quarter of voter turnout". In 2024 they were still "larger than the percentage of the electorate that voted by mail in pre-pandemic elections". [1](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-1)

In 2024, early in-person voting reached its highest share in both the Census survey and the official counts. [1](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-1) [23](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-23)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-cps-age}

### Voters aged 65 and over vote by mail most {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--older-voters-use-mail-most}

In every election since 2018, voters aged 65 and over were the age group most likely to vote by mail (chart). [9](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-9) [23](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-23) The other age groups differ little from each other.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-cps-region}

### Mail voting is mainly a Western practice {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--west-votes-by-mail}

Most voters in the West vote by mail, and did so before the pandemic. In the South, mail stayed under a quarter of votes even in 2020 (chart). [8](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-8) [9](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-9) [23](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-23) The Northeast and Midwest moved most in 2020 and fell back most after it.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-eavs-method}

### The official counts and the Census survey tell the same story {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--official-counts-match-survey}

Election offices' counts and voters' own reports agree on the shape: a peak in 2020, then a fall to about three in ten. [6](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-6) [1](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-1) Their levels differ by up to two and a half points, because one counts ballots and the other asks people.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-state-rule-counts}

### More states offer mail voting to everyone than in 2016 {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--no-excuse-states-grew}

NCSL expects seven more states to offer no-excuse or all-mail voting in November 2026 than in 2016 (chart). [25](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-25) The count peaked in 2020, dropped by 2022 and has crept up since.

States have moved in both directions: several adopted all-mail voting after 2016, while Utah has voted to end automatic mail ballots (events under the chart). [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11) [12](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-12)

### Utah, a Republican-led state, votes by mail, but is moving to mail on request {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--utah-all-mail-and-opt-in}

Utah has sent every voter a ballot since all its counties adopted it in 2019, and in 2024 more than nine in ten of its votes came by mail. [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11) [5](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-5)

A 2025 law changes that: from 2029 "a voter will not receive a ballot by mail unless the voter requests to receive a ballot by mail". A request lasts eight years. [12](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-12) NCSL's table does not yet show the change.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-pew-mail}

### Republican support for mail voting for all fell after 2020 {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--republican-support-fell}

Republican support for letting any voter vote by mail fell from about half in April 2020 to under a third in 2024 and 2025. [20](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-20) [21](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-21) Democratic support stayed above eight in ten (chart). [20](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-20)

The first reading came early in the pandemic. Gallup links that year's fall in Republican confidence in elections to President Trump's "public questioning of the validity of mail voting". [22](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-22) Neither survey tests whether his statements moved support for mail voting.

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-adoption}

### Oregon's legislature did not start all-mail voting; its voters did {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--oregon-by-initiative}

Oregon's statewide system came from a 1998 citizen initiative, after the state had tested mail elections locally since 1981. [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11) [10](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-10) The other states on the list adopted it by bills passed by their legislatures.

### Most mostly-mail states adopted it from 2019 on {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--recent-adoptions}

Six of the nine first used it statewide from 2019 onward, around and after the pandemic; only Oregon, Washington and Colorado ran statewide mail elections before 2019. [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-state-mail-share}

### The mostly-mail states lead, but not all of them vote mostly by mail {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--all-mail-states-lead}

Oregon, Washington, Hawaii, Utah and Colorado cast more than nine in ten ballots by mail in 2024. Vermont, DC and Nevada, which also mail every voter a ballot, cast between two-fifths and two-thirds that way (chart). [5](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-5)

Arizona, which does not mail every voter a ballot, cast about three-quarters of its votes by mail. [5](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-5)

### In much of the South, mail is a small share of the vote {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--low-mail-states}

Arkansas, Tennessee, West Virginia and Texas had the lowest mail shares of any state, each casting about one ballot in thirty or fewer by mail (chart). [5](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-5)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-mail-use-by-party}

### Democrats vote by mail far more than Republicans {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--democrats-use-mail-more}

In 2024 Democratic voters were about half as likely again as Republicans to vote by mail (chart). [13](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-13) The gap has narrowed since 2020, "primarily due to Democrats pulling back in the use of voting by mail rather than Republicans increasing their usage". [13](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-13)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-mail-use-by-disability}

### Voters with disabilities rely on mail more, and find it easier {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--disabled-voters-use-mail-more}

In 2020 more than half of voters with disabilities voted by mail, a larger share than other voters (chart). [14](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-14) Disabled voters who voted by mail were far less likely to report difficulty than those who voted at a polling place: 5% against 18%. [14](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-14)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-effect-estimates}

### Sending every voter a ballot raised turnout by about two points {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--universal-mail-raised-turnout-modestly}

As California, Utah and Washington counties switched to universal mail ballots between 1996 and 2018, turnout rose by about 2 points of the citizen voting-age population (chart). [15](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-15) That is a real but small effect, and it comes from years before mail voting split by party.

### Mail voting gave neither party an advantage {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--no-partisan-tilt}

The same study found universal mail raised the Democratic share of turnout by only 0.1 points with trends included, with an upper bound of "only about 0.3%". [16](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-16) Vote-share effects were "modest or null". [17](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-17)

In Texas and Indiana in 2020, being old enough for no-excuse mail voting "did not substantially increase Democratic turnout relative to Republican turnout". [18](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-18)

### No-excuse eligibility in 2020 changed how people voted, not whether {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--eligibility-shifted-method}

Texans who had just become old enough to vote by mail without an excuse turned out at almost the same rate as those a year younger (chart). [18](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-18) They voted by mail more and early in person less. [19](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-19)

## Observations {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--observations-support-by-state-rule}

### Support for mail voting is highest where every voter already gets a ballot {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--support-higher-where-used}

In 2025, support for letting any voter vote by mail was highest in states that mail every voter a ballot and lowest in states that require an excuse (chart). [20](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-20) The pattern holds within each party. [20](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-20) The survey cannot say whether using mail voting builds support or supportive states adopt it.

## Subtleties {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--subtleties}

### Three measures give slightly different 2024 mail shares {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--three-measures}

The 2024 mail share comes out a little differently depending on who is counted:

- **Census survey:** 29.0% of people who said they voted. [6](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-6)
- **Official counts (EAVS):** 30.3% of counted ballots. [1](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-1)
- **Pew's validated voters:** 34%. [20](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-20)

## About this data {#mail-voting-use-and-effects--about}

**What is measured.** Three kinds of data: how people voted (the Census Current Population Survey, a household survey, and the EAC's Election Administration and Voting Survey, which collects counts from election offices); which states offer mail voting (NCSL); and what mail rules do and what people think of them (two studies, MIT and Pew surveys).

**Census survey.** Table 14 gives the method of voting as a share of people who said they voted. The Census survey over-reports turnout and relies on what voters say about how they voted. The age and region readings are the "By mail" column of each year's table. [6](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-6) [7](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-7)

**Official counts.** The EAVS mail share is counted mail ballots divided by total turnout. Each share is calculated only from states that reported it, so the methods do not add to 100%. [24](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-24) The 2016 EAVS report printed lower 2016 figures than the later reports' recalculation. [4](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-4)

**State rules.** NCSL's counts put DC aside; DC offered no-excuse voting at every date. [25](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-25) A state is counted as all-mail when every voter is sent a ballot; Vermont does so for general elections only. [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11)

**What it is commonly mistaken for.** Mail voting is not the same as all-mail voting. Every state lets at least some voters vote by mail, and federal law requires states to let military and overseas voters do so; only eight states and DC send every voter a ballot. [10](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-10) [11](#source-mail-voting-use-and-effects-11)
