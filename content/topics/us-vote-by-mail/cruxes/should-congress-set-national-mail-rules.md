---
question: Should Congress set national rules for mail voting in federal elections?
kind: priority
divides: [states-decide, mail-works, mail-exception]
positions:
  - viewpoint: states-decide
    holds: No, beyond the existing floor for military and overseas voters. States differ, change course when their voters want, and can be held to account locally; a national rule would swing with whoever holds Washington.
  - viewpoint: mail-works
    holds: A national floor would be right. A citizen's access to a mail ballot should not depend on the state they live in, any more than their right to vote does.
  - viewpoint: mail-exception
    holds: National limits would be justified where mail voting weakens the secrecy and integrity of federal elections that every state's voters share. The power is Congress's to use.
---
Congress plainly has the power: [its authority over the manner of congressional elections is "paramount"](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/congress-power-paramount), and [bills on both sides would use it in opposite directions](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/bills-on-both-sides). The question is whether it should.

This is a choice between principles. [Decisions made closest to the people](#principle-decisions-made-closest-to-the-people) favours letting [states that have moved in both directions](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/state-rules-move-both-ways) keep doing so. [An equal voice for every citizen](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen) and [verifiably honest elections](#principle-verifiably-honest-elections) can each favour one national rule, though they point to different rules.

Evidence bears on it only at the margins. If state rules produced large differences in who can vote or in fraud, the case for a national rule would grow. [Rejection rates vary widely by state](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/state-rates-range-widely), but studies find [no partisan effect from mail rules](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/no-partisan-tilt). The rest is a judgement about who should decide.
