---
question: Would tighter mail-voting rules raise the losing side's confidence in results, or lower the other side's?
kind: prediction
divides: [mail-exception, election-day-receipt, mail-works]
positions:
  - viewpoint: mail-exception
    holds: It would raise it. The side that distrusts mail ballots most would have less to distrust, and a result built on booth votes leaves less room for a story of stolen ballots.
  - viewpoint: election-day-receipt
    holds: Deadlines and fast counts would help most. A result known within a day, from ballots all in hand on Election Day, gives doubt less time and fewer moving parts to grow on.
  - viewpoint: mail-works
    holds: It would mostly move distrust to the other side. Confidence follows who wins; people trust the methods they use, and voters who lose an option they rely on would trust results less.
---
[Voters trust the mail count less than the in-person count](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/mail-below-in-person), and [mail ballots were central in the three most recent disputed results](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/mail-central-in-recent-disputes). But [confidence follows the party in power](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/confidence-follows-power), and [people who vote by mail trust the mail count](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/mail-voters-trust-mail).

[No study tests whether restricting mail voting would raise losers' confidence](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/would-restriction-raise-confidence), and [a rule's effect is hard to separate from winning](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/rule-effect-confounded). If tighter rules raise the losing side's trust without lowering the other's, the [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) argument for restriction stands. If they only move distrust from one party to the other, it falls, and the case rests on secrecy and fraud alone.

The test is a panel survey that follows the same voters across a state's rule change, and across a win and a loss, so the rule's effect can be separated from the winner's.
