---
question: Can checks on mail ballots catch ballots cast by someone else without rejecting genuine voters?
kind: tradeoff
divides: [mail-exception, mail-works, signature-discretion]
positions:
  - viewpoint: mail-exception
    holds: Not well enough. Any check strict enough to stop a household signing for another member will reject real voters, and any check loose enough to spare them will wave forgeries through. That is a reason to limit mail ballots, not to tune the check.
  - viewpoint: mail-works
    holds: Yes, with notice and cure. Rejection is already low, and where voters are told and can fix a problem most flagged ballots end up counted. The remaining unevenness is a design flaw to fix.
  - viewpoint: signature-discretion
    holds: Not by signature comparison. Its errors depend on the county and fall on young and minority voters. Objective checks, such as a date of birth paired with the signature, plus cure, come closer, though they reject valid ballots too.
---
The same data serves all three. [Signature problems are the largest listed reason for rejection](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/signatures-largest-reason), [the county matters more than anything else](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/county-strongest-predictor), and [an envelope signed by a spouse still got through](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/cobb-spouse-signature-passed). A stricter check would catch more of the second kind of error and make more of the first.

Objective checks do not escape the trade-off: [Texas's rejections rose under an ID-number rule while Georgia's fell](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/id-number-checks-also-reject), and [Britain's signature-and-birth-date check rejected 2.2% of returned postal votes](#fact-secret-ballot-and-postal-voting-abroad/uk-checks-identifiers). Curing helps: [more than half of flagged ballots were fixed and counted](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/more-than-half-cured).

What would move people is a measure nobody has: how many forged signatures each kind of check accepts, beside how many genuine voters it rejects. If good design can make both small, the first position loses its strongest reason to restrict mail voting. If it cannot, the second must say how many rejected genuine voters it will accept for each forgery stopped.
