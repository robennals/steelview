Voting should be easy for every citizen, as long as it is honest. The states that mail every voter a ballot show that it can be both.

### The federal government is moving to cut mail voting back

The President has signed two orders on mail ballots. The second would have the Postal Service carry ballots only for voters on a state's list, and the Supreme Court let it go ahead. Two bills in Congress would stop states mailing ballots to voters who have not asked for one. Utah, after years of mailing every voter a ballot, is moving to send them only on request. While a court blocked the Postal Service rule for the November 2026 election, the rule could return for later ones.

### States that mail every voter a ballot have done it for years without stolen elections

Oregon, Washington, Hawaii, Utah and Colorado cast more than nine in ten ballots by mail. Yet none of the elections thrown out for fraud in the Heritage Foundation's database was in Colorado, Oregon, Utah or Washington. A bipartisan commission that warned about absentee ballots still found "little evidence of fraud in Oregon, where the entire state votes by mail". Nor are more ballots thrown out: all-mail states fall on both sides of the national rate of rejected mail ballots.

### Every mail ballot is checked, and the schemes that were tried were caught

Every state checks the returned envelope before the ballot counts, though the checks differ. The schemes that did overturn elections were exposed by an investigation, an election challenge or a paper trail, and most of those elections were rerun. That includes one held entirely by mail: in Paterson's 2020 council election, postal inspectors found bundled ballots, and nearly a quarter of the ward's mail ballots were rejected. Large schemes have left traces: North Carolina's 2018 scheme came to light through its lopsided mail vote.

### Mail helps the voters polling places fail most

For many voters, a polling place is the hard way to vote. Voters with disabilities who voted by mail were far less likely to report difficulty than those who voted at a polling place, and older voters use mail most. Mailing the ballot also brings more people in. When counties in three Western states began mailing every voter a ballot, turnout rose by about two points, and the Democratic share of turnout barely moved.

### Big democracies vote by post, and Britain fixed its fraud without ending it

Germany cast almost half its 2021 vote by post, and Switzerland votes about 90% by post. While only 11 countries let every voter vote by post, they include the United States, Britain, Germany, Switzerland and Canada. Britain shows that fraud can be fixed without ending postal voting. After a court found thousands of postal votes falsified in Birmingham, Britain added signature and birth-date checks and banned campaigners from handling postal votes. From 2019 to 2023 its election watchdog found no evidence of large-scale fraud.

### Voting at home rarely means voting under pressure

The fear that voters at home are bullied has little evidence behind it. While nearly a quarter of mail voters had someone else in the room, in Oregon surveys 1% or fewer felt pressured by another person being there. An Oregon researcher found no sign that bribes or threats had been a problem. Nor is a polling place fully private: in the same national survey, 22% of in-person voters had no curtain or privacy screen.

### So every voter should get the choice, under rules both parties can trust

Democrats now vote by mail far more than Republicans, so the rules must earn both parties' trust. Every voter should be able to vote by mail, with ballot tracking, a chance to fix signature problems, and a ban on campaigners handling ballots, as a bipartisan commission urged. Low trust among one party's voters does not show that mail voting fails. The people who vote by mail trust the mail count most, and each party's voters have usually trusted the count more when their side holds the White House.
