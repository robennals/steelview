A free vote needs a booth, where no one can see how you vote. Mail ballots should stay open to people who cannot get to one, such as the sick, the disabled and people away from home. They should not be sent to everyone.

### Most states now let anyone vote by mail, and the habit has stuck

Mail voting is no longer kept for people who cannot reach the polls. Most states now let any voter vote by mail without giving a reason, seven more than in 2016. Mailing every voter a ballot is newer still: six of the nine places that do it began statewide in 2019 or later. And the habit has stuck, since mail voting is still above its level before the pandemic.

### The secret ballot was brought in to stop bribes and bullying

The booth exists for a reason. Before the secret ballot, a vote buyer could hand a bribed voter a ballot and watch him cast it. Reformers argued that secrecy would protect workers and debtors from their employers and creditors. A buyer who cannot check a vote has no reason to pay for it. And secrecy worked: in Chile, places where landlords held sway voted far more for the right, until the ballot became secret in 1958. Then the difference disappeared.

### A ballot filled in at home has no booth

At a kitchen table, nobody protects the voter's privacy. In a 2010 national survey, over 23% of mail voters had someone else in the room, and more than 6% showed their ballot to someone. Mail voters were also more likely to say their choices were not kept secret. That matters, because people who doubt their vote is secret are more swayed by others in how they vote. While few Oregon voters said they felt pressured, pressure at home can go unreported, and a booth is built so that the question never arises.

### Where fraud has overturned elections, it has mostly come through the mail

In the Heritage Foundation's database of fraud cases, about two-thirds of the elections thrown out or overturned involved absentee or mail ballots. A bipartisan commission called absentee ballots "the largest source of potential voter fraud". Fraud has struck where every ballot was mailed: Paterson's 2020 council election, held entirely by mail, was voided. While known cases are few against tens of millions of mail ballots, they are only the ones that were caught. Checks miss some: in Cobb County, Georgia, an envelope signed by the voter's spouse passed.

### Few democracies let everyone vote by post, and Britain found fraud when it did

Only 11 countries let every voter vote by post from home. Britain became one in 2001. Four years later, a judge found "literally thousands of votes" falsified in Birmingham, and an election court later held that postal voting for all had made voting in another person's name "once again viable". France went the other way, ending postal voting in 1975 after every party condemned the serious fraud it brought. While Britain kept postal voting and added identity checks, those checks came only after the fraud had been done.

### Mailing everyone a ballot adds few voters, and is trusted less

The gain from mailing everyone a ballot is small. When counties in three Western states began mailing every voter a ballot, turnout rose by about two points. Letting more voters ask for a mail ballot did less still: Texans who became eligible at 65 switched how they voted, not whether they voted. Meanwhile, in every Pew poll since 2020, fewer voters trusted the mail count than the in-person count, and fewer than half of Republican voters have ever been confident in it. A result the losers accept is easier to reach with a way of voting both sides trust.

### So polling booths should be the default, with mail for anyone who needs it

Voters with disabilities and older voters rely on mail more than others, and they should keep it. So should anyone who is sick, away from home or serving abroad. Everyone else should vote in a booth, on Election Day or in weeks of early voting, which reached its highest share yet in 2024. That keeps a secret vote for everyone, and a mail ballot for every voter who needs one.
