A mail ballot should not be thrown out because an election worker thinks a signature looks wrong. Where states compare signatures, they should tell voters about a problem and let them fix it.

### Matching signatures is a judgement call, yet it is the top reason ballots are rejected

[A signature that does not match is now the most common reason given for rejecting a mail ballot](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/signatures-largest-reason). Yet [Washington's state auditor called signature matching "inherently subjective", and its own reviewers disagreed about many signatures](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/reviewers-disagree). The check can also miss a wrong signature: [in Cobb County, Georgia, an envelope signed by the voter's spouse passed](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/cobb-spouse-signature-passed).

### The same rule gives very different results from county to county

In Washington, [the county a ballot was cast in mattered more than anything else the auditor measured. For mismatches alone, two counties were 18 times as likely as another to reject a ballot](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/county-strongest-predictor). That falls short of [an equal voice for every citizen](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen).

While [the auditor found no sign of bias in individual decisions](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/no-evidence-of-bias), honest judgement still differs. [Counties that rejected fewer ballots accepted less certain matches](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/low-rejection-counties-accept-more). A standard that honest people apply this differently is not a standard. [Officials have tampered with counts through absentee ballots before](#fact-absentee-fraud-and-manipulation-of-the-count/officials-manipulated-count). While [no case was found of signature checks used to change a result](#fact-absentee-fraud-and-manipulation-of-the-count/no-signature-review-case-found), rules should bind officials before any abuse, not after.

### Signature checks reject young, first-time and Black voters far more often

[In Washington, Black voters' ballots were rejected at four times white voters' rate](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/washington-race-gaps), counting unsigned envelopes too. [The youngest voters were rejected most](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/washington-age-gaps), and [that gap held for mismatches alone](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/youngest-mismatches-alone). [First-time voters were rejected far more than others](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/first-time-voters-rejected-more), and [studies in Florida found the same for young and inexperienced voters](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/florida-young-and-inexperienced).

### Courts have found signature rejections unfair, or not allowed by law

[A federal court found that New Hampshire voters were not told their ballot had been rejected, and that the officials checking had no handwriting training or standards](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/new-hampshire-due-process). [Pennsylvania's top court ruled that its election law does not allow rejecting ballots because signatures do not match](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/pennsylvania-no-signature-rejection).

While [a federal appeals court let Texas go on rejecting ballots without notice, citing the risk of fraud](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/fifth-circuit-fraud-risk), that was a short-term order, not a final ruling.

### The answer is a better check, not no check

Dropping checks altogether is not the answer. [Texas's rejections rose after it added an ID-number check](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/texas-rose-under-id-number), while [Georgia's stayed below its 2016 and 2018 levels after it swapped signatures for an ID number](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/georgia-fell-after-sb202). So the design of a check matters.

### So voters must be told of a problem and allowed to fix it

Where voters can fix a problem, [more than half the ballots set aside were corrected and counted](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/more-than-half-cured). So every state that compares signatures should let voters fix problems, [as 35 states and DC must already](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/most-states-cure). States should also have two trained reviewers from different parties check every mismatch, [as Colorado does](#fact-mail-ballot-rejection-and-signature-checks/training-and-bipartisan-review), and pair the signature with a fact that needs no judgement, as [Britain does with the date of birth](#fact-secret-ballot-and-postal-voting-abroad/uk-checks-identifiers).
