The Constitution lets each state's lawmakers set the rules for elections, and lets Congress change them. It gives the President no part beyond signing or vetoing bills. The states should keep making these choices, and presidents should stay out.

### The Constitution names state legislatures and Congress, not the President

[The Constitution's Elections Clause names state legislatures and Congress, not the President](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/elections-clause-text). Courts have blocked some of his moves. [A court has blocked the Postal Service rule he ordered, for November 2026](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/usps-rule-blocked). [In a separate case, a court ruled that the 2025 order's deadline rules went beyond his power](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/eo-14248-deadlines-enjoined), a ruling now under appeal.

While [the Supreme Court let the March 2026 order go ahead](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/eo-14399-stands), [it did not decide the order was lawful](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/no-merits-ruling). So the principle should be stated clearly now.

### States have set their own mail rules, by law or by direct vote

[Oregon's all-mail system came from its own voters, who voted for it directly](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/oregon-by-initiative). [Utah, after years of mailing every voter a ballot, is moving to mail ballots only on request](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/utah-all-mail-and-opt-in). [States have changed their mail-voting rules in both directions](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/states-moved-both-ways), and [three ended their extra days for late ballots in 2025](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/receipt-deadlines-swung-back). Each state can change course when its voters want.

### Mail voting differs widely by region

[Mail voting is mainly a Western practice](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/west-votes-by-mail). [In much of the South, mail ballots are a small share of the vote](#fact-mail-voting-use-and-effects/low-mail-states). [Decisions made closest to the people](#principle-decisions-made-closest-to-the-people) can fit each place. One national rule would force one state's habits on another.

### Congress already has a role, for military and overseas voters

[Congress already sets mail-ballot rules for military and overseas voters](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/congress-set-uocava-rules). That rule serves voters no single state can reach. It sets a minimum, and does not take over. [Congress has strong power over federal elections](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/congress-power-paramount), and [the Supreme Court said a national ballot deadline is for elected representatives to choose](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/watson-leaves-it-to-legislatures).

While Congress has this power, it should hold back. [Bills now pending would push mail voting in opposite directions](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-mail-voting/bills-on-both-sides), so a national rule would swing back and forth with whichever party controls Washington.

### Each state's voters should decide their own rules

[People should have a say in the rules that govern them](#principle-democratic-consent-over-membership). Fifty states, each able to change its own rules, give voters more say than one national rule. The Supreme Court said arguments over deadlines [belong to lawmakers, not courts](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/watson-left-it-to-legislatures). They belong to state lawmakers, not to the White House.
