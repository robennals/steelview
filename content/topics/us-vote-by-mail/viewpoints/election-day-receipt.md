An election should have a clear end. Every ballot should be in officials' hands when the polls close, and the result should be known within a day or so. Both are choices states can make, and both leave the losing side less room to doubt the result.

### The Supreme Court left the deadline to lawmakers

[Rules should mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say). Election Day is the day of the election, and a ballot that arrives a week later was not part of it. [The Supreme Court left the choice of deadline to lawmakers](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/watson-left-it-to-legislatures). [It noted that lawmakers may choose an Election Day deadline because it "avoids the appearance of fraud from late-arriving ballots"](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/watson-appearance-of-fraud).

### States are moving back to Election Day deadlines

[The number of states counting ballots that arrive late peaked at 21 in 2020, and is down to 15, against 13 in 2016](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/back-to-2016-level). This is not just a rule in Democratic states. [States still counting late ballots include Texas, Alaska and Mississippi, as well as California and New York](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/grace-states-mixed).

### A deadline turns some voters away

[Since 2016, late arrival has caused 12.1% to 26.9% of rejected mail ballots, depending on the election](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-share-of-rejections), and [late rejections rose in some 2026 primaries](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-rejections-rising-2026). While that is a real cost, the answer is to help voters meet the deadline, not to drop it. States can mail ballots early, provide drop boxes, and say plainly when to post them.

Postmarks are also a weak guide: [since 2024, the Postal Service has postmarked mail at regional centres](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/postal-delays-and-postmarks). A deadline based on arrival does not need them.

### Big states can count fast

[Texas and Florida each counted more than ten million votes in about a day](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/texas-florida-counted-fast). [The slowest counts are mostly in western states, and many mail ballots slow a count](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/slowest-states-mostly-heavy-mail). [Letting officials prepare mail ballots before Election Day speeds up the first hours of the count](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/preprocessing-speeds-first-hours).

While early preparation helps, it is not enough on its own. [Arizona and Nevada prepare ballots early yet still count slowly](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/prepare-early-still-slow). Ending late arrival would remove one more cause of delay.

### Late ballots were too few to decide Nevada's race

[In Nevada's two largest counties, late ballots were far below the winner's margins](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-ballots-small-in-nevada). While that race was not close enough for late ballots to matter, a rule should not be judged only by that. [Republican trust fell to 22% after the 2020 count](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/count-length-not-separated), but they had also lost, so no one can say if a long count lowers trust on its own. A democracy that wants [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) should not wait to find out.

Every state should require arrival by poll close, prepare ballots weeks ahead, and staff its count to finish within a day or two.
