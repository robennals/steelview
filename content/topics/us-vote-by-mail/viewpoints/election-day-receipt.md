Ballots should arrive by Election Day, and be counted fast.

### Election Day should be the last day a ballot can arrive

Election Day is the day of the election, and rules should mean what they say: a ballot that arrives a week later was not part of it. The Supreme Court left the choice of deadline to lawmakers, and noted a reason they may choose Election Day: it "avoids the appearance of fraud from late-arriving ballots".

### Ballots that arrive late become something to fight over

In 2020, Pennsylvania's late-arriving ballots were kept apart on a Supreme Court justice's order, left out of the certified count, and fought over until 2021. A ballot in by poll close leaves nothing to fight over.

Those 10,097 ballots were far fewer than Biden's margin, so they changed nothing. But in a race closer than the number of late ballots, the winner would have turned on how the courts ruled. Elections need results the losers can accept, and a rule must work in the close race, not only the easy one.

### Slow counts make people trust the result less

In a survey experiment, people told that a race took six days to call trusted the result less than people told nothing, and late ballots slow the count: MIT found counts were slower where states let mail ballots arrive after Election Day. Explaining the count in advance undid most of the drop, but a count that ends sooner leaves less to explain. The experiment tells people about a delay rather than watching voters after a real one, and the 2020 fall in Republican trust cannot be separated from losing.

### Most states already require ballots by Election Day, and more are returning to it

The number of states counting ballots that arrive late peaked at 21 in 2020. It is down to 15, against 13 in 2016. The other 35 states want ballots in by Election Day. This is not one party's rule: states still counting late ballots include Texas, Alaska and Mississippi, as well as California and New York.

### Some voters miss a deadline, so states should help them meet it

While late arrival has caused 12.1% to 26.9% of rejected mail ballots since 2016, depending on the election, the answer is to help voters meet the deadline, not to drop it: mail ballots early, provide drop boxes, and say plainly when to post them. A later deadline does not end late ballots either. Washington counts ballots that arrive weeks after Election Day, yet late rejections rose in its 2026 primary, as they did in Wisconsin and Michigan.

### Big states show a count can finish within a day

In 2024, most swing states counted nearly all their votes within hours, and Texas and Florida each counted more than ten million votes in about a day. So a fast count is a choice a state can make. Late arrival is not the only cause of delay: Arizona and Nevada still count slowly though they prepare ballots early. But it is one cause a state controls.

### So every state should require ballots by poll close, and count fast

Letting officials prepare mail ballots before Election Day speeds up the first hours of the count. So every state should require arrival by poll close, prepare ballots weeks ahead, and staff its count to finish within a day or two.
