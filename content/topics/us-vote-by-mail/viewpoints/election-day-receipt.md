---
name: Ballots should arrive by Election Day, and be counted fast
summary: "An election should have an end. Ballots should reach officials by the time polls close on Election Day, and states should count fast enough that the result is known within a day or so. States that do both show it can be done."
order: 3
---
An election should have a clear end. Every ballot should be in officials' hands when the polls close, and the result should be known within a day or so. Both are choices states can make, and both leave the losing side less room to doubt the result.

### A deadline should mean what it says

[Rules should mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say). Election Day is the day of the election, and a ballot that arrives a week later was not part of it. [The Supreme Court left the choice of deadline to lawmakers](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/watson-left-it-to-legislatures). [It noted that lawmakers may choose an Election Day deadline because it "avoids the appearance of fraud from late-arriving ballots"](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/watson-appearance-of-fraud).

### States are moving back to Election Day deadlines, and the issue is not partisan

[The number of states counting ballots that arrive late peaked in 2020, and is back to its 2016 level](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/back-to-2016-level). This is not a fight between the parties. [States still counting late ballots include Texas, Alaska and Mississippi, as well as California and New York](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/grace-states-mixed).

### A deadline turns some voters away, but that can be fixed

[Late arrival causes a steady share of rejected mail ballots](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-share-of-rejections), and [late rejections rose in some 2026 primaries](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-rejections-rising-2026). While that is a real cost, the answer is to help voters meet the deadline, not to drop it. States can mail ballots early, provide drop boxes, and say plainly when to post them.

Postmarks are also a weak guide: [since 2024, the Postal Service has postmarked mail at regional centres](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/postal-delays-and-postmarks). A deadline based on arrival does not need them.

### States can count fast if they prepare

[Texas and Florida each counted more than ten million votes in about a day](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/slowest-states-mostly-heavy-mail). [The slowest counts are mostly in western states that vote heavily by mail](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/slowest-states-mostly-heavy-mail). [Letting officials prepare mail ballots before Election Day speeds up the first hours of the count](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/preprocessing-speeds-first-hours).

While early preparation helps, it is not enough on its own. [Arizona and Nevada prepare ballots early yet still count slowly](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/gap-gone-by-24-hours). Fast counts need enough staff, early preparation and ballots already in hand.

### Late ballots rarely decide elections, but slow counts invite doubt

[In Nevada's two largest counties, late ballots were far below the winner's margins](#fact-ballot-deadlines-and-counting-speed/late-ballots-small-in-nevada). While late ballots rarely change a result, a rule should not be judged only by that. [No one has measured whether a long count lowers trust on its own](#fact-confidence-and-disputed-results/count-length-not-separated). A democracy that wants [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) should not wait to find out.

Every state should require arrival by poll close, prepare ballots weeks ahead, and staff its count to finish within a day or two.
