---
question: Should Congress set one national standard for voter ID and proof of citizenship?
kind: priority
divides: [states-decide, prove-identity, turned-away, id-plus-access]
positions:
  - viewpoint: states-decide
    holds: "No. Which document a voter shows is for each state's voters to choose; Congress should protect the right to vote, not pick the document, and the President has no say at all."
  - viewpoint: prove-identity
    holds: "Yes, for federal elections. Congress's power is paramount there, and a citizenship requirement that some states never check is not a national requirement."
  - viewpoint: turned-away
    holds: "Only a floor that protects access, such as requiring alternatives to ID. A national document mandate would spread Kansas's mistake to every state."
  - viewpoint: id-plus-access
    holds: "Yes, if it is a package both parties vote for. One national bargain is the only way to take the rules out of each election's fight."
---
Congress clearly has the power: [the Supreme Court calls it
"paramount"](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/congress-power-paramount),
and [it has set national floors several times since
1965](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/congress-has-set-national-floors).
The question is whether it should use that power to choose the document, which
evidence cannot settle.

The disagreement is about which principle comes first.
[Decisions made closest to the people](#principle-decisions-made-closest-to-the-people)
favours fifty choices; [an equal voice for every
citizen](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen) and [results the losers
can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) can favour one rule for
everyone. Since [both parties' national bills stalled in the
Senate](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/national-bills-stalled),
the case for leaving it to the states and the case for pairing ID with access disagree most sharply here: one wants no
national bill, the other wants a different one.
