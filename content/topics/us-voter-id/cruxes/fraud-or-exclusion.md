Both records are well documented, and they cannot be added up against each
other. [The fraud record is counted in convictions and altered ballots per
precinct, and the exclusion record in registration rates across whole
states](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/no-common-measure).

How a reader weighs that history decides how much risk each rule carries.
Someone who reads [Louisiana's collapse in Black
registration](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/louisiana-registration-collapse)
as the pattern to fear will treat any eligibility rule run by officials as a
danger, as the case against these rules does. Someone who reads [the machine
fraud](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/machine-fraud-real-and-prosecuted)
as the pattern will see the same rules as a safeguard. The sources do not
settle [whether a uniform, free ID resembles the Jim Crow
tools](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/jim-crow-tools-discretionary).
