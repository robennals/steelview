---
question: Is an eligible citizen kept from voting a worse error than an ineligible vote counted?
kind: tradeoff
divides: [prove-identity, turned-away, clean-rolls]
positions:
  - viewpoint: prove-identity
    holds: "Both are wrongs, but an ineligible vote silently cancels a lawful one and can never be found and removed, while a citizen without ID can get one free. A check that turns away a few people who could have complied is a fair price for a check that works."
  - viewpoint: turned-away
    holds: "Keeping an eligible citizen from voting is the worse error, because it falls on a real person with a right, and the rules make many such errors to prevent very few of the other kind."
  - viewpoint: clean-rolls
    holds: "The choice is forced only by bad tools. Records checks with notice and time to answer cut both errors at once; what matters is publishing how often each happens."
---
Every check makes both kinds of mistake, and the same reviews count both. In
the ones with published follow-ups, [citizens wrongly flagged outnumber the
non-citizens confirmed](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/both-errors-side-by-side).
Evidence can say how many of each a rule produces; it cannot say how many
wrongly blocked citizens one prevented illegal vote is worth.

If a reader holds that one ineligible vote counted is as bad as one eligible
voter blocked, the ratios in these reviews favour lighter checks, and
the case for proving identity loses its main reason for documents. If they hold that an
undetectable ineligible vote is worse, because it cannot be corrected, a
measurable burden on citizens is the acceptable side of the trade, and
the counts of blocked applicants stop being decisive.
