An election should be decided by the people allowed to vote, each voting once. That needs a check before the ballot goes in, because a secret ballot cannot be taken back out. That is what [verifiably honest elections](#principle-verifiably-honest-elections) means.

### Fraud at the polls has happened, and ID checks are meant to stop it

[Party machines in Kansas City and Chicago ran large frauds, and hundreds of people were convicted](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/machine-fraud-real-and-prosecuted). Some of that fraud was exactly what an ID check stops. [People voted at the polls several times, or in other people's names](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/in-person-ghost-voting). [A commission led by Democrat Jimmy Carter and Republican James Baker said good ID could deter voting in other people's names](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/carter-baker-id-deters).

While [most fraud on record was done another way, by officials or by mail](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/id-would-not-reach), that is a reason to guard those routes too. It is not a reason to leave the polls open. And [the cases on record are only the ones that were caught](#fact-election-fraud-and-voter-exclusion-history/detected-is-a-floor). Someone who votes as another person and gets away with it leaves no trace.

### Citizenship is the most basic rule, and the least checked

Only citizens may vote. Yet [the federal registration form asks people only to swear that they are citizens](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/federal-form-sworn-citizenship). [Every state that has checked its voter list has found non-citizens on it](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/found-on-rolls). [Ohio has found them in every batch it has checked since 2019](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/ohio-recurs).

[Most got there through mistakes at registration, such as clerks' errors, wrong licence records and confusion about the rules](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/how-noncitizens-reach-rolls). Those are failures of the check at registration, so that is where to fix them.

### Even a few illegal votes matter

While [the non-citizens found are hundredths of a percent of voters or fewer](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/hundredths-of-a-percent), that does not make them harmless. [Their votes are far fewer than the margins in recent close races](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/below-margins), but [some local races are decided by a handful of votes](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/close-local-races). And [checks find only non-citizens who show up in some government record](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/detected-is-a-floor), so others may go unseen. A rule that is broken rarely still has to be enforced.

### Checking ID costs voters little

[Very few citizens have no photo ID at all; many more just have a licence with an old address](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/no-id-versus-outdated-id). [The largest national study found strict ID laws did not lower registration or turnout, for any group](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/national-panel-no-turnout-effect). [Northern Ireland brought in ID after organised voting in other people's names](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/northern-ireland-after-personation), and [turnout did not fall once it required photo ID](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/ni-after-photo-id).

### Most Americans, and the Supreme Court, back the check

[Large majorities in both parties support photo ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-majorities), as do [at least seven in ten people in every racial group Pew reports](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-across-races). [Requiring proof of citizenship to register is just as popular](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/proof-of-citizenship-support). The Supreme Court upheld Indiana's ID law because [the state had good reasons: preventing fraud and keeping public trust](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/crawford-upheld-photo-id).

While [two national studies found that ID laws do not raise trust in elections](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/studies-find-no-effect), [another found that strict ID did raise trust in state elections](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/milyo-finds-effect).

### The check must be done the right way

How the check is done matters. [Kansas demanded citizenship documents from every new applicant. It blocked 31,089 people, while finding at most 39 non-citizens on its rolls](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/kansas-blocked-vs-noncitizens). That is the model to avoid. [Several states ask for documents only when state records cannot confirm citizenship](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/dpoc-not-all-in-effect), and [checking a whole state's voter list against records is now routine](#fact-non-citizens-on-voter-rolls/full-roll-check-feasible).

So states should ask for photo ID at the polls, and [give a free card to anyone without one, as Georgia does](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-exceptions). They should check citizenship against records at registration, and ask for documents only when the records fail. [Rules should mean what they say](#principle-rules-must-mean-what-they-say). A citizenship rule that is never checked is a rule in name only.
