Democracy works only if the losing side accepts defeat and waits for the next election. It will do that only if it trusts the result. That is why [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) matter more than any one rule.

### Americans are losing trust in election results

[Trust in the national count now follows the party in power, and the gap between the parties hit a record in 2024](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/confidence-follows-power). [After each presidential election, the winning side grows more confident and the losing side less](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/post-election-winner-swing). [The share of Americans with no confidence at all has roughly tripled in 20 years](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/level-versus-not-at-all).

### Each side fears a different problem, but both care about both

[Republicans increasingly worry that ineligible people will vote](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/different-fears). [Democrats doubt that every eligible voter will be able to vote](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/eligibility-confidence-2026). Yet [about nine in ten Americans say both goals matter](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/shared-goals). A rule that answers one fear and ignores the other gives half the country a reason to reject a defeat.

### Rules passed by one side lower the other side's trust

In Georgia, [most Republicans said the 2021 election law raised their trust, while the largest group of Democrats said it lowered theirs](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/sb202-self-reported). [Voters trust results less when they oppose the rules used, especially for voter ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/procedures-they-oppose).

Each party's national bill would do the same on a bigger scale. [One would require ID and proof of citizenship. The other would have required early voting and limited which ID states could ask for](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/freedom-to-vote-act-provisions). [Both stalled in the Senate](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/national-bills-stalled).

### Most voters already back both halves of a deal

[Large majorities in both parties support photo ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-majorities), and [Democratic support has grown since 2021–22](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/democrats-moved-toward-id). [Republican support for two weeks of early voting rose in 2025](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/republicans-moved-toward-early-voting). While [no poll publishes how many people back both](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/overlap-not-published), [both shares are so high that most people must](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/overlap-not-published).

[A commission led by Democrat Jimmy Carter and Republican James Baker paired photo ID with free cards](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-bargain). Carter [called Georgia's 2005 law a poll tax because voters had to pay](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-split). [Georgia now gives the card free](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-exceptions).

### Most of the pieces are already in place

[Eight of the 10 strict photo-ID states also let everyone vote early](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-early-voting), though [two offer no early voting](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-without-early-voting). Canada shows a fallback: [a voter without ID may vote if a voter from the same polling place vouches for them](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/canada-vouching).

How an ID rule is written matters. [Georgia's rejected mail ballots fell after it replaced signature checks with an ID number](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/georgia-rejections-near-national). But [Texas's rule, which had to match an old registration record, made rejections rise](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/two-states-opposite).

### Both parties should pass free ID and early voting together

Congress, or states one by one, should pass the two together: [free photo ID](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/chart-carter-baker), at least two weeks of early voting, and a sworn statement for those without ID.

While [no rule ends the boost in trust from winning, as Georgia shows](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/georgia-rebound), and [studies disagree on whether ID itself raises trust](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/milyo-finds-effect), the aim is narrower: to remove the losers' complaint about unfair rules. Passed by both parties, this gives [every citizen an equal voice](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen) and a result both sides can accept.
