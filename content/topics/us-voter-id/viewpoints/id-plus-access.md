Democracy works only if the losing side accepts defeat and waits for the next election. It will do that only if it trusts the result. That is why a result the losing side can accept matters more than any one rule.

### Trust in the count mostly follows which party holds power

[Trust in the national count has mostly followed the party in power](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/confidence-follows-power). In 2024 the gap between the parties hit a record 56 points, and [the share of Americans with no confidence at all has roughly tripled in 20 years](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/no-confidence-tripled). A side that distrusts the count may refuse to accept a defeat.

### Each side fears a different failure, but nearly everyone wants both prevented

[Republicans doubt non-citizens will be stopped](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/eligibility-confidence-2026). Democrats are less sure than Republicans that every eligible voter can vote. Yet [at least 85% of Americans call each goal important](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/shared-goals). A rule that answers one fear and ignores the other gives half the country a reason to reject a defeat.

### Rules passed by one side lower the other side's trust

In Georgia, [most Republicans said the 2021 election law raised their trust](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/sb202-self-reported). The largest group of Democrats said it lowered theirs. [Voters trust results less when they oppose the rules used, especially for voter ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/procedures-they-oppose). Each party's national bill would do the same on a bigger scale: [one would require proof of citizenship and photo ID](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/save-america-act-provisions), and [the other would have required early voting and made ID states accept alternatives](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/freedom-to-vote-act-provisions). [Both stalled in the Senate short of the 60 votes needed](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/national-bills-stalled).

### Most Americans back both photo ID and early voting

[83% of adults back photo ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-83-percent), and [80% back two weeks of early voting](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/overlap-not-published). No poll publishes how many back both, but with shares that high, most people must. And large majorities in both parties support photo ID.

### Strict ID and easy voting can work together, as most strict-ID states show

[Eight of the 10 strict photo-ID states also let everyone vote early, starting two to four weeks before Election Day](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-early-voting). In those states, voters must show photo ID but have weeks in which to vote. The other two offer no early voting for all voters, so pairing the two is a choice a state makes. How an ID rule is written matters too. [Georgia's rejected mail ballots have been below their 2016 and 2018 rates since it replaced signature checks with an ID number](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/georgia-rejections-near-national), but [Texas's rejections rose under its rule, which had to match the voter's registration record](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/two-states-opposite).

### So both parties should pass free ID and early voting together

Congress, or states one by one, should pass the two together. That means free photo ID, at least two weeks of early voting, and a sworn statement for those without ID. Passed by both parties, this gives every citizen an equal voice.

A commission led by Democrat Jimmy Carter and Republican James Baker [paired photo ID with free cards](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-bargain). Trust will still rise and fall with winning: [Georgia Republicans' trust rose from 26% after 2020 to 70% in 2022](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/georgia-rebound). And [studies disagree on whether ID itself raises trust](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/studies-disagree). While no rule can end distrust, this one removes the losers' complaint that the rules were unfair.
