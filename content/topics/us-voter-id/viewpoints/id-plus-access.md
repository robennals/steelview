name: Require ID and guarantee access, so both sides trust the result

Free photo ID together with guaranteed early voting, passed by both parties, would give each side the safeguard it fears losing."
order: 4

Democracy works only if the losing side hands over power and waits for the next election. It will do that reliably only if it trusts the contest, which is why [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) matter more than any single rule. America is losing that trust.

[Confidence in the national count follows the party in power, and the partisan gap hit a record in 2024](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/confidence-follows-power). [After each presidential election the winner's side grows more confident and the loser's less](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/post-election-winner-swing). [The share of Americans with no confidence at all has roughly tripled in twenty years](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/level-versus-not-at-all).

The two sides fear different failures. [Republicans have grown more likely to expect ineligible voting to be a major problem](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/different-fears), while [Democrats doubt that every eligible voter will be able to vote](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/eligibility-confidence-2026).

Yet [about nine in ten Americans say both goals matter](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/shared-goals). A rule that answers one fear and ignores the other hands half the country a reason to reject a defeat.

That is what one-sided rules do. [Voters are less confident in results run under procedures they oppose, with particularly large effects for voter ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/procedures-they-oppose). In Georgia, [most Republicans said the 2021 election law raised their confidence and a plurality of Democrats said it lowered theirs](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/sb202-self-reported).

Each party's national bill would repeat that on a larger scale: [one mandates ID and proof of citizenship, the other would have mandated early voting and limited what ID states could demand](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/freedom-to-vote-act-provisions). [Both stalled in the Senate](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/national-bills-stalled).

The voters for a deal already exist. [Photo ID has majority support in both parties](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-majorities), [Democratic support for it has risen since 2021–22](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/democrats-moved-toward-id), and [Republican support for two weeks of early voting rose in 2025](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/republicans-moved-toward-early-voting). [How many back both is not published](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/overlap-not-published), though the two shares guarantee a large majority does.

The bargain has a bipartisan pedigree. [The Carter–Baker Commission paired photo ID with free cards and mobile offices to register voters and issue them](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-bargain). Carter himself [called Georgia's 2005 law a poll tax because it charged for the card](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-split); [Georgia now offers one free](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-exceptions).

Most of the pieces are already in place. [Eight of the 10 strict-photo states also offer early voting to everyone](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-early-voting), though [two offer none](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-without-early-voting). Canada shows the fallback: [a voter without ID may vote if another elector vouches for them](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/canada-vouching).

Design decides whether an ID rule shuts voters out. [Georgia's mail-ballot rejections fell after it replaced signature matching with an ID number](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/georgia-rejections-near-national), while [Texas's version, which had to match an old registration record, sent rejections up](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/two-states-opposite).

No rule ends the winner effect. [Georgia's confidence rebound came on the side that won](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/georgia-rebound), and [studies disagree on whether ID itself raises confidence](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/milyo-finds-effect). The aim is narrower: to take away the procedural grievance each loser now has.

So Congress, or states one by one, should pair the two: photo ID, free to anyone who needs it; at least two weeks of early voting; and a sworn-affidavit fallback for a voter without ID on the day.

Passed with votes from both parties, that package would give [an equal voice for every citizen](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen) and a result each side can live with.
