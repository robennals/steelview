Democracy works only if the losing side accepts defeat and waits for the next election. It will do that only if it trusts the result. That is why [results the losers can accept](#principle-results-the-losers-can-accept) matter more than any one rule.

### Distrust in the count is growing, and splits by party

[The share of Americans with no confidence at all in the vote has roughly tripled in 20 years, to about one in five](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/no-confidence-tripled). [In 2024 the gap between the parties hit a record 56 points](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/gallup-gap-record-2024).

[Most Americans are still confident](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/level-versus-not-at-all), and [trust in the national count has mostly followed the party in power](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/confidence-follows-power). [After each presidential election, the winning side grows more confident and the losing side less](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/post-election-winner-swing). So at any time, much of one side doubts the count.

### The people who run elections now work under threat, and most say false claims make it worse

[About a third of local election officials report threats, harassment or abuse because of their job](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/officials-threatened). [Three in four say false information about elections makes their job harder or more dangerous](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/officials-blame-false-information).

That is the officials' own concern, and no survey measures what caused any threat. But a count that many people doubt still has to be run by someone, and those people now face that doubt directly.

### Each side fears a different failure, but nearly everyone wants both prevented

[Republicans doubt non-citizens will be stopped](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/eligibility-confidence-2026). [Democrats are less sure than Republicans that every eligible voter can vote](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/eligibility-confidence-2026). Yet [at least 85% of Americans call each goal important](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/shared-goals).

[Republicans have grown more likely to expect ineligible people to vote](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/different-fears). A rule that answers one fear and ignores the other gives half the country a reason to reject a defeat.

### Beyond who wins, rules passed by one side lower the other side's trust

In Georgia, [most Republicans said the 2021 election law raised their trust](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/sb202-self-reported), and [the largest group of Democrats said it lowered theirs](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/sb202-self-reported). Across states, [voters trust results less when they oppose the rules used, even allowing for who won, and especially for voter ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/procedures-they-oppose).

So the rules are one part of trust that lawmakers can change. Each party's national bill would split the country on a bigger scale. [One would require proof of citizenship and photo ID](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/save-america-act-provisions). [The other would have required early voting and made ID states accept alternatives](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/freedom-to-vote-act-provisions). [Both stalled in the Senate short of the 60 votes needed](#fact-federal-and-state-power-over-voting-rules/national-bills-stalled).

### Most Americans back both photo ID and early voting

[83% of adults back photo ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-83-percent), and [80% back two weeks of early voting](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/overlap-not-published). No poll publishes how many back both, but with shares that high, at least six in ten must.

[Large majorities in both parties support photo ID](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/photo-id-majorities). [Democratic support is higher than when Pew first asked in 2018](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/democrats-moved-toward-id). [Republican support for two weeks of early voting has risen since Pew first asked in 2021](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/republicans-moved-toward-early-voting).

### So both parties should pass free ID and early voting together

The two already go together: [eight of the 10 strict photo-ID states also let everyone vote early, starting two to four weeks before Election Day](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/strict-states-early-voting). Congress, or states one by one, should pass both: free photo ID, at least two weeks of early voting, and a sworn statement for those without ID. Passed by both parties, this gives [every citizen an equal voice](#principle-an-equal-voice-for-every-citizen).

A commission led by Democrat Jimmy Carter and Republican James Baker [paired photo ID with free cards](#fact-voter-id-rules-in-the-us-and-abroad/carter-baker-bargain). How the rule is written matters: [Texas's ID-number rule rejected more mail ballots, and Georgia's fewer](#fact-burdens-of-id-and-citizenship-rules/two-states-opposite). Trust will still rise and fall with winning, and [studies disagree on whether ID itself raises trust](#fact-opinion-and-confidence-in-elections/studies-disagree). No rule can end distrust, but a shared one takes away the losers' complaint that the rules were rigged against them.
