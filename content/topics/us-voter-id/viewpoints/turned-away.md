Every citizen should have an equal voice. A rule that makes voting harder for citizens has to stop enough fraud to be worth it. Photo-ID and proof-of-citizenship rules aim at fraud that is almost never found, and they cost real citizens their votes.

### Photo ID stops a kind of fraud that is almost never found

An ID check at the polls stops one thing: a person voting in someone else's name. In one count of alleged fraud since 2000, that was 10 of 2,068 cases. Another found about 31 incidents among more than 1 billion ballots. When the Supreme Court upheld Indiana's ID law, the record held no evidence of such fraud in Indiana's whole history. While cases caught are only a floor, a national study found strict ID had no effect on fraud, actual or perceived. And in 14 of 20 recorded cases, the fraud was done by officials or by mail, or by selling votes, which an ID check at the door never reaches.

### Non-citizens' votes are too rare to justify demanding papers from every citizen

Votes by non-citizens are rare too. The reviews that have counted them find between about 1 and 22 per million ballots, far fewer than the margins in recent close races. The reviews that give a cause trace non-citizens on the lists to mistakes, such as a clerk registering someone who had ticked "not a citizen". A clerk's mistake is fixed by checking records at registration, not by asking every citizen for a birth certificate.

### In Kansas, demanding papers blocked far more citizens than non-citizens

Kansas tried demanding citizenship papers, and blocked 31,089 applicants who did not show them in time. From 1999 to 2013, only 39 non-citizens were confirmed on its rolls. Even the state's own expert's figures implied that more than 99% of those still held back were citizens. While the court did not know how many of them lacked a document, about one adult citizen in ten cannot readily produce proof of citizenship. A rule that asks every applicant for papers stops many citizens to catch a few others.

### Texas's ID-number rule for mail ballots cost thousands of voters their vote

The ID rules already in force show the cost. In the first primary under its new rule, Texas rejected 12.38% of mail ballots, mostly for a missing or mismatched ID number. About 90% of the voters rejected did not vote another way, though most had voted in every general election from 2016 to 2020. While the rate fell sharply after that first primary, Texas has stayed well above its 2020 rate and the national rate. These were voters already on the list.

### Records checks flag many citizens, so a flag must start a question, not end a registration

Checking records is better than demanding papers, but records are often wrong. Most people Alabama made inactive as non-citizens in 2024 were citizens. In every program with a published follow-up, a large share of those flagged turned out to be citizens, and in Texas and Alabama more citizens were wrongly flagged than people were referred or removed.

### The burden falls hardest on young, poor, Black and Hispanic citizens

The citizens these rules stop are not a random few. The young and the poor are the most likely to lack a licence with current details, and Black and Hispanic citizens are at least three times as likely as white citizens to have no licence at all. While a national study found no clear fall in turnout, it cannot rule out drops like those in Kansas and Tennessee, which were largest among young, newly registered and Black voters. Campaigns contacted more non-white voters, which may have offset some of the effect. A cost that voters must work to overcome is still a cost.

### So keep the sworn statement of citizenship, and back it with records checks that give notice

Keep the federal form's sworn statement of citizenship, made under penalty of perjury. Check new registrations against government records, but treat a match as a reason to ask, and give flagged voters notice and time to answer before anything changes. Where states ask for ID at the polls, let a voter without it sign a sworn statement instead. And Congress should not pass the SAVE America Act, which would require proof of citizenship to register.
