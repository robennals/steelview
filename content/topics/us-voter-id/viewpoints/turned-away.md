Every citizen should have an equal voice. A rule that makes voting harder for some citizens must prevent enough harm to be worth it. Photo-ID and proof-of-citizenship rules aim at fraud that is rarely found, and their cost falls on people who can least afford it.

### ID checks at the polls aim at a kind of fraud that is almost never found

An ID check at the polls stops people voting in someone else's name. In one count of alleged fraud since 2000, that was 10 of 2,068 cases, and another found about 31 incidents among more than 1 billion ballots. Found cases are only the ones caught, but nothing shows a large hidden share, and a national study found strict ID had no effect on fraud, actual or perceived. When the Supreme Court upheld Indiana's ID law, the record held no evidence of such fraud in Indiana's whole history. And in 14 of 20 recorded cases, an ID check would not have reached the fraud.

### Non-citizens' votes are too rare to justify demanding papers from every citizen

The votes by non-citizens that reviews have found are between about 1 and 22 per million ballots, far fewer than the margins in recent close races. Each review charted found some non-citizens who voted, but the numbers are tiny. The reviews that give a cause trace non-citizens on the lists to mistakes. Officials can fix those without asking every citizen for a birth certificate.

### In Kansas, demanding citizenship papers blocked far more citizens than non-citizens

Kansas blocked 31,089 applicants who did not show citizenship documents in time. From 1999 to 2013, only 39 non-citizens were confirmed on its rolls. Even the state's own expert's figures suggested nearly all those on its suspense list were citizens. While the court did not know how many actually lacked a document, every one was kept off the list, and many citizens would struggle: about one adult citizen in ten cannot quickly produce proof of citizenship.

### Texas's ID-number rule for mail ballots cost thousands of voters their vote

In the first primary under its new rule, Texas rejected 12.38% of mail ballots, mostly for a missing or mismatched ID number, and about 90% of the voters rejected did not vote another way. While the rate fell sharply after that first primary, Texas has stayed well above its 2020 rate and the national rate. These were voters already on the list. How an ID rule is written decides who loses a vote.

### Records checks flag many citizens, so a flag must start a check, not end a registration

Most people Alabama made inactive as non-citizens in 2024 were citizens, and in every program with a published follow-up, a large share of those flagged turned out to be citizens. In Texas and Alabama, more citizens were wrongly flagged than people were referred or removed. A flag is a question, not an answer. Officials have shut voters out before: Louisiana cut Black voters from about 44% of registered voters to under 1%. A free, uniform check is not a literacy test, but a flag acted on without notice can still keep citizens out.

### The burden falls hardest on young, poor, Black and Hispanic citizens

The young and poor are most likely to lack an up-to-date licence, and Black and Hispanic citizens are at least three times as likely as white citizens to have no licence. The worst off should be weighed first. While a national study found no clear fall in turnout, it cannot rule out drops like those in Kansas and Tennessee, which were biggest among young, newly registered and Black voters. Campaigns contacted more non-white voters, which may have offset some. But a cost voters must overcome is still a cost.

### So keep the sworn promise of citizenship, and back it with records checks that give notice

Keep the federal form's sworn statement of citizenship, made under penalty of perjury. Check registrations against records, but treat a match as a reason to ask, and give flagged voters notice and time to answer before anything changes. Where states ask for ID, let anyone without it sign a sworn statement instead.

Congress should not pass the SAVE America Act, which would require proof of citizenship to register.
