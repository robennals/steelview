draft 1 (C1–C39), then updated the same day for draft 2 (3a rebuilt, C40–C80

**How figures are marked.**

- **read** — the figure was read on the fetched page named next to it. It is
  still not quoted verbatim; stage 5 must re-fetch and quote it.
- *unverified* — seen only in search snippets, secondary coverage, or a
  summary of a page that could not be opened.
- Several primary sites refused automated fetches (HTTP 403/402):
  congress.gov, senate.gov, centcom.mil, war.gov, cbo.gov, iaea.org,
  ukmto.org, iranhr.net, en-hrana.org (except one report), DCAS. Stage 5 will
  need a browser or a human for these.

### Nuclear programme

**C1. IAEA quarterly Iran verification reports (GOV/ series)**
- Source: IAEA Board of Governors, "Verification and monitoring in Iran" / "NPT Safeguards Agreement with Iran"; latest GOV/2026/50 (Sept 2026). iaea.org (403); mirrors at [IranWatch](https://www.iranwatch.org/sites/default/files/iaea-gov-2026-61.pdf).
- Measures: declared/verified enriched uranium stock by enrichment level; inspections. Excludes anything unverified after access ended.
- Coverage: quarterly since JCPOA (2016); 60% stock from April 2021. No country comparison (unique case).
- Slices: by enrichment level (5%, 20%, 60%), by form (UF6), by site.
- Shows: 440.9 kg of 60% uranium at 13 June 2025, the last verified figure (read, via [ISIS June 2026](https://isis-online.org/isis-reports/analysis-of-iaea-iran-verification-and-monitoring-and-npt-safeguards-reports-june-2026)). Earlier points 164.7 kg (Aug 2024), 274.8 kg (Feb 2025), 408.6 kg (May 2025) *unverified*. June 2026 report: IAEA "cannot provide any information on the current size, composition or whereabouts" (read, via ISIS).
- Helps 1, 6 (rapid growth to mid-2025; nothing verified since). Hurts 1's "closer than ever in Feb 2026" (the series stops in June 2025, before the war).
- Status: stock growth to June 2025 well-supported; state in Feb 2026 `unknown` — narrow the claim to "last verified".
- Draft 2: the JCPOA-era quarters (2016–19) are in C60; the reports on past weapons work are C47.

**C2. ISIS quarterly analyses of IAEA reports**
- Source: Institute for Science and International Security, per-report analyses, latest 9 June 2026 (URL above).
- Measures: IAEA figures plus ISIS's own estimates (e.g. Esfahan ~265–287 kg, ISIS's, not IAEA's — read).
- Coverage: one per IAEA report; easiest route to the full series.
- Shows: inspections in Feb 2026 at 7 of 13 unaffected facilities, 0 of 8 affected (read); Iran "has no identifiable route to produce weapon-grade uranium", >1 year to a crude weapon (read).
- Helps 3, 4 (no route to bomb-grade). Hurts 1's urgency; helps 1/6 on loss of verification. Hawk-leaning publisher, so its own estimates are leads; IAEA figures are evidence.
- Status: well-supported for the access record.

**C3. IAEA Board resolutions and votes**
- Source: GOV/2025/38 (12 Jun 2025 non-compliance), GOV/2025/71 (21 Nov 2025), GOV/2026/40 (10 Jun 2026, 21–3–10), GOV/2026/61 (9 Sep 2026, 23–3–8) — read via IranWatch PDF and AA.
- Measures: Board findings and vote tallies. Coverage: every resolution since 2003 (series possible).
- Shows: first non-compliance finding since 2005; Sept 2026 resolution asks the DG to report to the UNSC and UNGA (read).
- Helps 1, 6. Hurts nobody directly; 4 can cite the abstentions. Cairo agreement 9 Sep 2025, terminated 20 Nov 2025 (read, ISIS).
- Status: well-supported.

**C4. ODNI Annual Threat Assessment 2025 and 2026**
- Source: [ODNI ATA 2026](https://archive.dni.gov/files/ODNI/documents/assessments/ATA-2026-Unclassified-Report.pdf) (March 2026, information to 14 March).
- Shows: Iran "was intending to try to recover from the devastation of its nuclear infrastructure sustained during the 12-Day War" (read). The 2025 line "Iran is not building a nuclear weapon" is absent in 2026 (read). Gabbard's oral remarks differ from the written text ([Foreign Policy, 19 Mar 2026](https://foreignpolicy.com/), read by research — exact URL to recover in stage 5).
- Coverage: annual since 2006 (Iran weapons-decision language each year — a usable series of statements).
- Helps 4, 3 (no weapon decision assessed). Hurts 1's "closer than ever"; helps 1 slightly (intent to recover).
- Status: "US intelligence did not assess Iran had decided to build a weapon" — likely well-supported if the 2025–26 texts agree.

**C5. CRS IF12106 "Iran and Nuclear Weapons Production"**
- Source: Paul Kerr, CRS, updated 13 Oct 2026, [EveryCRSReport PDF](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-10-13_IF12106_ff4611e8ad95fe2ddcc2266816c7d28beec63b35.pdf).
- Shows (all read): "The status of Iran's nuclear program is unclear"; DIA in May 2025 assessed <1 week to enough weapons-grade uranium for one weapon; in 2026 "Iran has not resumed enriching uranium"; June 2026 MoU text "Iran reaffirms that it shall not procure or develop nuclear weapons".
- Coverage: many editions since 2021 — the breakout estimates form a series.
- Helps 1 (pre-2025 breakout <1 week), 4/3 (no resumed enrichment), 5/"deal now" (MoU commitment). Hurts 6's "pauses favour Iran" on enrichment.
- Status: neutral compiler; well-supported for what officials said.

**C6. Strike-damage claims: DoD briefings vs intelligence leaks**
- Sources: Hegseth/Caine briefing, "nearly 80%" of nuclear infrastructure destroyed, 13,000+ targets in 38 days ([Rev transcript](https://www.rev.com/transcripts/hegseth-and-caine-hold-a-briefing-on-iran), *unverified*); Reuters 4 May 2026, US intelligence sees limited new nuclear damage and breakout ~1 year (*unverified*, via IranWatch); [FDD rebuttal](https://www.fdd.org/analysis/2026/05/06/oversights-mar-low-end-estimate-of-tehrans-nuclear-weapons-timeline/) (*unverified*); 2025 leaked DIA "3–6 months" (*unverified*).
- Snapshot claims, not a series. Directly contradictory official vs leaked figures.
- Helps 1/6 (official) or 3/4 (leak), depending which is believed.
- Status: `contested` candidate, but narrow first: "officials and intelligence leaks disagree; IAEA cannot verify" is well-supported.

## Notes on the workflow

**From the draft-2 pass:**

- **The context rule roughly doubles stage-3 research**, and several context
  items have no standard source (deaths per protest in other countries'
  crackdowns; foiled vs carried-out plots). Saying that "searched, none found"
  is an acceptable answer for a context item, listed in 3a, would keep the
  rule from stalling the stage.
- **Viewpoint numbers changed between drafts**, so draft-1 candidates now
  carry stale helps/hurts labels. Stable short names for viewpoints (for
  example `nuclear-threat`, `war-powers`) instead of numbers would survive
  redrafts.
- **Parallel research produced overlapping candidates** (two researchers
  found the IHRNGO series and the IAEA JCPOA reports). A dedupe step when
  merging research is worth naming.
- **Archived copies work where WebFetch fails.** iaea.org, iranhr.net and
  en-hrana.org were readable via curl or web.archive.org; the doc's
  "blocked sources" rule could name curl and archives as the first fallback.

**From the draft-1 pass** (some since addressed in WORKFLOW.md):

- **Stage order vs ownership.** The outline is human-owned, yet this run had
  the agent draft it, critique it and then research it. The workflow should
  say what to do when the outline's factual framing (here, the timeline) is
  wrong: the critique can't edit it, and stage 3 is meant to work from it.
  I put the corrected timeline in both the critique and the top of this file.
- **"Unverified" is ambiguous when research is delegated.** 3b says mark
  figures *unverified* "until stage 5 fetches and quotes it", which makes
  every stage-3 figure unverified. A two-level mark (read on a page vs only
  seen secondhand) is more useful; this file uses one. The doc should define it.
- **Critique-added questions.** Stage 2 adds questions (values that are
  really claims) before the human signs off; stage 3 doesn't say whether to
  research them. I included them, marked +.
- **Blocked primary sources.** The rules prefer primary sources, but many
  official sites refuse automated fetches. The doc should say whether a
  reliable secondary quote of a primary is acceptable at stage 3, and how
  stage 5 gets the primary (human fetch?).
- **8–12 reports vs "broad enough".** For a war topic the natural split was
  12; the cap pushed nuclear intent and stockpile into separate reports
  rather than one, which is arguable. Saying why the cap exists would help.
- **Live-event dating.** Nothing asks for an "as of" date per figure, which
  matters when counts froze at different dates (casualties stop in April,
  costs run to September). 3b's "date of latest release" is per source, not
  per figure.
- **Status at stage 3 for snapshots.** Many war facts (damage claims,
  ceasefire incidents) cannot have a full series; AUTHORING's "numeric fact
  carries a series or says why" should be flagged at stage 3 so reports are
  planned as tables from the start.
