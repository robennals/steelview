# Decisions made on the human's behalf: voter ID, stages 4a (repeat) and 6

The human waived the stage gates for this round. Each decision below is open
to reversal; the rejected alternative is given with it.

1. **Viewpoint frontmatter is `name`, `summary` and `order` only.** The brief
   asked for `citesFacts`, `acknowledges`, `setsAside` and `principles`;
   AUTHORING ("Citations and data order") and the UK viewpoints use none of
   them, the schema keeps them only for migration, and nothing reads them. The
   coordinator confirmed AUTHORING wins. What each viewpoint builds on,
   concedes and sets aside is carried by its inline pills.
   *Rejected:* filling the four lists as well, which would be inert and could
   drift from the prose.
2. **Headline order set in `topic.md` `dataOrder`:** rules → fraud and
   exclusion history → non-citizens → burdens → roll maintenance → opinion →
   federal and state power. The rules come first so a reader knows what is
   being argued about; the two histories of harm (fraud, exclusion) and the
   two kinds of error (non-citizens found, citizens burdened) sit side by
   side. *Rejected:* leading with the burdens report, which would open the
   page on one side's strongest evidence.
3. **Viewpoint order alternates:** `prove-identity` (1), `turned-away` (2),
   `clean-rolls` (3), `id-plus-access` (4), `states-decide` (5).
   *Rejected:* grouping the two compromise positions (3 and 4) after the two
   poles and before the federalist one in a different order; the chosen order
   puts an integrity-leaning view (clean rolls) between the access view and
   the bipartisan one.
4. **`prove-identity` argues for records-first citizenship checks**, with
   documents only when records fail, and concedes Kansas as the model to
   avoid. *Rejected:* defending documentary proof from every applicant, which
   the Kansas record (31,089 blocked against at most 39 non-citizens) would
   leave the viewpoint unable to answer.
5. **`turned-away` no longer claims the Kansas applicants could not comply.**
   It says they were eligible applicants kept off the rolls, cites the
   court's "no evidence about how many" lacked a document, and leaves the
   rest to a crux. *Rejected:* "31,089 citizens who could not prove
   citizenship", which the record does not support.
6. **`turned-away` keeps the Jim Crow history but concedes a free, uniform ID
   is not a literacy test**, and leans on Florida 2000 for the modern risk.
   Georgia's 2018 "exact match" was left out of the prose because voters on
   hold could still vote with photo ID, which cuts both ways. *Rejected:*
   presenting ID rules as continuous with Jim Crow tools.
7. **`id-plus-access` uses Georgia's mail-ballot rejections falling after
   SB 202** (the corrected finding) as evidence that ID rules can be designed
   to reduce rejections, beside Texas's opposite result. *Rejected:* dropping
   Georgia now that its confidence rebound is shown to follow the winner.
8. **`states-decide` concedes the Voting Rights Act was needed** and draws its
   line between protecting the right to vote (national) and choosing the
   document (state). *Rejected:* a pure states'-rights essay, which a reader
   would rightly find blind to the registration record before 1965.
9. **Six cruxes, merging two candidates.** The "were Kansas's blocked
   applicants able to comply?" assumption and the "would national proof of
   citizenship block citizens?" prediction became one crux
   (`could-blocked-applicants-have-complied`), because the first is the
   evidence that would settle the second. The outline's "does a rule that
   leaves turnout unchanged still burden voters?" was folded into
   `which-error-is-worse` and the burden observations, since on its own it
   restates the two viewpoints' conclusions. *Rejected:* seven or eight
   cruxes with overlapping questions.
10. **Crux bodies name viewpoints in words** ("the case for proving
    identity") rather than by id, matching the UK cruxes. *Rejected:* ids in
    code formatting, which render as code on the page.
11. **No `#needed` pills.** Every empirical premise in the five viewpoints
    maps to an existing observation, so no new report slices were required.
    Claims that had no observation were rewritten or cut (for example a
    statement that most Pew "inaccurate" records belonged to eligible voters
    became "many", which the observation supports).
12. **No report edits.** Writing the viewpoints found no report problem that
    needed a change; every observation id listed in `stage5-results.md`
    still exists. The five "missing" ids in that file are comparison chart
    ids (`registrants-found`, `votes-found`, `flags-reversed`,
    `against-margins`, `ohio-referrals`), not observations.
