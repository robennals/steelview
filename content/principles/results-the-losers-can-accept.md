---
name: Results the losers can accept
---
Democracy depends less on how winners behave than on how losers behave. The
side that lost has to hand over power and wait for the next election, and it
will do that reliably only if it believes the contest was fair. Rules that
both sides trust are worth a great deal, because they make defeat something to
recover from rather than something to resist.

In 1801 John Adams handed the presidency to Thomas Jefferson, the rival who
had beaten him, in the first transfer of power between opposing parties in
American history. Two centuries later Al Gore lost Florida, and with it the
presidency, by 537 votes after the Supreme Court halted a recount. He said he
disagreed with the ruling, and accepted it.
