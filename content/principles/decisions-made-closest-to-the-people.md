---
name: Decisions made closest to the people
---
People can judge a decision best when they can see its effects and reach the
people who made it. A distant government has to apply one rule to places that
differ widely; a local one can fit the rule to the place, and can be voted out
by neighbours who watched it work. Decisions should therefore be made at the
lowest level that can handle them, and taken higher only when they must be.

In New England town meetings, residents still vote in person on their own
budget, roads and schools, as they have for more than three centuries.
Switzerland leaves so much to its cantons that taxes, schooling and even
voting procedures differ from one to the next, within a country that is among
the most stable in the world.
