---
name: War only as a last resort
---
Wars are easier to start than to stop, and they are paid for by people who
did not choose them: soldiers, and civilians who live where the fighting
happens. Those who begin them rarely foresee how they end. So whoever wants
to fight carries the burden of proof. Every other way of getting what is
needed should have been tried and have failed, and the fighting should stop
as soon as something short of it will do.

In October 1962 President Kennedy's military advisers urged him to bomb the
Soviet missile sites in Cuba. He chose a naval quarantine and a negotiated
deal instead, and the crisis ended without a shot fired between the
superpowers. The idea is far older than that crisis: the just-war tradition
has held for centuries that force is legitimate only once peaceful means are
exhausted.
