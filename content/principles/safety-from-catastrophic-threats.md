---
name: Safety from catastrophic threats
---
Some dangers are too large to learn from. A disaster that kills a city's
population leaves no second chance, so it has to be prevented rather than
survived. Against that kind of threat, waiting for proof that it is imminent
means waiting until it is too late, and a cautious person spends heavily on
lowering the odds even when they are already low.

Smallpox killed hundreds of millions of people before a worldwide vaccination
campaign ended it in 1980. Rival governments that agreed on little else funded
the campaign, because no country could be safe while the virus survived
anywhere. Nuclear safeguards rest on the same reasoning: the material for a
bomb is counted and watched not because anyone expects a theft this year, but
because a single theft would be too many.
