---
name: Loyalty to allies
---
A promise to stand by a friend is tested only when keeping it costs
something. Allies who rely on that promise make choices they could not
otherwise afford — to resist a threat, to stay out of an arms race, to take
risks on our behalf — and they are owed the support they were counting on.

In 1948 the Soviet Union cut off road and rail access to West Berlin to force
the Western allies out. Instead of leaving, they supplied more than two million
people by air for nearly a year, until the blockade was lifted. Berliners had
been told they would not be abandoned, and they were not. The day after the
attacks of 11 September 2001, NATO's members invoked its mutual-defence clause
for the first time, on behalf of the United States.
