---
name: Obligation to people in danger
---
We have a duty to help people facing grave danger, even when they are strangers.
A human life has a claim on our care that does not depend on friendship,
nationality or what the person can offer us in return.

Giving sanctuary to Jews fleeing Hitler is an iconic expression of this duty.
People who opened their homes or helped families escape persecution acted on the
conviction that someone in mortal danger deserved protection. Rescuing a drowning
stranger expresses the same principle in an immediate, everyday form: their need
and our ability to help give us a reason to act.
