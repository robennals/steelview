---
name: Verifiably honest elections
---
An election should be won by the votes of the people entitled to cast them,
each voting once, with every vote counted as cast. Honesty is not enough on
its own; it has to be checkable, so that anyone who doubts a result can see
how it was produced. An honest count that no one can verify is hard to tell
apart from a dishonest one.

The secret ballot, introduced in Australia in the 1850s and Britain in 1872,
ended an era when employers and party agents could watch how a man voted and
pay or punish him for it. In South Africa's first free election in 1994,
voters' fingers were marked with ink that would not wash off for days, so
that nearly twenty million people, many voting for the first time, could see
that no one had voted twice.
