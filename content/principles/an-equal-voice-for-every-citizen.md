---
name: An equal voice for every citizen
---
In choosing a government, every citizen counts the same: the poor and the
rich, the busy and the idle, the city dweller and the farmer a long drive from
the nearest office. Political equality is the one kind of equality a democracy
promises outright, and it is real only if each eligible person can actually
use their vote. A right that is hard to exercise for some people and easy for
others is not equal in practice.

In 1965 marchers crossing the Edmund Pettus Bridge in Selma, Alabama, were
beaten by state troopers for demanding the right to register. Within months
Congress passed the Voting Rights Act. Around the same time the Supreme Court
ruled that legislative districts must hold roughly equal numbers of people,
ending systems in which a rural voter could outweigh many city voters:
one person, one vote.
